# Art Tech Spec

The technical spec that makes the look reproducible in code: the isometric grid and screen math, sprite anchoring, depth sorting, and the code-generated pixel art format. What things look like (palettes, factions, VFX, tells, UI) is in [art-direction.md](art-direction.md). The renderer that consumes this spec is in [architecture.md § Rendering](../tech/architecture.md#rendering).

Binding constraint: **D001 #1.** All art is generated by code from palette-indexed grids.

---

## 1. Isometric spec

### Tile and screen

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Tile footprint | **32 × 16 px diamond** (2:1 dimetric, the classic "pixel isometric") |
| Elevation step | 8 px per level (cliff side faces drawn below the diamond) |
| Internal render resolution | **640 × 360** (16:9) |
| Scaling | **Integer only**: 2× = 1280×720, 3× = 1920×1080, 4× = 2560×1440, 6× = 3840×2160. Letterbox with `ink.0`. |
| Map size without panning | `cols + rows ≤ 36`. Every campaign map fits on one screen ([design/maps.md §1](../design/maps.md#1-grid-and-coordinates)). Only endless and challenge maps may be larger and pan. |

**Screen budget for the largest campaign map** (22×14, Act III and the finale): the diamond is `(cols + rows) × 16` by `(cols + rows) × 8` = **576 × 288 px**. Add up to 32 px of tower overhang above the top row and 8 px for high ground, and it fits in **576 × 328** of the 640 × 360 view, leaving 32 px side margins. An isometric diamond leaves the four screen corners empty, so the HUD lives in the corners ([art-direction.md §9](art-direction.md#9-ui-style)) and never covers tiles. The camera centers the map's bounding box and doesn't move on campaign maps.

**Why 640×360 and 32×16:** 640×360 scales to every common 16:9 display by an integer, so there are no mixels anywhere. A 32×16 tile gives towers about 32×40 px of canvas, which is enough room for readable silhouettes and upgrade tiers while still letting about 18×18 tiles fit on screen.

The scaling factor is computed in **device pixels** so that fractional `devicePixelRatio` values (1.25, 1.5) still map each game pixel to a whole number of physical pixels: `n = max(1, floor(min(innerWidth·dpr / 640, innerHeight·dpr / 360)))`, with the canvas CSS size set to `640·n / dpr` by `360·n / dpr`. The implementation is in `src/render/scale.ts` ([architecture.md § Rendering](../tech/architecture.md#rendering)).

### The canonical tile mask

Ground tiles tile seamlessly at a step of (16, 8) with no overlap and no gaps when the diamond is exactly this shape (256 px, which equals the lattice cell area):

```
row r (0..7):   x from 15−2r to 16+2r   (widths 2, 6, 10, … 30)
row r (8..15):  mirror of row 15−r
```

The two widest rows (7 and 8) are 30 px wide. The tile's extreme left and right points (x = 0 and x = 31) are owned by neighboring tiles. The procedural tile generator and the buildable-tile overlay both use this mask.

### Grid ↔ screen math

The grid is a continuous coordinate space in **tile units**, addressed as `(col, row)` in design docs and map data. In code, `gx = col` and `gy = row`. Tile `(i, j)` covers `[i, i+1) × [j, j+1)`, and its center is `(i + 0.5, j + 0.5)`. The sim works only in grid units; the render layer converts.

```ts
const TW = 32, TH = 16;          // tile width/height in px
const HW = TW / 2, HH = TH / 2;  // 16, 8
const ZH = 8;                    // px per elevation level

// grid → screen (world pixels, before camera scroll)
sx = (gx - gy) * HW + originX
sy = (gx + gy) * HH + originY - z * ZH

// screen → grid (z = 0)
const u = (sx - originX) / HW, v = (sy - originY) / HH;
gx = (u + v) / 2
gy = (v - u) / 2
// picking a tile: (floor(gx), floor(gy)), then bounds-check
```

`originX/originY` is the screen position of grid point `(0, 0)`, the top vertex of tile `(0, 0)`. A tile image (32×16) is drawn with its top-left at `(sx(i, j) − 16, sy(i, j))`. **All final screen positions are rounded to integers** (`Math.round`) before they reach Phaser.

The implementation is `src/render/iso.ts` (tested in `iso.test.ts`). Two details it settles:
- **Picking snaps to the game pixel.** The pointer position is first moved to the center of the game pixel it falls in, then projected and floored. Pixel centers never lie on a diamond edge, so picking matches [the canonical tile mask](#the-canonical-tile-mask) exactly: each mask pixel picks its own tile and no pixel picks two. High ground is tested first at elevation 1 (its top face, drawn `ZH` higher, covers the tiles behind it); a point on its cliff faces falls in its own ground footprint, so it picks the raised tile too.
- **Centering** (`mapOrigin`): the origin centers the ground diamond horizontally, and centers the diamond plus 40 px of headroom above it (tower overhang and one elevation step) vertically, in whole pixels. A1M1 (14 × 10) gets origin (288, 104) in the 640 × 360 view.

**Ranges are circles in grid space**, so they appear on screen as 2:1 ellipses. Range rings are pre-rasterized pixel ellipses ([art-direction.md §7](art-direction.md#range-rings-and-placement)), not anti-aliased vector shapes.

### Sprite anchoring

Every sprite definition declares an **anchor**: the point that sits on the ground point (the foot point). It is a whole-pixel **edge** coordinate, exactly what the renderer passes to `setDisplayOrigin(ax, ay)` (never a fraction): `(16, 0)` on a 32 px tile is the top vertex between pixels 15 and 16. The outline pass shifts it by 1, a mirrored frame's anchor is `w − ax`, and `SpriteSource.frameAnchor` returns each baked frame's own anchor.

| Thing | Ground point | Typical canvas | Anchor convention |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ground tile | top vertex of tile | 32×16 (+8 per elevation level) | top-left at `(sx − 16, sy)`. `tile.high_ground` is 32×24 with anchor `(16, 8)`: its top face sits `ZH` above the ground-plane top vertex |
| Tower | tile center | up to 32×48 | the center of the base's footprint diamond (where it meets the ground), horizontally centered |
| Enemy (small / medium / large) | its grid position | 10–16 / 16–24 / 24–32 px | between the feet, 1 px above the bottom row |
| Boss | its grid position | up to 64×64 (`enemy.chitterbloom`: 58×46) | between the feet |
| Flying enemy | ground position (shadow) | as above | sprite drawn 12 px above the anchor, with a shadow blob at the anchor |
| Contact shadow (ground enemy) | the enemy's grid position | 18×6 (`fx.ground_shadow`: `small`, `medium`, `large`) | center; drawn in `decal` at 35% (`groundShadowFor(spriteId)` in `src/content/sprites/shadows.ts` picks the frame by the body's width and gives the alpha). Towers and props bake their own shadow into the frame |
| Allied unit (for example Skitterkin swarmlings sent out by a tower) | its grid position | 8–12 px | between the feet |
| Bricks at his post (`ally.bricks`) | his post's position | 28×30 | the center of the round post plate he stands on (the plate and its shadow are baked into every frame) |
| Link tether (`fx.link_tether`) | tower A's tile center | 37×35, one canvas for every direction | tower A's ground point; the arc rises `TETHER_LIFT` (12 px) above both towers' ground points. `tetherDir(dc, dr)` in the sprite file picks the direction and which tower is A |
| Projectile | its grid position | 2–8 px | center |
| Prop (single tile) | tile center | up to 32×48 | as towers. `prop.wreckage` (32×30) draws its scorched patch inside the frame, so it stands on whatever ground the tile has |
| Banner line (`prop.bunting`) | the front pole's tile center | 40×32 | the front pole's foot; frame `x` slings the string back to a pole two tiles up grid x (32 px left, 16 up), `y` (mirrored) two tiles up grid y. It sorts by that front tile, so units walking under it draw behind the string |
| Burrow mound (`fx.burrow_mound`) | the burrowed unit's grid position | 18×9 | its contact shadow's center, at the hump's foot; drawn in `world` in place of a burrowed body that has no `under` anim (the Digger) |
| Salvage drone (`prop.salvage_drone`) | the ground point under it | 22×30 | that ground point: the body hovers 14 px up inside the frame (no render lift) so the work beam reaches the ground; `fx.shadow` (`small`) goes at the anchor |

### Depth sorting

The world is drawn in **fixed layers** (Phaser `Layer`s), bottom to top:

| # | Layer | Contents | Sorting |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `backdrop` | Sky gradient, stars, far parallax | none |
| 2 | `ground` | Ground tiles, cliffs, water; last, the lighting schedule's multiply grade over the world (A2M3 onward; it also tints the world's scenery in `world`, Sprites too, never the actors, D134, D135), then on a map with darkness the night's ground multiply and the lamplight multiply (`DarknessView`); all kept last when a salvaged tile is drawn | static, by `(i + j)` then `i` |
| 3 | `decal` | Path markings, buildable-tile markers, range rings, shadows, scorch marks; in the dark, the pads' bracket glow and the lamps' light pools (additive, `WorldGlowView`) | insertion order |
| 4 | `world` | Towers, ground enemies, props, tall terrain, Suppressing Fire's live strip; a glowing prop's additive glow just in front of it (`depth + 0.00005`); a boss's shard never under the boss that shed it (at least its owner's depth `+ 0.00005`, `views/shardLook.ts`) | **`depth = footY + kindBias + (uid & 0xff) / 1e4`** |
| 5 | `air` | Flying enemies, lobbed projectiles at the top of their arc | same formula |
| 6 | `vfx` | Projectiles, impacts, particles, glow sprites (additive; a beaming Prism's crystal halo in the dark) | insertion order |
| 7 | `lighting` | Time Dilation's field tint; on a map with darkness (A2M6 through the finale) the actors' night veil (multiply) and the night's lift (additive), both punched out at the sim's lit discs, then the moons added back over the night sky | none |
| 8 | `overlay` | Health bars, tells, floating credits, selection brackets | insertion order |

- `footY` is the anchor's screen y **on the ground plane** (no elevation offset). `kindBias`: high ground 0.05, prop 0.1, enemy 0.2, strip 0.25, tower 0.3, so at equal y, towers draw in front. A **ground strip** (Suppressing Fire's live zone, T232) sorts each tile at its bottom vertex less 1 px (`stripDepth` in `render/depth.ts`): over the enemies on the tile, under a tower or high-ground block on the tile in front.
- The `uid` term breaks ties deterministically so sprites never flicker.
- **An armed clickable sorts raised:** while Pebble can be reached on the Peeker's rock and as she lifts off it, the rock (and her lift) sort as a prop `KEEP_CLEAR_RAISE_PX` (34 px) further down than its `footY`, so ground walkers within its keep-clear footprint and the tile row in front pass behind it (D135; `render/vfx/keepClear.ts`, `views/PeekerView.ts`).
- **Multi-tile props** sort by their front-most (largest `footY`) tile. If a large prop clips wrong, split it into per-tile slices.
- **Elevation:** the playfield has at most **one raised level**: **high ground** tiles (buildable, +range; [design/maps.md](../design/maps.md)). A high-ground tile is a block (diamond top plus 8 px cliff faces) drawn in the **`world` layer** with `kindBias` 0.05, so things in front of it draw over its cliff faces and things behind it are hidden. A tower on high ground draws its anchor `ZH` (8 px) higher but sorts by the tile's ground-plane `footY`. Decorative cliffs at the map edges stay in the `ground` layer. Playfields with more than one raised level are out of scope.
- **Terraces (render-only):** a map's optional `terraces` levels ([design/maps.md § 3](../design/maps.md#3-tile-types)) step the ground down without changing play. Everything stays on the ground plane; the step is drawn as a `tile.cliff` face in the `ground` layer over the **back half of the lower tile** (the half of the tile mask nearest the shared edge, `gy ∈ [row, row + ½)` for a step from row − 1, `gx ∈ [col, col + ½)` for a step from col − 1), so faces along a row of tiles join seamlessly, with a contact shadow below the foot. A path tile takes no face (the road cuts through), a high-ground block covers its own footprint, and a pad or power node keeps its plate clear (`_pad` frames). `cliffFrames` in `src/render/views/groundTiles.ts` picks them.

## 2. Code-generated pixel art format

Per D001, sprites are **palette-indexed character grids** in TypeScript data files. At boot they're rasterized into RGBA buffers, packed into a texture atlas, and registered with Phaser. Nothing about the format is Phaser-specific.

| `src/content/palettes/factions.ts`, `env.ts`, `index.ts` | Faction slot palettes ([art-direction.md §4](art-direction.md#4-faction-visual-language)), environment slot palettes and sky gradients ([art-direction.md §3](art-direction.md#3-environment-palettes-by-act)), and the `PALETTES` registry |

| `src/content/sprites/helpers.ts` | Procedural helpers: the tile mask, `diamond`, `isoBox`, `disc`, `line`, `stamp`, `mirrorRows`, `recolor`, seeded `hash01` |

### The standard legend

Every sprite uses this legend unless it adds its own entries. Each character names a **slot**, and a palette maps slots to master colors, so one grid can be re-skinned by swapping the palette.

| Char | Slot | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `.` | — | transparent |
| `0`–`4` | `p0`–`p4` | primary ramp (0 darkest) |
| `5`–`9` | `s0`–`s4` | secondary ramp |
| `a`–`e` | `a0`–`a4` | accent ramp |
| `+` | `e0` | emissive glow (outer) |
| `*` | `e1` | emissive core |
| `x` | `outline` | hand-placed outline pixel |
| `k` | `eye` | pupils, the darkest detail |
| `w` | `hi` | specular glint |
| `_` | `shadow` | ground shadow (`ink.0` at 50% alpha) |
| `A`–`Z` | custom | per-sprite legend entries mapping directly to master colors (use sparingly) |

### Example: a small Skitterkin enemy

Illustrative only; the real roster is in [design/enemies.md](../design/enemies.md).

```ts
// src/content/sprites/enemies/example_mite.ts
import { defineSprite } from '../defineSprite';

export const exampleMite = defineSprite({
  id: 'enemy.example_mite',
  size: { w: 12, h: 10 },
  anchor: { x: 6, y: 9 },        // between the feet
  palette: 'skitterkin',         // default palette (slot → master color)
  variants: ['skitterkin.ally'], // extra palette variants to bake
  outline: 'auto',               // baker adds a 1px `outline` ring; size and anchor grow by 1
  glow: { slots: ['e0', 'e1'], radius: 2 }, // bakes an additive halo frame
  frames: {
    walk_se_0: [
      '.....aa.....',
      '....1221....',
      '..11233221..',
      '.1123344321.',
      '.12kw23kw21.',
      '112kk233kk21',
      '.1127887211.',
      '..11*77*11..',
      '...111111...',
      '..0.0..0.0..',
    ],
    // A patch frame: copy walk_se_0 and overwrite only the rows that change.
    walk_se_1: { from: 'walk_se_0', patch: { x: 0, y: 9, rows: ['.0.0....0.0.'] } },
  },
  anims: {
    walk_se: { frames: ['walk_se_0', 'walk_se_1'], fps: 8 },
    walk_sw: { mirrorOf: 'walk_se' },  // horizontal mirror, no extra authoring
    // walk_ne / walk_nw: back views, authored separately (no eyes), then mirrored
  },
});
```

### Format rules

- **Grids are validated:** every row must have exactly `w` characters, there must be `h` rows, and every character must be in the legend. Errors name the sprite, frame, row, and column.
- **Frames** are a grid, a patch (`{ from, patch }`, one patch or a list), or a mirror (`{ mirrorOf }`). In patch rows `.` writes transparency and a **space keeps** the pixel underneath. Patches must fit inside the frame.
- **Mirror anims** (`{ mirrorOf: 'walk_se' }`) name each mirrored frame by swapping the facing token in the source frame's name, as a whole `_`-separated token (`se`↔`sw`, `ne`↔`nw`): `walk_se_0` → `walk_sw_0`, `se_fire` → `sw_fire`. A mirrored name that is also authored is an error.
- **Facings:** actors that move have 4 diagonal facings (SE, SW, NE, NW). Author SE and NE; SW and NW are `mirrorOf`. Tower heads that aim may author up to 8 facings (4 authored + mirrors); the minimum is SE and NE.
- **No runtime rotation of pixel art.** Rotating a sprite blurs or jags it unpredictably. Exceptions: projectiles of 4 px or smaller, and particles.
- **Composition:** a tower is a `base` sprite plus a `head` sprite (`tower.<id>` and `tower.<id>_head`). The head's anchor is drawn at the base's `mount: { x, y }` (authored pixels; `SpriteSource.mount` returns it in outlined pixels). Tier visuals are separate frames or patches of the same sprite (`t1`, `t2`, `t3`, `spec_a`, `spec_b`). Everglow bases share one builder: the teal logo chevron on the left face and one orange chevron per tier on the right face. Tiers also change the silhouette so they read at 1× without the chevrons: T2 bolts a striped armor pod onto each side corner (4+ px wider), and T3 adds a sensor mast with a teal tip on the left pod (6+ px taller). **Vrell bases** (`vrellBase` in `towers/vrellBase.ts`, the Zapcoil's) keep the same canvas, anchor, and mount but are patched scrap: a brass plinth with a rusted panel, a crooked patina plate, bolt heads, a pipe elbow, and a white price tag on a string at the front corner; one orange weld bead per tier; T2 adds mismatched pods (a patina capacitor can left, a rusty crate right) and T3 a lightning rod with a welding-hot tip on the crate, so the same 4+ px wider and 6+ px taller rule holds. **Aelari bases** (`aelariBase` in `towers/aelariBase.ts`) keep the canvas, anchor, and mount too: a woven glass dais floating two rows over its shadow, one ruby knot per tier; T2 stands a crystal shard on each side corner and T3 floats a crystal over the left one. Heads that aim author `se`, `ne`, `se_fire`, `ne_fire` and get `sw`/`nw` from `fire_sw`/`fire_nw` mirror anims; heads that don't aim (Sprinkler) author an idle cycle. Rivet, Thumper, Skyhook, and Zapcoil heads also author an up-armored **T2+ head** used from tier 2 on: frames `<facing>_t2` and `<facing>_t2_fire`, anims `fire_<facing>_t2` (Rivet: longer barrels, an orange ammo drum, an armor cap; Thumper: a ready rack of orange shells, a flared muzzle brake; Skyhook: a quad mount with an orange ready-round box; Zapcoil: insulator discs, a bigger toroid with a discharge spike, a scrap counterweight). A head without `_t2` frames is the same at every tier. **Tier-4 specs** change both halves so the tower reads as changed at 1×: base frames `spec_a` and `spec_b` (the plinth with three chevrons and a lit status light, the spec's own kit in place of T2's pods and T3's mast; `everglowSpecBase` in `towers/base.ts`), and head frames `<facing>_spec_<a|b>` and `<facing>_spec_<a|b>_fire` with anims `fire_<facing>_spec_<a|b>`. Rivet Storm (`rivet` A): two big orange ammo crates on the base, a short, fat six-barrel rotary with a belt feed (its fire anim adds a `_spin` frame, the barrels turning). Railspike (`rivet` B): outrigger legs and two teal-banded capacitor coils on the base, one long two-rail gun with a teal charge and a scope. Cluster Thumper (`thumper` A): a bin heaped with orange bomblets at each side corner, a pepperbox muzzle of four bores and a bomblet hopper on the head. Bunker Buster (`thumper` B): slab blast shields with hazard stripes, a longer, thicker tube in reinforcing rings with a heavy ported brake and a big orange round in the bore. Thumper's NE muzzle shows the near lip lit and only a 1 px sliver of the far bore (no tube above it), so it never reads as a visor. Every tower has its specs drawn (T440b; the looks are [art-direction.md §6](art-direction.md#6-story-and-world-sprites)). **Vrell spec bases** use `vrellSpecBase` (`towers/vrellBase.ts`): the patched plinth with three welds, a welding-hot status lamp by the tag, and the spec's kit in place of the pods and rod; **Aelari spec bases** `aelariSpecBase`: the dais with three knots, a white status knot, and the spec's kit. **Idle heads' specs** (Sprinkler, Lookout, Seer's Lantern) are their own idle anims, `<idle>_spec_<a|b>` (`spin_spec_a`, `look_spec_b`) over frames `<idle>_spec_<a|b>_<k>`; the head's first anim stays the tier 1–3 idle (`headStyle`), and render plays the spec's own once the tower specializes (`idleAnim` in `render/views/spriteInfo.ts`). A head whose tower doesn't attack at every tier (the Crane: only Magnet Crane slams) still authors one-frame `fire_<facing>[_suffix]` anims, so the mirrored facings are baked.
- **Tiles** are 32×16 with anchor `(16, 0)` and no outline. They use environment palettes, whose slots are mapped the same way in every act: `p` ground (grass; Act III and finale ground), `s` path, `a` clay/cliff (Act III and finale: crystal), `e0`/`e1` the act's flora accent, `outline` the act's `shade`, `hi` its `light`. Act III's 4-step path ramp repeats its top step, and the finale borrows Act III's path and crystal ramps. Everglow hardware set into tiles (the pad's plate and brackets) uses custom legend entries, so it keeps its steel and teal in every act. `tile.cliff` is an overlay too: frames `ne_<k>` (a lit face stepping down from the tile at row − 1), `nw_<k>` (a shaded face from col − 1), and `ne_pad_<k>`/`nw_pad_<k>` (the same around a plate), two noise variants each, a step darker than the high-ground block's faces (`b`/`a` against `c`/`b`) so the buildable block stays the bright one ([Depth sorting](#depth-sorting)). Ground tiles ship noise variants (`v0`, `v1`, …); `tile.path` adds `tread_x` and `tread_y` (tread marks along each grid axis). `tile.verge` is an overlay, not a ground: frames `<edge>_<k>` (`nw`, `ne`, `se`, `sw`, two noise variants each) hold only the dark grass steps, and `GroundView` draws one over a grass tile for each edge it shares with the road (`dressTile`'s `verge`). Two tiles break the 32×16 rule: `tile.high_ground` (32×24, see [Sprite anchoring](#sprite-anchoring)) and `tile.power_node`, whose `pulse` anim (`v0`, `pulse_1`–`pulse_3`, 4 fps) loops, so `GroundView` draws it as a sprite. **Act II's looks are frames of the same tiles**, which `groundTiles` picks on an Act II map (`isActTwo`): `tile.power_node`'s `conduit` anim (`conduit_0`–`conduit_3`), `tile.high_ground`'s `ledge` and `outcrop` (`highGroundLook`; an `outcrop*` marker picks the second), and `prop.wreckage`'s `v2`/`v3`. `tile.hush` is an overlay like the verge: `d1`–`d3`, a 4×4 ordered dither of `ink.1` (master colors) over a decor tile's grass and the apron near a map's `halls_dark` marker (`hushFrame`), drawn after the cliffs.
- **Portraits** (`portrait.<speaker>`) are 48×48 busts declared through `definePortrait` (`portraits/portrait.ts`), which takes a function that paints whole frames and stores them as the format wants: the default `neutral` is a grid, every other expression a patch over its variant's `neutral` (only the pixels that change), and each talking frame a patch over its expression. They have no `auto` outline: the builder rings the figure with hand-placed `x` pixels inside the frame, and none along the bottom edge where the bust is cut. The anchor is the bottom center (24, 48); portraits are drawn in panels, not on the map. **Frame names:** `[<variant>.]<expression>[_talk]`, where `_talk` is the open mouth of the 2-frame flap and the variant prefix names a portrait variant (an alternate base frame, not a palette variant): `neutral`, `happy_talk`, `party_hat.policy`, `reflect_confetti.sad`. **Anims:** `[<variant>.]talk_<expression>` alternates closed and open at 8 fps. Comms screens (`scanlines: true`, QUILL) add a `<frame>_scan` frame per expression (the other scanline phase; their `_talk` frames use it too, so talking flickers) and `[<variant>.]idle_<expression>` at 4 fps. QUILL's `policy_flash` is the `policy` face with the whole screen in the gold ramp (`gold.3`), and `[<variant>.]policy_in` plays it once for 125 ms before `policy`. The frame-name helpers (`portraitFrame`, `portraitTalkAnim`, `portraitIdleAnim`) and `BASE_EXPRESSIONS` are exported from `portraits/portrait.ts`. The one non-bust is `portrait.gerald`, Gerald's 16×16 speaker icon: a plain `defineSprite` (14×14 with the `auto` outline) that keeps the same frame contract with a single expression (`neutral`, `neutral_talk`, `talk_neutral`).
- **Procedural helpers are allowed.** Sprite files are TypeScript, so a definition can be computed (`mirrorH`, `stamp`, `recolor`, and noise fills for tiles). Ground tiles are mostly generated from the tile mask, the environment ramps, and a seeded noise pattern, not hand-gridded one by one.
- **Enemy anims** are `walk_<facing>` in four facings (SE and NE authored, SW and NW mirrored) for every enemy, flyers included (a flyer's "walk" is its flap or rotor; render lifts it 12 px). An enemy may add another anim in the same four facings for a tell: the Digger's `dig_<facing>` (nose down, dirt flung, the `burrower` tell's 0.8 s dig-in). A sprite for a wave modifier is its own id: `enemy.youngling` (the `youngling` tag's small Nibbler), and `enemy.<id>_fleeing` (a `fleeing` runner's body, built from the unit's walk frames by `fleeingSprite` in `enemies/flee.ts`: `walk` the scurry, `glance` one frame of the look back, `panic` the hop, plus `death`; no palette variants; the Hauler's is `YOUNG_HEADROOM` px taller again for its young's heads). A fleeing body's wave-preview icon, where it has one, is `icon.enemy.<id>_fleeing` (the Hauler's). A Hollow sprite adds `heavy_<facing>` (the Heavy walk) and `tell_<facing>` (the hush reach, one frame). Every Aelari construct (Glimmer, Weft Warden, Lightwing, Veilwalker, Blinkstrider, Cantor, Glimmer-shard) adds `fray1_<facing>` and `fray2_<facing>`, whole copies of its walk with threads loose (`frayLooks` in `enemies/lattice.ts`); the Blinkstrider adds `blink_<facing>` (its blink tell, held); the Rift Moth adds `phase_<facing>` (Phased: its wings folded into a blade, 2 frames, 6 fps). The refugee ally (`ally.refugee`) has `walk_<facing>` and `carry_<facing>` (a little one riding). A **boss** adds an anim per state the sim exposes, each in the same four facings: Chitterbloom has `walk` (4 frames), `tell` (the sneeze tell, `bossTelling` with a sneeze next), `sneeze` (once), `dig` (the dig tell), `under` (`burrowed`: a mound with her crown poking out), `surface` (once), `panic` (phase 3), `puff` (`invulnerableTicks` > 0; render alternates it with its `default+selected` frame for a white-outline shimmer, since there is no baked invulnerable variant), and `flee` (`leaving`). The Glass Witch has `walk` (4 frames), `tell` (the shield pulse's tell), `blink` (the blink tell pose, one held frame), `puff` (between phases), and `flee` (`retreat`); render shows `tell` through a shield pulse's tell (`pulseTelling`) and holds `blink` through a blink tell (`blinkTelling`). The Sunderglass Penitent has `walk` (4 frames), `tell` (the recharge pulse's tell), `broken` (the shield-broken march, 4 frames), `kneel` (the Ward: WardView plays `kneel_se`, 2 frames, 2 fps), and `flee` (the stand-down, once, ending on the kneel's frame). Every Act I Skitterkin a tower can kill also has a facing-free **`death`** anim (`death_0`–`death_3`, 12 fps, once; `deathFrames` in `enemies/death.ts`): squash, a pop of its own fluff round a white star, confetti flung, flecks settling. `EnemyView.die()` plays it on a corpse sprite where the unit fell. Gerald and Chitterbloom have none. **Render picks the anim** from what the sim says the unit is doing (`render/views/enemyPose.ts`, T312b): leaving → `flee`, burrowed → `under`, a boss between phases → `puff`, Phased → `phase`, a tell (`bossTelling`/`bossNextAbility`, `burrowTelling`) → `tell` or `dig`, a blink tell → `blink`, a shield pulse tell → `tell`, a boss phase with haste or a huddle, or a runner held in a hush tell or bolting → `panic`, a Hollow unit in its hush tell → `tell`, a boss in a shield-broken phase with its shield down → `broken`, a Heavy unit → `heavy`, else `walk`, which a hurt Aelari construct swaps for `fray1` below two thirds of its HP and `fray2` below a third (`frayBand`, carrying on from the stride's frame); a fleeing runner glancing back shows `glance` over its walk; `sneeze` plays once when a sneeze tell ends in a stride and `surface` once out of the ground. A sprite without the anim walks, except that a burrowed one without `under` hides under the generic `fx.burrow_mound`. A unit with the `youngling` tag draws `enemy.youngling` whatever its def (`enemyBody` in `render/views/spriteInfo.ts`).
- **Animation budget:** idle 2 frames at 2–4 fps, walk 2–4 frames at 6–10 fps, fire 2–3 frames, death 3–5 frames. Walk playback rate scales with the enemy's current speed, so a slowed enemy visibly walks slower. **Clumps read as individuals** (T117): each unit walks a few pixels to the side of the path's center line by uid (D026), at its own tempo (×0.88–1.12), and from its own start frame (`render/views/walkTempo.ts`), so a tight clump's legs never move in lockstep.

### What the baker produces

For every sprite × palette variant × frame:

1. **Resolve** patches and mirrors into full grids.
2. **Rasterize** into an RGBA buffer through the palette (pure function, tested).
3. **Outline pass** (`outline: 'auto'`): every transparent pixel that is 4-adjacent to an opaque one becomes the palette's `outline` color. (`'auto8'` uses 8-adjacency for chunkier outlines.) Shadow (`_`) pixels don't grow an outline. Shadow pixels bake at 50% alpha.
4. **Derived variants**, baked automatically for every actor:
   - `selected`: outline replaced with `white.1` (hover and selection). Baked for towers and enemies.
   - `cloak`: the Cloaked tell's look ([art-direction.md §8](art-direction.md#trait-tells)), baked for enemies whose default palette is `aelari` or `hollow` (the factions that cloak or phase): the outline goes `white.1` as for `selected`, then body pixels on the odd squares of a checker ((x + y) odd) go clear, shadow pixels with them, and the outline keeps two pixels in every four along each diagonal, so it reads as dashes, the gaps between them its `ink.0` under-stroke at 60% (T501k), so the dashes read on bright ground (`cloakDither` in `bake.ts`). Render draws it for a cloaked, unrevealed unit.
   - `jammed`: every color mapped onto the `steel` step nearest it in luminance (towers under a jam, D001 #4 and [design/enemies.md §4](../design/enemies.md#4-tower-disabling-jamming)). Baked for towers only.
   - Derived variants are named `<variant>+selected`, `<variant>+jammed`, and `<variant>+cloak` and are baked for every palette variant (`default+selected`, `everglow.worn+jammed`).
   - `@glow`: if `glow` is set, emissive pixels (the slots in `glow.slots`) dilated by `radius` with stepped alpha by rounded Euclidean distance (60% on the pixel, 35% at 1 px, 15% at 2 px and beyond; never smooth gradients), each halo pixel taking its nearest emissive pixel's color, drawn additively. The glow frame is padded by `radius` on every side and has its own anchor. Glow frames exist for palette variants, not derived ones.
   - Heavy is not a derived variant: it shows the victim inside, so a Hollow sprite authors it as an anim family, `heavy_<facing>` (Format rules), and render switches anims.
   - `dark` (portraits only, `default+dark`): every color mapped onto an ink step by relative luminance (`ink.3` from 0.2 up, `ink.2` from 0.035, `ink.1` below), so the frame stays a readable silhouette with its brightest parts lightest; the outline goes to `ink.0` ([art-direction.md § Portrait roster](art-direction.md#portrait-roster)).
5. **Pack** into atlas pages and register with Phaser.

**Hit flashes are not baked.** They use Phaser 4's tint fill mode at runtime (`setTint(0xffffff)` plus `setTintMode(Phaser.TintModes.FILL)` for 2 frames), which works the same for hand-made sheets.

### Naming contract (what makes the renderer swappable)

Render code never touches grids. It asks a `SpriteSource` for frames by **stable names**:

```
frame key:  <spriteId>#<variant>:<frame>     e.g. enemy.example_mite#default:walk_se_0
anim key:   <spriteId>#<variant>:<anim>      e.g. enemy.example_mite#skitterkin.ally:walk_se
glow frame: <spriteId>#<variant>:<frame>@glow
```

```ts
interface SpriteSource {
  load(scene: Phaser.Scene): Promise<void>;     // registers textures, frames, anims
  frame(spriteId: SpriteId, frame: string, variant?: string): { texture: string; frame: string };
  glowFrame(spriteId: SpriteId, frame: string, variant?: string): { texture: string; frame: string } | null;
  anim(spriteId: SpriteId, anim: string, variant?: string): string;
  meta(spriteId: SpriteId): { w: number; h: number; anchor: { x: number; y: number } };
  frameAnchor(spriteId: SpriteId, frame: string): { x: number; y: number };  // mirrored frames differ
  mount(spriteId: SpriteId): { x: number; y: number } | null;                // tower bases
}
```

`variant` defaults to `default`. Anims loop unless their definition sets `loop: false` (fire, bursts). The code is the home of the interface: `src/render/sprites/SpriteSource.ts`.

`CodeSpriteSource` is the only implementation for now. `BootScene` bakes and registers it synchronously at boot (`register(scene)`) and stores it in the game registry under `spriteSource`. A future `SheetSpriteSource` would load a PNG atlas plus JSON (for example an Aseprite export) whose frame names follow the same contract, with anchors coming from the same sprite registry. Swapping is then one line in `BootScene`, and sprites can be migrated one at a time because a composite source can try sheets first and fall back to code.

The baked RGBA buffers are also available to the DOM UI (portraits, icons, and scene sprites) as canvases, so the HUD and Phaser share one art pipeline: the UI finds the definition in `SPRITES`, bakes it with `bakeSprite`, picks a frame by `frameKey(id, 'default', frame)`, and shows `imageToCanvas(frame.image).toDataURL()`, cached per frame (`ui/hud/Icon.tsx`, `ui/dialogue/Portrait.tsx`); an anim's frames and fps are its definition's `anims` entry.

### The sprite gallery

`gallery.html` (built alongside the game, entry `src/app/gallery.ts`) shows every registered sprite as a labeled card. Cards pack side by side, each as wide as its frames. The page is laid out as **sheets** of at most 8,000 px (`SheetPacker` in `gallery.ts`): the dioramas, then the sprite cards, then the portraits, each section continuing on the next sheet under a "(continued)" heading when a block wouldn't fit, and each sheet titled "sheet n of N". A new sprite adds sheets, never height to one. A card shows the `default` palette's authored frames at 1× and at an integer zoom (3× up to 20 px, else 2×), on dark (`ink.0`, composited with its `@glow`) and light (`steel.4`), then its mirrored frames at 1× only, on dark; **one more palette variant** (`act3` for env-tinted sprites, else the first: `skitterkin.ally`, `everglow.worn`), its authored frames only (mirrors left out) on dark; and each derived variant (`default+selected`, `default+jammed`, `default+cloak`) by its first frame. A Hollow sprite's `heavy_` and `tell_` frames get a dark row of their own, and a fleeing body (`enemy.<id>_fleeing`; not its `icon.` preview) a compact card: its authored frames only, on dark. Above the cards, small **dioramas** of a map corner place tiles (high ground and a power node among them, the road verge), props (A1M2's three decor markers, the Peeker, and two wreckage tiles, one half cleared under the working salvage drone), towers (heads on mounts, T2+ heads from tier 2, both specs of the Rivet and Thumper, a Skyhook, a firing Zapcoil, one tower on high ground), enemies (Matriarch Chitterbloom, a Brood Hauler, a burst sack and its young, a digging Digger, a Bunchling, an Elder with three younglings), flyers drawn 12 px up over `fx.shadow` (a Puffwing flock and Gerald), a Tidybot vacuuming (Acts I–II), Bricks at his post, link tethers, and VFX by their anchors with depth sorting, once in `act1` at 2× and once per environment palette at 1×; the Act II palettes dress the corner as an Act II map (`dioramaGrid`: its decor in Act II's looks, the crew's hut, an exposed warren, a spoil heap, Rigs 7 and 8 damaged, grey soil and a mining scar from the map's dressing layers, a dark corner), each over its env's sky gradient (stepped bands, ordered-dithered seams) and `shade`, with ground enemies on their contact shadows. After them, the **M5 corner** (`m5Corner`, at 2× on `act2.dusk`, in its decor apron): by dusk, the Ward (the Penitent's `kneel`) over the road as refugees file past and a Prism beaming at a Hollow shard (the beam at its cap, its knot holding); at full dark, a lamp's pool, the beaming Prism's lit disc, a resting Prism's ghost and the Hollow on the road, composited as the game layers the night (the ground's multiply and lamplight, the pools, the actors, the beam and the crystal's halo, then the veil and the lift; `vfx/darknessPixels.ts`, `vfx/glowPixels.ts`). A full-screen scene sprite (`scene.*`) shows its frames on dark only. Portraits get their own section after the sprite cards, two cards to a row (a portrait with more than 16 frames takes a whole row): each portrait variant's expressions at 2× in a comms-panel frame, then every frame of `default` and `default+dark` at 1×. The page is deterministic (no time, no randomness, no running animations).

The `smoke/gallery.spec.ts` smoke test opens it at a 1600 px wide viewport (the width the sheets are packed at), checks the `data-*` counters on `<body>` (sprite count equals the registry; every frame a card plans to show rendered; the sheet count), and captures each sheet as its own image: **`test-results/gallery/sheet-01.png`, `sheet-02.png`, …** (under `SMOKE_OUT` when set), the milestone art trail. Each sheet is one screenshot, under Chrome's 16,384 px limit and small enough to send whole; the test fails, naming the sheet, if a single block alone is taller than a sheet. The art trail keeps them in `docs/art/gallery/m<N>/` from M5 on (`m1.png`–`m4.png` are single stitched pages).
