# Art Direction

How Stardust looks: the palette, faction visual language, portraits, VFX, readability, and UI style. The technical spec that makes the look reproducible in code (isometric math, anchoring, depth sorting, the sprite format, the baker) is in [tech-spec.md](tech-spec.md). For *what* the factions, characters, and places are, see [story/races.md](../story/races.md), [story/characters.md](../story/characters.md), and [design/maps.md](../design/maps.md). This doc covers how they **look**.

Binding constraint: **D001 #1.** All art is generated by code from palette-indexed grids. The one exception to "no binary art assets" is listed in [§9 UI style](#9-ui-style).

---

## 1. The look in one paragraph

A sun-bleached sci-fi frontier seen from a 2:1 isometric camera, drawn in chunky, hand-placed pixels. Corporate Everglow hardware is clean, white, and teal, like frontier supply depots repainted by a cheerful brand team. Starship Troopers supplies the propaganda gloss and the bug-hunt framing. Everything that matters to play **glows**: saturated emissives, white-hot projectile cores, and bright ability tells, all set against **deep, inky darks** that get deeper every act. Act I reads like a candy-colored brochure. Act III looks like the brochure on fire at night.

### Principles

1. **Value first, hue second.** Every scene has three value bands: environment (low to mid), actors (mid to high, with dark outlines), and emissives and projectiles (highest, with white cores). If a screenshot turned to grayscale is still readable, the art is correct.
2. **Darks are colored, never gray.** The darkest ink, `ink.0`, is violet-black. Shadows shift hue toward violet or blue, and highlights shift toward yellow (hue-shifted ramps).
3. **Saturation is earned.** Large areas (ground, sky) stay softer. Full saturation is reserved for actors, emissives, and UI accents.
4. **Shape tells the truth.** Every gameplay-relevant state has a shape or motion cue as well as a color (see [§8](#8-readability-and-enemy-ability-tells)).
5. **The palette is a story dial.** The palette darkens gradually across maps, not with a switch at each act boundary. Actors keep their faction palettes; the world, the light, and the UI theme around them change.
6. **Warm gold means remembered; cold violet means forgotten.** This color language is canon ([story/bible.md](../story/bible.md#what-stardust-really-is)): sung stardust, the dead at rest, and anything laid to rest are warm pink-gold; unsung stardust, the vats, drill spoil, and the Hollow's scattered pieces are cold violet. Never swap them for decoration.
7. **No mixels.** Every on-screen pixel is the same size. Nothing is scaled by a non-integer, and nothing pixel-art is scaled at all inside the game view (portraits and icons are authored at their display size).

## 2. Master palette

**Every color in the game comes from the master palette.** Its hex values live in one place, [`src/content/palettes/master.ts`](../../src/content/palettes/master.ts): change colors there, never here. Sprite palettes, environment ramps, and UI themes reference colors by name (for example `teal.3`), never by raw hex. A content test enforces this ([conventions.md](../tech/conventions.md#testing)). This section names the ramps and what each is for.

Ramps run from `0` (darkest) to `4` (lightest) and are hue-shifted: shadows lean violet or blue, and highlights lean warm.

### Neutrals

| Ramp | Steps | Use |
|---|---|---|
| `ink` | 0–4 | Outlines, the deepest shadows, UI panels, letterbox. `ink.0` is violet-black, never gray |
| `steel` | 0–4 | Metal, Everglow hardware, UI chrome |
| `white` | 0–1 | Highlights, projectile cores, Everglow paint (`white.1` is pure white) |

### Hues

Nine saturated five-step ramps: `red`, `coral`, `orange`, `gold`, `green`, `teal`, `blue`, `violet`, `pink`. Their faction and UI roles are in [§4](#4-faction-visual-language) and [§9](#9-ui-style).

### Materials and skin

| Ramp | Use |
|---|---|
| `skin.a`, `skin.b`, `skin.c` | Human skin: light to medium, medium to deep, deep |
| `brass` | Vrell hardware |
| `patina` | Vrell oxidized metal |
| `grime` | Dirt, scavenged plating, mining scars |
| `hollow` | The Hollow's drained body color |
| `acid` | **Reserved for the Hollow** (see the hue reservation below) |

**Hue reservation.** Saturated yellow-green (hue 60–85°, HSV saturation above 60%, value above 50%) belongs **only** to the Hollow. No other faction, environment, or UI element may use it. When players see that color, they should feel that something is wrong before they can say why. A palette test enforces the reservation.

### Stardust shimmer

The mineral that gives the game its name is drawn with **palette cycles** (3 colors rotating every 150 ms on its emissive pixels). There are two kinds, plus the currency icon:

| Material | Cycle | Where |
|---|---|---|
| `stardust.sung` | `pink.3` → `gold.3` → `gold.4` | Natural seams, the Deep Halls, Bricks's Tumble IV stone, the Returned's sacks once warmed, anything laid to rest. Never changes by act |
| `stardust.unsung` | `violet.3` → `violet.4` → `white.1` | Second Harvest's vats and output, drill spoil, the Ascendant's cargo, Sterling's snow globe, the Hollow's scattered motes. Never changes by act |

**The Stardust currency icon** follows where the Stardust comes from ([design/progression.md §1](../design/progression.md#1-currencies)). In Acts I–II it is Everglow's processed product, sold as candy, and its cycle cools as the truth comes out. From A3M1 the warrens *give* it as sung stones, so the currency becomes trust and the icon changes shape as well as color:

| Phase | Icon shape | Cycle | Feel |
|---|---|---|---|
| Act I | Candy-cut gem | `pink.3` → `gold.3` → `gold.4` | Sparkly pink-gold candy (the brochure) |
| Act II | Candy-cut gem | `pink.3` → `orange.3` → `violet.4` | Warm, with a cold edge creeping in |
| Act III | Gift stone ([§6](#6-story-and-world-sprites)) | `stardust.sung` | Warm gold, given rather than harvested |
| Finale | Gift stone | `gold.3` → `pink.3` → `gold.4` | Warm gold: remembered, not undone |

As built (T843; `icons/stardust.ts`): one 8×8 icon per phase (6×6 plus outline, the currency icons' size), each a still with the icons' one `icon` frame: `icon.stardust` (Act I), `icon.stardust_act2`, `icon.stardust_act3`, `icon.stardust_finale` (`STARDUST_ICON` by phase). The gem is a cut diamond with a `white.1` glint on its cycle's colours over a shade (`pink.2` in Act I, `violet.2` in Act II); the stone is gold all through (`gold.3` body, `gold.2`/`gold.1` shade) with only its glints in the cycle and its scratch `pink.3`. The icons hold still (the cycle's first step); the stone set down in the world shimmers (§6). Which phase a screen shows, and where (the banks, the payroll), is the screens' choice.

## 3. Environment palettes by act

Each map picks an **environment palette** (`env` field in its map data). The palette is a set of named ramps that the procedural tile generator and props use. Maps within an act step gradually through the palette variants listed here (for example, Act II maps move from `act2.gold` to `act2.dusk`), so the darkening is continuous.

The light is **diegetic**: one Tumble day lasts about 38 standard days, so the acts are the Long Afternoon, the Long Dusk, the Long Night, and the First Dawn ([story/bible.md §3](../story/bible.md#tumble-iv)). The sun really sets at the end of A2M6 and rises once, in the finale's last phase. Two small moons hang in the sky backdrop in every act (they are the only sky light in Act III). The battle sky (a map with a lighting schedule, D114) hangs them up right of the map's north corner, where the HUD and the playfield leave sky (`SKY_MOONS` in `render/vfx/lighting.ts`: a 6 px `white.0` moon shaded `steel.3`, a 3 px `pink.4` one shaded `coral.3`), the same in every stop so a crossfade never doubles them.

Actors (towers, enemies, allies, projectiles) **keep their faction palettes in every act**. Only the world, sky, lighting overlay, and UI theme change; a lighting schedule's grade dims the world, never the actors (§7, D134).

### Act I: the Long Afternoon (`act1`)

Deep, saturated greens, pink clay, a dusty-rose sand track, and a cyan sky. Shadows are lavender, not black, but still clearly dark. The ground sits a value band **below** the actors (principle 1): body luminance runs grass ≈ 0.20 < path ≈ 0.31 < every enemy, ally, and tower (≈ 0.37–0.42), so a Skitterkin on the road reads in grayscale by its value, not only its outline. A palette test holds the order (grass < path, path/grass ≥ 1.3:1, each actor ≥ 1.2:1 over the path). Grass tiles carry two soft mid-dark clumps each (a solid `p1` heart with a dithered rim), and grass beside the road carries a **verge** (`tile.verge`, an overlay per shared edge): clumps of `p1` 1–3 px deep with a dithered rim and a few `p0` roots at the road's lip. Together they give the playfield mid-darks between the lit ground and the deep apron, and the road a darker edge.

Ramps (`act1.*`): `grass` (deep green), `clay` (pink: cliffs, soil), `path` (the dusty-rose sand track), `water` (4 steps), `sky` (the cyan backdrop gradient, 3 steps), and `shade` (lavender) / `light` (warm cream). Flora accents: `pink.3`, `pink.4`, `gold.4`. Everglow billboards and domes: `white.0` and `teal.3`.

### The apron (every act)

No map floats in the void. `GroundView` draws a 4-tile **apron** of non-playable ground around the grid (`tile.apron`): ring 1 is the env's ground one ramp step darker, and rings 2–4 dither it to `ink.0` with a 4×4 ordered pattern that thickens ring by ring (4, 9, then 14 of 16 cells), so the map's edge sinks into the deep dark instead of stopping at a hard diamond. Ring 1 carries the colony's perimeter **fence** (`prop.fence`, Everglow steel posts and wire), with gaps beside border spawns and where the road leaves. The **road runs on** out of every border core tile, across all four rings (the road out through the gate). Ring 2 carries sparse brush and rocks plus the act's dressing, always on the north edges so it never hides the playfield or the HUD's top-center banner. Act I's dressing is the colony: an Evie billboard and lettuce patches on the north-west, Dome Row domes on the north-east. Act III's is the colony in the Long Night: Evie's billboard `dark` on the north-west under an emergency lamp (`prop.emergency_lamp`, alarm red), crystal beside it, and Dome Row `dark` on the north-east with a lamp, its sparse brush crystal and its rocks `night`. The apron is outside the grid, so picking never reaches it. Placement rules: `apronCells` in `src/render/views/groundTiles.ts`.

### Act II: the Long Dusk (`act2.gold`, `act2.dusk`)

Sun-baked gold grass, rust soil, ember highlights. By the end of the act the sky is violet and the shadows are deep plum. Grey **mining scars** (`grime` ramp) spread across revisited maps as dressing layers over the grass (`tile.grime`): `greySoil` streaks it, `mineScar` cuts it to bare grey earth with bench lines and no flora (A2M3's grey-streaked terraces and its strip mine; [design/maps.md §7](../design/maps.md#7-storytelling-with-the-map)). The light can move during a map: **A2M3 (Second Look)** crossfades its sky and shade from `act2.gold` to `act2.dusk` across its waves, a cosmetic change with no effect on play (the lighting overlay's tint path, [§7](#glow-and-bloom)). **A2M6 (Sundown)** is the one map where the dark changes play: it starts on `act2.dusk`, and the lighting overlay fades in across its waves until the last wave is fought in full Long Night darkness.

Like Act I's, the ground sits a value band **below** the units (principle 1): body luminance runs grass ≈ 0.22 < the rust road ≈ 0.31 < every Skitterkin, ally, and tower (≈ 0.39 and up), and the Hollow's dark bodies (≈ 0.11) sit ≥ 2:1 under the road and ≥ 1.6:1 under the grass. The Aelari's lattices have real holes, so they read by their knots (≥ 2:1 over the road) inside a `blue.0` rim (≥ 3:1 under it). A palette test holds all of it for both palettes. The band leaves little room: the road must stay ≥ 1.5:1 off the pad and 1.3:1 over the grass while Act I's dimmest bodies stay 1.2:1 over it, so retune the three together.

The playfield's Act II landmarks: the **power node** is a stardust conduit (a flanged Everglow pipe across the plate, its glass channel flowing cold violet), **high ground** is a canyon ledge (rock lips, three strata, cracks) or, where a map marks it `outcrop*`, an Aelari thread-glass outcrop (a `catwalk*` or `watchtower*` marker builds a plant catwalk or the colony's old watchtower, [§6](#6-story-and-world-sprites)), and a map's **dark edge** (`halls_dark` marker, `tile.hush`) dithers `ink.1` over the decor nearest it, deepening toward the marker, with a stray cold violet mote. Wreckage is the broken mining rigs ([§6](#6-story-and-world-sprites)), and the apron's dressing is the mining frontier: an Evie billboard `peeling` on the north-west, drill spoil heaps on the north.

Ramps: `act2.grass` (sun-baked gold: hue 38–42° on its body steps, saturated ≥ 75%, so it reads as gold and never olive; the palette test holds the hue, the saturation, and its ≈ 0.22 value), `act2.clay` (rust), and `act2.path` are shared by both palettes. Each has its own `sky` (3 steps) and `shade` / `light`: `act2.gold` has a sunset-orange sky, plum shade, and peach light; `act2.dusk` has a violet-to-rose sky, near-black plum shade, and lilac light. Accents: `violet.3`, `orange.3`, and embers `orange.4`.

### Act III: the Long Night (`act3`)

Near-black ground, cold-violet crystal growths (unsung stardust spreading from the mines), and alarm-red Everglow emergency lighting. Darkness is now the default, and light comes only from emissives and the two moons (see the lighting overlay in [§7](#7-vfx)).

Ramps (`act3.*`): `ground` (near-black to deep violet), `crystal` (cold violet up to white), `path` (4 steps; its top step repeats in the tile palette), `alarm` (`red.1`, two alarm reds of its own, `red.4`), `sky` (2 steps of coloured near-black: a deep violet-black at the top over an `ink`-dark one), and `shade` (a violet near-black, never `#000`; it is the tiles' outline) / `light` (pale violet) / `emergency` (alarm red).

Like Acts I–II's, the ground sits a value band **below** everything standing on it (principle 1), and here by a wide one: body luminance runs ground ≈ 0.01 < the road ≈ 0.04 < every unit, ally, and tower (≈ 0.2 and up, each ≥ 2:1 over the road). The Hollow, dark on dark, read by their pale `hollow.4` rim (≥ 3:1 over the road and over their own bodies), their bodies staying under the units' band. No sky band or env shade in any act is pure black: every dark keeps its hue (principle 2). A palette test holds all of it.

The playfield's Act III looks (`ACT3_LOOKS` in `render/views/groundTiles.ts`; which env takes which decor looks, hides the Peeker, draws night rings and glows as the Long Night is one row per env in `render/views/envLook.ts`): the brush is **crystal** (`prop.flora`'s `crystal`: cold violet shards breaking out of the ground, dim `a`–`c` faces with a few lit tips, never a bright blob), the rocks sit in the dark (`prop.rock`'s `night`: the boulder's crystal steps pushed down to the ground's, only its crown catching light), and a terrace bank is crystal too (the `a` ramp), so a bowl's back walls glow faintly violet (A3M1's Preserve, a bowl stepping down to its inner basin). Everglow kit keeps its colours but is out or on emergency power: the billboards `dark`, the domes `dark`, the fountain `night`, the podium empty; A2M4's vats, at maximum throughput, burn at full `blaze`. A rift lane's ground cracks ([§8](#tile-readability)).

**The Long Night's light** (`render/vfx/nightGlow.ts`, `views/WorldGlowView.ts`). Act III's maps carry no lighting schedule and no darkness (D141), so the `act3` look is the env palette baked into the ground and the props, plus the looks above; nothing grades the world, the actors, or the `decal` layer. The world still glows as at night: on an `act3` map every piece of scenery with a baked `@glow` wears it at 75% of its full-dark strength (`NIGHT_GLOW`: the core's heart, the domes' one red slit, the crystal's lit tips, a warren home's glowworm lantern and lit window), the apron's emergency lamps burn red (`PROP_GLOWS`), and the two moons hang steady at full strength up right of the map's north corner, as over a scheduled map's night ([§7](#7-vfx)). The props whose frame changes on a cue (Sterling's shuttle and its pad, the blast doors) and the rift cracks wear a glow that follows the frame they show (`views/frameGlow.ts`). Every other prop's glow follows its frame too (WorldGlowView: its `PROP_GLOWS` light, over the union of the anim the frame is in, else its baked `@glow`): A2M4's vats at full `blaze` glow over the blaze's own frames, overflow included; A3M5's burst feed lines (wreckage `v6`/`v7`) glow where their slurry wells up; Dome 1's warm door and slits glow through its frost; the hangar's lamps follow it open; and A3M4's generator wears the core's full-strength heart. Acts I–II, and any map with darkness, light exactly as their schedule and the sim's `dark` say (`nightGlow.test.ts` pins it).

### Finale: dawn through the dark (`finale`)

The finale follows its three phases ([design/maps.md §5.5](../design/maps.md#55-the-leaving-song-finale-rules)):
- **Phases 1–2 (the Descent, the Unsung):** still night, on the Act III ground, with one new warm source: the **choir's song aura is a warm-gold light** punched into the darkness overlay, moving with the choir. The Halls' natural seams glow `stardust.sung`; the Deep Bore glows `stardust.unsung`. The world's light does not brighten per wave; only the **eastern sky** does (D154's T701 ruling): the horizon behind the map's east edge warms a step as each Phase 1 wave clears.
- **Phase 3 (Dawn):** when the countdown reaches 0, the first sunlight in nineteen days rakes in from the east edge over about 8 s, lifting the darkness overlay and switching to the dawn ramps below. Highlights go gold and cyan. Sunrise is the reward, and it comes from the song, not from clearing waves.

**As built (T831).** The finale's light is render's alone (D154 addendum): the map stands on `act3` with no Sundown darkness, so no tower loses range and nothing on screen reads as a range penalty.
- **The night** (`views/SongNightView.ts`, `vfx/songNightPixels.ts`): on a song map without darkness, from the first tick until Dawn, one view-sized mask darkens the **world only**, 70% of the way to Sundown's plum (`SONG_NIGHT_STRENGTH`): a multiply over the drawn ground (the map and its apron; the sky behind stays clear, so the horizon shows), and the same tone as a tint on every piece of scenery standing in the dark. The actors take no veil and no lift. Pads, the road and every tell keep reading (the pads' teal brackets, the Hollow's pale rims). The relit waymark lanterns are lights of their own and never darken.
- **The song's light:** each song circle (the leader's, each Returned's) is a hard-edged hole in the night exactly the size of its drawn ring (ChoirWalkView's snapped radius at the ring's pixel), so what reads lit is where a kill is laid to rest, with a `gold.3` pool in it (`decal`, additive; glowPixels' stepped bands). The mask redraws only when a circle's pixel or radius changes (about one redraw every three seconds at 1×).
- **The eastern sky** (`fin_m01/lighting.ts` `FIN_SKY`; `vfx/lighting.ts` `skyStops`, `eastSkyImage`): sky-only lighting keys hold the night through W1, then five steps to W19 (W18 cleared), reached in equal steps per wave. Each step is the `finale` sky's gradient risen from the sky's foot at its east (right) edge in an ellipse (its radii the step's share of `EAST_REACH`, 55% of the width and 85% of the sky's height at the last step), the dawn's gold at the horizon and its night violet at the rim, each seam dithered into Act III's night. The grade and the night never follow them. On a Long Night map the skies leave the moons to WorldGlowView, so they aren't added twice.
- **Dawn:** from Dawn's `setLighting: 'dawn'`, the sun rakes in from the east edge (grid +x, the map's lower-right edge) over the set's 8 s, in 48 steps: a front crossing the map westward, the night cleared behind it (a dithered edge), a band of raking `gold.3` just behind it (32% additive), and the morning's wash (10% additive) over all the ground it has passed, which stays. The sky crossfades to the whole dawn gradient over the same 8 s. The rift cracks heal behind the front: each `tile.rift` half the sun has reached closes back to its old scar (`views/RiftView.ts`, from `SongNightView.sunStep`; T871), so no violet tear burns across the morning. The sun lands as the map is won. Under reduced motion it lands at once.

The playfield's Halls looks (the map with a `halls_mouth` marker, `isHalls` in `render/views/groundTiles.ts`; the finale's map is baked on `act3`): **high ground** is a Halls-mouth ledge (`tile.high_ground`'s `halls`: the Spires' dark rock, `ink.2`–`ink.4` under a lit `steel.1` lip, two strata, a sung-gold seam running through its faces), each **power node** is a natural seam of sung stardust (`tile.power_node`'s `seam`, `seam_0`–`seam_3` at 4 fps: the plate set on a jagged gold vein along the grid's y axis, pink where it runs thickest, a dark lip under it, a glint creeping along it, a knot of gold crystal where the socket would be), the north edge is the **Halls' mouth** on the apron (§6), and the apron is the Spires (`spiresDecor`: crystal and rocks in the dark, no billboard and no Dome Row; nothing behind the mouth).

Ramps (`finale.*`): `ground` (dark violet warming to peach), `sky` (the dawn gradient, 5 steps: night violet through rose and orange to gold), and `shade` / `light`; it borrows Act III's path and crystal ramps. The `hope` accents are `gold.3`, `teal.4`, and `white.1`.

Every environment ramp is an entry in the master palette, namespaced by act (`act1.grass.2`), in [`master.ts`](../../src/content/palettes/master.ts).

## 4. Faction visual language

Each faction's palette fills the same **slots**: primary ramp `p`, secondary ramp `s`, accent ramp `a`, emissives `e0`/`e1`, `eye`, `hi`, and `outline` (see [tech-spec.md § The standard legend](tech-spec.md#the-standard-legend)). Swapping one palette for another re-skins any sprite. Lore lives in [story/races.md](../story/races.md).

| Faction | Shape language | `p` | `s` | `a` | `e0` / `e1` | `outline` |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Everglow** (clean corporate: the human towers and Everglow Security) | Rounded rectangles, panel lines, chevrons, logo plates, symmetry | `steel` + `white` | `teal` | `orange` (safety) | `teal.3` / `teal.4` | `ink.0` |
| **Skitterkin** (warm, organic, cute) | Round bodies, big eyes, tufts, stubby legs, a slight bounce in every animation | `coral` | `gold` (butter bellies) | `green` (moss tufts) | `gold.3` / `gold.4` (bioluminescent spots) | `ink.0` |
| **Aelari** (chorded and woven, jewel-toned, humming) | **Woven, never plated:** bodies and constructs are open lattices of glass thread, like stained-glass weaving. Taut 1 px strands cross at bright knot-nodes, with see-through gaps between them; silhouettes are tall and near-symmetric | `blue` (sapphire) | `teal` (emerald) | `pink` (ruby) | `teal.4` / `white.1` | `blue.0` (colored outline, which makes them look lit from inside) |
| **Vrell** (patchwork, brass, scavenged) | Asymmetry, bolted plates, mismatched parts, exposed pipes, one oversized limb | `brass` | `orange` 0–2 (rust) | `patina` | `orange.3` / `orange.4` (welding glow) | `ink.0` |
| **The Hollow** (wrong, drained, unmourned) | Wrong geometry: silhouettes of what its pieces were (mostly Skitterkin, some Aelari, a few unrecognizable) drawn wrong, shapes that don't match their animation, missing pixels, limbs that pop between frames, no eyes or too many | `hollow` | `hollow` | `acid` | `acid.2` / `acid.3` | **`hollow.4` (inverted)** |

### Faction rules

- **Inverted outlines belong to the Hollow alone.** Every other actor has a dark outline. Hollow sprites have a *pale* outline around dark interiors. That makes them readable as "wrong" whatever the viewer's color vision, because it's a value cue, not a hue cue.
- **Hollow glitch:** Hollow sprites drop 1–3 pixels per frame (baked into each authored frame from a seed, `dropPixels` in `enemies/hollow.ts`: a hole inside the body comes out of the outline pass as a pale speck, one on the edge bites the silhouette, and the missing pixels change as the walk plays), and every 2–4 s they show one frame of horizontal offset (render's, §7 *The Hollow in motion*).
- **Skitterkin animations are cute by default:** a squash-and-stretch bounce on the walk cycle, blinks on idle. When Skitterkin sprites are reused as allied towers ([research §4](../research/emotional-storytelling.md)), use the **same sprite** with the `skitterkin.ally` palette variant (identical `p`/`s`, with `a` swapped to `teal`, a nod to the alliance) so the player recognizes the creatures they used to shoot.
- **Aelari threads hum and fray.** Strands carry a slow 1 px vibration (alternate frames shift one strand pixel, 2–4 fps) so constructs read as sounding, not solid. When an Aelari unit is damaged, loose 1 px thread-ends fray out from its edges instead of chipping or cracking: every construct's walk has two hurt copies, `fray1_<facing>` (three threads pulled loose) and `fray2_<facing>` (those and three more, longer, the rim notched where each leaves and a few strands snapped inside), at fixed angles round its middle so they stay put as it bobs (`frayLooks` in `enemies/lattice.ts`). Render swaps the walk for `fray1` below two thirds of the unit's HP and `fray2` below a third, picking up the stride on the frame it was on (`frayBand` in `render/views/enemyPose.ts`; HP only, not the shield; bosses never fray). Interior gaps are real transparent pixels, so the ground shows through the lattice: no other faction has holes in its silhouette.
- **Everglow hardware wears the brand:** the logo plate (a 3×3 teal chevron) appears on every Everglow tower base. Later acts can swap in vandalized or faded variants via palette (`everglow.worn`: `white.0` → `grime.4`, `teal.3` → `teal.2`).
- **Jailbroken Everglow towers** (after A3M4, [design/towers.md §9](../design/towers.md#9-rules-for-story-driven-tower-changes)) use the `everglow.jailbroken` palette: `everglow.worn` with its status lights (`e0`/`e1`) burning the Vrell's welding `orange.3`/`orange.4` (Zibzab's firmware), so the class reads changed at 1× by its glow. Every Everglow base also authors a `_jb` twin of each frame (`t1_jb` … `spec_b_jb`, `withJailbreak` in `towers/base.ts`): the 3×3 logo plate scratched out with a `grime.3` cross and a small `brass` Vrell patch bolted on beside it, painted only over the plate and the bare left face, so a spec kit standing there keeps its pixels. Render draws a class in the save's `unlocks.jailbroken` with the twin, base and head in the palette (`jailbreakLook` in `render/views/towerLook.ts`).

## 7. VFX

All VFX follow the pixel rules: integer positions, palette colors only, and **stepped** alpha (100/60/35/15%) or dithering instead of smooth gradients.

### Projectiles

Projectiles are the brightest things on screen. Every projectile has a **white or `*e1` core** and a faction-colored edge, so it reads against any act's ground.

Each **damage type** ([design/enemies.md §2](../design/enemies.md#2-combat-resolution)) has its own *shape* of delivery, so the player can tell types apart without relying on color:

| Damage type | Shape | Look |
|---|---|---|
| **Kinetic** | Straight, hard-edged | 2×1 or 3×1 tracer streaks, rivets, harpoons with a 1 px tether line; 1-frame muzzle flash; `steel`/`white` with a warm core |
| **Explosive** | Lobbed arc, then a burst | Shell drawn in the `air` layer at the top of its arc, with a ground shadow that shrinks toward the apex and a 2–3 px smoke-puff trail; round burst sized to the splash radius |
| **Energy** | Glowing, electric | 3×3 to 5×5 bolts with a `@glow` halo; beams (1–2 px line with a 1 px white core, flickering between 2 widths at 15 Hz); chain arcs (a jagged polyline regenerated every 2 frames with cosmetic RNG); `teal`/`blue` |
| **Psionic** | Ripples, no solid projectile | Concentric 1 px rings or a wavering line that bends space; target sprite jitters 1 px for 2 frames on hit; `violet`/`pink` |

Faction flavor goes on top: Aelari shots are thrown threads (a taut 1 px jewel-toned strand that snaps from tower to target, with a 1-frame knot sparkle at the hit: `fx.beam_knot`'s `spark`, a white star flinging four thread-ends, then a teal knot in a ruby ring, loosening, once at 15 fps; its `hold` is the knot burning on a held target, its threads flicking between two lengths at the beam's 15 Hz), and Vrell shots are mismatched scrap. **Beams as built** (`render/views/BeamView.ts`): on each lock the strand is thrown from the crystal (14 px over the tile) to the middle of the target's body over 90 ms, then the knot sparks there and holds while the beam stays on; a retarget throws a new thread (under reduced motion the thread is whole at once and the hold doesn't flicker). The ramp reads as heat in thirds of the way to its cap: a taut 1 px `blue.3` thread, a 3 px `teal.4` one, then a 4 px `teal.4` beam with a `white.1` core, each over a `blue.0` under-stroke and a pixel wider on the 15 Hz flicker.

### Impacts, hits, and deaths

- **Hit flash:** 2 frames of white tint fill on the target. Never longer: hits must not hide the sprite.
- **Impact burst:** a 3–5 frame sprite, sized by damage class: small 5×5, medium about 11×11 (`fx.bomblet_burst`), large about 19×19 (`fx.explosion`). Its first frame is the biggest and brightest (a white-hot star with spikes), so the hit lands on the frame it happens; then fireball, ring, smoke. The smoke is small solid puffs (a little cloud: a `p3` rim round a `p4` core, `puff` in `fx/explosion.ts`), inside the ring and then drifting apart, never a 1 px checker, which reads as a transparency grid at 1×; the view fades it out by alpha.
- **Death tone ramp (a story dial):** Skitterkin deaths in Act I are slapstick: a "poof" of fluff and confetti pixels. As built, every Act I Skitterkin a tower can kill has a `death` anim in its own colours (a third of a second, once): the body squashes flat and wide, pops into a lumpy cloud of its own fluff around a white star, flings confetti (its shimmer, belly, and light colours), and leaves a few flecks settling on the ground. Over Act II the confetti thins and a small stardust fleck lingers. By Act III there is no confetti, just a short, quiet fade of a still body. The variant is chosen by the map's act; the rules are in [design/enemies.md §8](../design/enemies.md#8-death-and-feedback-by-act). **As built (T430a; `render/views/deathLook.ts`):** an Act II Skitterkin plays only its `death` anim's first two frames (the squash and the pop, no confetti) and leaves one fleck, a `gold.4` and a `pink.3` pixel side by side, lying on the ground for 1.4 s; an Aelari unit shatters (no body left: twelve 1–2 px shards in its jewel tones, `blue.4`, `teal.4`, `white.1`, `pink.4`, `blue.3`, flung out on a 2:1 squashed circle and falling over 560 ms, with a half-tile `white.1` flash); a Hollow unit unravels into eight `stardust.unsung` motes that drift up and apart over 780 ms, stepping down the alpha ladder, never a pop and never warm. The shards, shield shards and flecks are pooled debris pixels on real time, at most 128 alive; the motes are pooled `fx.unravel_mote` sprites (below), at most 48 alive. Act III draws no still body: no Skitterkin is an enemy in Act III ([act3.md](../story/campaign/act3.md)), so nothing dies that way (a guard test in `sprites/m6units.test.ts` holds it; the `still` style falls back to the quiet pop). **Everglow Security (T603; `views/EverglowView.ts`, `everglowLook.ts`)** shuts down in every act: a `white.1` power-off line over an `ink.0` rim at the body collapses in whole pixels from 13 px wide to a dot over 140 ms and winks out by 260 ms, and an 11×10 price tag (a white tag with a punched hole and a `gold.3` price line on a `steel.4` string, in an `ink.0` rim) hung over the body drops under gravity to the ground in 450 ms and fades out by 900 ms (it fades where it hung under reduced motion). ### Glow and bloom

There are three tiers, cheapest first. The game must look right with only tier 1.

1. **Baked emissives (always on).** Emissive pixels use the brightest ramp steps plus white cores. Most of the "glow" is painted in.
2. **Glow sprites (default on).** `@glow` frames (stepped-alpha halos) drawn additively in the `vfx` layer, under projectiles and emissive actors.
3. **Camera bloom (setting: Off / Low / High; default Low on desktop).** A Phaser 4 bloom filter on the world camera (`Phaser.Actions.AddEffectBloom`). Because the camera renders at the 640×360 internal resolution, the bloom stays chunky and pixel-consistent. It's skipped automatically if frame time exceeds budget.

**The lighting overlay's tint path (A2M3 onward).** A map's lighting schedule (`MapDef.lighting`: keys that each name a wave and an env palette) moves the light as its waves start, easing over 4 s of sim time (so it holds while paused), and stepping under reduced motion. Two parts follow it, nothing else: the **sky**, each env's `sky` gradient as dithered bands across the top of the view that dither out into the void behind the map (the `backdrop` layer), crossfaded by alpha; and the **shade**, a multiply grade over the **world, not the actors** (D134): a multiply rectangle laid last in the `ground` layer (the ground, the apron, and the sky behind them), and the same color as a multiply tint on every piece of the world's scenery in `world`, Images and Sprites alike (high ground, decor and wreckage props, assets, cue props, the Peeker's rock but not Pebble's lift; `render/views/scenery.ts` marks them; D135). A salvaged wreckage tile drawn later goes under the grade, and its new scenery takes the tint at once (`LightingView.relight`); the smoke flow checks that every piece of scenery, Sprites included, is tinted at the dusk stop. Units, allies, towers, air, projectiles, effects, and the decals under them (shadows, rings, highlights) are never graded, so they keep their faction palettes at dusk. The ground is baked in the map's own env, so the grade is relative to it: each env's grade is its `light` color over daylight (white for `act1` and `act2.gold`; `act2.dusk`'s lilac, `act3`'s pale violet, `finale`'s peach), divided by the base env's, capped at 1, then taken 40% of the way from white (`GRADE_STRENGTH`), so gold to dusk is a light lilac dimming and the map's own env is unchanged. The strength is what keeps the value bands (§3) at every stop: a test (`render/vfx/lightingBands.test.ts`) grades the ground at each stop of every scheduled map and holds the grass under the road (≥ 1.3:1), every ungraded unit, ally, and tower body ≥ 1.2:1 over the graded road, the Aelari's knots and rims, and the Hollow's dark body ≥ 1.6:1 under the road with its pale outline over it (the full dusk light sank the Hollow into the road). The pure math is `render/vfx/lighting.ts`; `LightingView` draws it. A map without a schedule draws no sky and no grade.

**Darkness and lighting overlay (A2M6's Sundown and any map with darkness; `views/DarknessView.ts`, `vfx/darknessPixels.ts`).** The finale's night is render's own, darkening the world only ([§3 *Finale*](#finale-dawn-through-the-dark-finale), `SongNightView`). It extends the tint path. The night falls **deep on the world and lightly on the actors** (principle 1, D134): its tone follows the sim's `state.dark` exactly (the sim eases it over 4 s), and at full dark the world (the ground, the sky behind it, and the scenery) keeps only a few percent of its light, 92% of the way to `act2.dusk.shade`'s deep plum, while the actors (units, allies, towers, projectiles, effects) take only a 22% veil toward it, and a faint lift of the same plum (60% of it, added) over everything out of the light keeps the blacks a deep night violet rather than a dead brown. One mask the size of the view, opaque except for the **light sources' discs**, carries it three times: a multiply over the ground (kept over the grade), the actors' veil (multiply) in the `lighting` layer, and the lift (additive) over that; a scenery piece out of the light takes the rest of the world's dark as its tint, judged at the tile it stands on with the sim's own test (`isLitAt`). The mask redraws only when a source turns on or off; an easing dark only retints. **Every hole is exactly the sim's lit disc** (a circle in grid space round the lamp or the beaming Prism, tested per pixel against the sim's squared radius, hard-edged), so what the player sees lit is exactly what the sim keeps at full range (D137; [architecture.md § Sim public API](../tech/architecture.md#sim-public-api)); towers inside it keep full range, and the HUD's range ring dims for a tower in the dark. **A Prism at rest shows a ghost of its light** (`views/LightGhostView.ts`): on a map with darkness, a live Prism whose beam is off draws the rim of exactly the disc it will light as a **dotted gold** ring, 1 px dots (one rim pixel in three, walked round by angle) alternating `gold.4` (it carries the ring on A2M6's daylight sand) and `gold.3` (it keeps it gold in the dark), each over a 1 px `ink.0` under-stroke, at 50% in `decal`, and arming a Prism draws the disc it would light from the hovered tile the same way at 85% (`ghostDiscImage` in `vfx/darknessPixels.ts`). The dotted gold is the light's own rhythm, apart from the pale dashes of the range rings and the link tethers (D139). A ghost is a rim only, never a hole or a pool, so it promises light without reading as lit (D138); a beaming Prism's real disc replaces its ghost. Inside a **lamp's** disc the ground and the scenery take **lamplight**, a multiply toward `orange.4` as the dark falls (a second mask, opaque only in the lamps' discs), so a lamp-lit stretch reads as warm lamplight at night, not a patch of daylight; a beaming **Prism's** disc stays clear (the Aelari's own cold light). Actors are never toned. Bars, tells and outlines draw above it all in `overlay`, so darkness never hides gameplay information.

**The world's glow (T530b; `views/WorldGlowView.ts`, `vfx/glowPixels.ts`).** Props paint their emissive pixels but bake no `@glow` (the dome, the core, flora and a few others do); render adds the light the world gives off, additively, in stepped alpha:
- **Glowing props** (`PROP_GLOWS`): a prop's emissive pixels, found by their master colors in its frame (every frame of an animated prop, so the churning slurry's glow covers wherever it turns), re-lit at full strength and ringed by a GLOW_STEPS halo, drawn just in front of the prop in `world` so an actor in front still hides it. Each shines a little at dusk and fully in the dark: A2M6's **wall lamps** (the lanterns, `gold.3`/`gold.4`/`white.1`, a 4 px `gold.4` halo, 35% at dusk), **Dome 3's window** (its warm glass, the lettuce and Pebble asleep in it, a 5 px `orange.4` halo, 25% at dusk), A2M4's **vats** (the slurry, `violet.3`/`violet.4`, a 3 px `violet.3` halo, 25%: more would wash out its swirl) and **seams** (the veins, a 2 px halo in their own gold and pink, 30%).
- **In the dark only:** every other piece of scenery with a baked `@glow` lights up with the dark (at 60%; the core's heart at full, so what you defend reads at night), and the buildable pads' teal brackets glow faintly (70%) in `decal`, under the towers on them, so where a tower can go still reads at night.
- **Lamp pools:** in the dark, light pools inside each lit disc in `decal` (lamps `orange.4`, a beaming Prism's `blue.4` at half strength): stepped bands by grid distance, 35% to a third of the radius, 15% to two thirds, a 15% dither to 85%, so the light falls off toward the edge while **the edge itself stays the sim's hard disc**; glow never reaches past it, so it never reads as lit range.
- **The Prism's crystal:** while its beam lights the dark, a round 6 px `pink.4` halo (its ruby-pink gem's) on the crystal (14 px over its tile, plus high ground), in `vfx`; its pool below stays the cold `blue.4` light.
- **The moons** are added back over the night sky in the `lighting` layer (at 85% of the dark), so they stay the night's lights (§3).

### Particles

Particles are pooled 1×1 and 2×2 palette pixels (Phaser particle emitters with code-baked textures), with lifetimes of at most 0.8 s and a global cap of 512 alive.

### Range rings and placement

- **Range rings** are pre-rasterized 1 px dashed pixel ellipses (2:1), baked per radius in 0.25-tile steps, drawn in the `decal` layer: `white.0` when valid, `red.3` when the placement is invalid, with a 1 px `ink.0` under-stroke so they read on light ground. A placed tower's ring (hovered or selected) is the range it fires at, its links, tile and the dark stacked as the sim stacks them (`render/input/ringRange.ts`); a tower without an attack shows its detection radius. The placement preview's ring shrinks and dims out of every light only for a tower the dark shortens (the sim's `darkDims`: never the Lookout or the Lantern, whose reach is the same everywhere; `previewRing`, D139). A Hum Choir's valid preview is its song's `gold.3` (`songRing`), the ring it will sing once placed.
- **Onboarding:** until the first build, every free pad blinks the `teal.3` tile highlight at 1 Hz (steady under reduced motion). From W2's countdown until the first upgrade (while a tower stands), the same highlight blinks under every built tower instead (the upgrade teach, D028). Every 1 Hz pulse, on the canvas and in the HUD, runs on the page clock, so they all blink in phase.
- The **placement ghost** is the tower sprite at 60% alpha over the target tile, plus the tile mask highlight. Over high ground or a power node it also carries the bonus it would get, in the 3×5 font's capitals in `teal.4` with an `ink.0` outline, over its head ("+25% RANGE", "+20% RATE"; D059).
- The **keyboard tile cursor** (T126) is four `gold.3` corner brackets 1 px outside the tile's diamond (about 6 px of outline at each side vertex, 4 at the top and bottom), each with a 1 px `ink.0` outer stroke, in the `overlay` layer so a tower on the tile never hides them (`render/vfx/cursorPixels.ts`). Gold and the open edges keep it apart from the tile-mask highlights (hover `white.0`, selected `teal.3`, invalid `red.3`), from a pad's own plate brackets (which sit inside the tile), and from the clickable-object outline (dashed `gold.4` around an object, not a tile). It's steady: it marks where Enter acts, so it never blinks. While it shows, the hover highlight, ghost, ring, and reticle draw on its tile.

## 9. UI style

The in-map HUD, menus, and dialogue are a **DOM overlay** sized to the 640×360 game-pixel space and scaled with CSS `zoom` by the same integer factor as the canvas (rationale in [architecture.md](../tech/architecture.md#ui-dom-overlay)). In-world UI (range rings, health bars, tells, floating credits) is drawn in Phaser.

### Look: a sci-fi tactical HUD

- **Panels:** `ink.1` at 92% opacity with a 1 px border in the act accent color and **45° chamfered corners** (2–3 px cuts; CSS `clip-path`). Optional 1 px scanline texture at 6% opacity.
- **Layout: HUD in the corners.** An isometric map leaves the four screen corners empty ([tech-spec.md § Tile and screen](tech-spec.md#tile-and-screen)), so: top-left has core integrity (or Evacuated) and credits; top-right has the wave counter, wave preview, call-wave, and speed and pause; bottom-left has the two commander ability slots (with the companion's portrait), then Hold Fire (all) and, once it unlocks, Spare runners (all); bottom-right has the 4 loadout build slots (16×16 icons and costs; a newly issued tower's slot wears a small `teal.3` "NEW" tag with `ink.0` text and edge, QUILL's color, centered on the slot's top edge, for its first battle, D061). ### Fonts: the one binary-asset exception

DOM text uses two **SIL Open Font License** pixel fonts, self-hosted through `@fontsource` packages (bundled by Vite, no external requests), with their license files included in the build:

| Font | Use | Size (game px) |
|---|---|---|
| **Silkscreen** | Headings, labels, HUD numbers, buttons, and body text (menu copy, shelf rows, the results debrief, the loadout screen) | 8 (or 16) |
| **Pixelify Sans** | Not used: dialogue is Silkscreen too (T302b) | — |

Fonts are used only at sizes where their pixel grid lines up with the game-pixel grid. T106 measured this in Chrome under CSS `zoom`: Silkscreen at 8 and 16 is crisp (under 2% of its edge pixels antialiased), so the HUD uses only Silkscreen. **Body text is Silkscreen too (T219):** Pixelify Sans draws "5" like "S" and its "fi" and "fl" ligatures like "A" at every size from 8 to 16 ("radius 1.S", "-1S%", "Ales"), so no size fixes it. The file also references each package's `LICENSE` so the build ships them. ## 10. The star map

The campaign screen from Act II on, titled **Sector map**: Everglow Perimeter Ops' survey chart of the valley, drawn full-bleed at 640×360 game px with the screen's chrome in the corners, as the HUD's is. What it shows and when is [design/core-loop.md §1.1](../design/core-loop.md#11-the-star-map); its strings are [story/style-guide.md §2](../story/style-guide.md#2-voice-by-act); its build is [architecture.md § UI: DOM overlay](../tech/architecture.md#ui-dom-overlay).

### What is code art and what is DOM

| Part | How it's drawn |
|---|---|
| **The chart** (terrain, the colony, roads, the Long Walk, the north band, the legs between nodes) | One 640×360 image from a pure, seeded pixel generator (as the menu backdrop is), painted 1:1 into a `<canvas>` in the DOM. | **Node glyphs, medal pips, the marker** | Sprites `ui.star_node`, `ui.star_pips`, `ui.star_marker`, baked from palette-indexed grids into data URLs as portraits are, shown 1:1 inside the DOM node buttons |
| **Header, banks, footer, name plates, region labels, the node panel, the notice** | DOM (Silkscreen 8, the §9 panel and button styles) |
| **Scanlines and the first-visit sweep** | CSS over the chart |

### The chart

North is up. It is a top-down survey with hill shading lit from the north-west, the cartographer's convention: each relief step's south-east faces take the phase's `shade`, its north-west lips its `light`, and a 1 px contour line one ramp step darker rings each step. **Value:** the whole chart sits in the environment band (principle 1), a step below a battle's ground, so the glyphs, the marker, and the plates read over it in grayscale. The 1 px scanline texture at 6% covers it, so it reads as an ops screen.

## Open questions

1. **Dropping Pixelify Sans.** Nothing draws in it since dialogue moved to Silkscreen 8 (T302b), so its `@fontsource` package and the `--font-dialogue` token could go. A code-generated bitmap font rendered to canvas stays an option if long lines in Silkscreen's capitals prove tiring to read.
2. **Lighting overlay cost.** Redrawing a 640×360 light map each frame should be cheap, but it needs a measurement on low-end integrated GPUs before A2M6 work.
3. **Hand-authored or generated ground tiles.** This doc assumes mostly procedural ground tiles. If they look too uniform, add hand-gridded variants per environment palette.
4. **Woven Aelari at 16 px.** A glass-thread lattice with see-through gaps needs enough pixels to read. Small Aelari units may need a denser weave (fewer gaps) than towers and bosses; check at T104's sprite gallery.

Technical open questions (raised levels, finale room) are in [tech-spec.md](tech-spec.md#open-questions).
