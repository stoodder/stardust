# Art Direction

How Stardust looks, and the technical spec that makes the look reproducible in code. For *what* the factions, characters, and places are, see [story/races.md](../story/races.md), [story/characters.md](../story/characters.md), and [design/maps.md](../design/maps.md). This doc covers how they **look**.

Binding constraint: **D001 #1.** All art is generated by code from palette-indexed grids. The one exception to "no binary art assets" is listed in [§9 UI style](#9-ui-style).

---

## 1. The look in one paragraph

A sun-bleached sci-fi frontier seen from a 2:1 isometric camera, drawn in chunky, hand-placed pixels. Corporate Halcyon hardware is clean, white, and teal, like StarCraft's Terran depots repainted by a cheerful brand team. Starship Troopers supplies the propaganda gloss and the bug-hunt framing. Everything that matters to play **glows**: saturated emissives, white-hot projectile cores, and bright ability tells, all set against **deep, inky darks** that get deeper every act. Act I reads like a candy-colored brochure. Act III looks like the brochure on fire at night.

### Principles

1. **Value first, hue second.** Every scene has three value bands: environment (low to mid), actors (mid to high, with dark outlines), and emissives and projectiles (highest, with white cores). If a screenshot turned to grayscale is still readable, the art is correct.
2. **Darks are colored, never gray.** The darkest ink is violet-black (`#05030a`). Shadows shift hue toward violet or blue, and highlights shift toward yellow (hue-shifted ramps).
3. **Saturation is earned.** Large areas (ground, sky) stay softer. Full saturation is reserved for actors, emissives, and UI accents.
4. **Shape tells the truth.** Every gameplay-relevant state has a shape or motion cue as well as a color (see [§8](#8-readability-and-enemy-ability-tells)).
5. **The palette is a story dial.** The palette darkens gradually across maps, not with a switch at each act boundary. Actors keep their faction palettes; the world, the light, and the UI theme around them change.
6. **Warm gold means remembered; cold violet means forgotten.** This color language is canon ([story/bible.md](../story/bible.md#what-stardust-really-is)): sung stardust, the dead at rest, and anything laid to rest are warm pink-gold; unsung stardust, the vats, drill spoil, and the Hollow's scattered pieces are cold violet. Never swap them for decoration.
7. **No mixels.** Every on-screen pixel is the same size. Nothing is scaled by a non-integer, and nothing pixel-art is scaled at all inside the game view (portraits and icons are authored at their display size).

## 3. Environment palettes by act

Each map picks an **environment palette** (`env` field in its map data). The palette is a set of named ramps that the procedural tile generator and props use. Maps within an act step gradually through the palette variants listed here (for example, Act II maps move from `act2.gold` to `act2.dusk`), so the darkening is continuous.

The light is **diegetic**: one Tumble day lasts about 38 standard days, so the acts are the Long Afternoon, the Long Dusk, the Long Night, and the First Dawn ([story/bible.md §3](../story/bible.md#tumble-iv)). The sun really sets at the end of A2M6 and rises once, in the finale's last phase. Two small moons hang in the sky backdrop in every act (they are the only sky light in Act III).

Actors (towers, enemies, projectiles) **keep their faction palettes in every act**. Only the world, sky, lighting overlay, and UI theme change.

## 4. Faction visual language

Each faction's palette fills the same **slots**: primary ramp `p`, secondary ramp `s`, accent ramp `a`, emissives `e0`/`e1`, `eye`, `hi`, and `outline` (see [§6](#6-code-generated-pixel-art-format)). Swapping one palette for another re-skins any sprite. Lore lives in [story/races.md](../story/races.md).

| Faction | Shape language | `p` | `s` | `a` | `e0` / `e1` | `outline` |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Terran / Halcyon** (clean corporate) | Rounded rectangles, panel lines, chevrons, logo plates, symmetry | `steel` + `white` | `teal` | `orange` (safety) | `teal.3` / `teal.4` | `ink.0` |
| **Skitterkin** (warm, organic, cute) | Round bodies, big eyes, tufts, stubby legs, a slight bounce in every animation | `coral` | `gold` (butter bellies) | `green` (moss tufts) | `gold.3` / `gold.4` (bioluminescent spots) | `ink.0` |
| **Aelari** (crystalline, cool, luminous) | Facets, tall vertical silhouettes, floating parts, hard 1px edges, near-symmetry | `blue` | `teal` | `violet` | `teal.4` / `white.1` | `blue.0` (colored outline, which makes them look lit from inside) |
| **Vrell** (patchwork, brass, scavenged) | Asymmetry, bolted plates, mismatched parts, exposed pipes, one oversized limb | `brass` | `orange` 0–2 (rust) | `patina` | `orange.3` / `orange.4` (welding glow) | `ink.0` |
| **The Hollow** (wrong, drained, unmourned) | Wrong geometry: silhouettes of what its pieces were (mostly Skitterkin, some Aelari, a few unrecognizable) drawn wrong, shapes that don't match their animation, missing pixels, limbs that pop between frames, no eyes or too many | `hollow` | `hollow` | `acid` | `acid.2` / `acid.3` | **`hollow.4` (inverted)** |

### Faction rules

- **Inverted outlines belong to the Hollow alone.** Every other actor has a dark outline. Hollow sprites have a *pale* outline around dark interiors. That makes them readable as "wrong" whatever the viewer's color vision, because it's a value cue, not a hue cue.
- **Hollow glitch:** Hollow sprites drop 1–3 random pixels per frame (render-side cosmetic RNG), and every 2–4 s they show one frame of horizontal offset.
- **Skitterkin animations are cute by default:** a squash-and-stretch bounce on the walk cycle, blinks on idle. When Skitterkin sprites are reused as allied towers ([research §4](../research/emotional-storytelling.md)), use the **same sprite** with the `skitterkin.ally` palette variant (identical `p`/`s`, with `a` swapped to `teal`, a nod to the alliance) so the player recognizes the creatures they used to shoot.
- **Halcyon hardware wears the brand:** the logo plate (a 3×3 teal chevron) appears on every Halcyon tower base. Later acts can swap in vandalized or faded variants via palette (`halcyon.worn`: `white.0` → `grime.4`, `teal.3` → `teal.2`).
- **Jailbroken Halcyon towers** (after A3M4, [design/towers.md §9](../design/towers.md#9-rules-for-story-driven-tower-changes)) use `halcyon.jailbroken`: `halcyon.worn` plus the logo plate patched over with a scratched `grime.3` cross and one `brass` Vrell patch (a palette variant plus a 3×3 patch frame, so every Halcyon tower gets it for free).
- **Hollow pieces scatter violet, rest gold.** A destroyed Hollow unit unravels into `stardust.unsung` motes that drift away. In the finale, a unit laid to rest turns `stardust.sung` gold from the outline in and its motes **rise** (§7). This is the only place Hollow sprites ever show warm color.

## 5. Isometric spec

### Tile and screen

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Tile footprint | **32 × 16 px diamond** (2:1 dimetric, the classic "pixel isometric") |
| Elevation step | 8 px per level (cliff side faces drawn below the diamond) |
| Internal render resolution | **640 × 360** (16:9) |
| Scaling | **Integer only**: 2× = 1280×720, 3× = 1920×1080, 4× = 2560×1440, 6× = 3840×2160. Letterbox with `ink.0`. |
| Map size without panning | `cols + rows ≤ 36`. Every campaign map fits on one screen ([design/maps.md §1](../design/maps.md#1-grid-and-coordinates)). Only endless and challenge maps may be larger and pan. |

**Screen budget for the largest campaign map** (22×14, Act III and the finale): the diamond is `(cols + rows) × 16` by `(cols + rows) × 8` = **576 × 288 px**. Add up to 32 px of tower overhang above the top row and 8 px for high ground, and it fits in **576 × 328** of the 640 × 360 view, leaving 32 px side margins. An isometric diamond leaves the four screen corners empty, so the HUD lives in the corners (§9) and never covers tiles. The camera centers the map's bounding box and doesn't move on campaign maps.

**Why 640×360 and 32×16:** 640×360 scales to every common 16:9 display by an integer, so there are no mixels anywhere. A 32×16 tile gives towers about 32×40 px of canvas, which is enough room for readable silhouettes and upgrade tiers while still letting about 18×18 tiles fit on screen.

The scaling factor is computed in **device pixels** so that fractional `devicePixelRatio` values (1.25, 1.5) still map each game pixel to a whole number of physical pixels: `n = max(1, floor(min(innerWidth·dpr / 640, innerHeight·dpr / 360)))`, with the canvas CSS size set to `640·n / dpr` by `360·n / dpr`. The implementation is in `src/render/scale.ts` ([architecture.md](../tech/architecture.md#rendering)).

### The canonical tile mask

Ground tiles tile seamlessly at a step of (16, 8) with no overlap and no gaps when the diamond is exactly this shape (256 px, which equals the lattice cell area):

```
row r (0..7):   x from 15−2r to 16+2r   (widths 2, 6, 10, … 30)
row r (8..15):  mirror of row 15−r
```

The two widest rows (7 and 8) are 30 px wide. The tile's extreme left and right points (x = 0 and x = 31) are owned by neighboring tiles. The procedural tile generator and the buildable-tile overlay both use this mask.

### Grid ↔ screen math

The grid is a continuous coordinate space in **tile units**, addressed as `(col, row)` in design docs and map data. In code, `gx = col` and `gy = row`. Tile `(i, j)` covers `[i, i+1) × [j, j+1)`, and its center is `(i + 0.5, j + 0.5)`. The sim works only in grid units; the render layer converts.

```ts
const TW = 32, TH = 16;          // tile width/height in px
const HW = TW / 2, HH = TH / 2;  // 16, 8
const ZH = 8;                    // px per elevation level

// grid → screen (world pixels, before camera scroll)
sx = (gx - gy) * HW + originX
sy = (gx + gy) * HH + originY - z * ZH

// screen → grid (z = 0)
const u = (sx - originX) / HW, v = (sy - originY) / HH;
gx = (u + v) / 2
gy = (v - u) / 2
// picking a tile: (floor(gx), floor(gy)), then bounds-check
```

`originX/originY` is the screen position of grid point `(0, 0)`, the top vertex of tile `(0, 0)`. A tile image (32×16) is drawn with its top-left at `(sx(i, j) − 16, sy(i, j))`. **All final screen positions are rounded to integers** (`Math.round`) before they reach Phaser.

**Ranges are circles in grid space**, so they appear on screen as 2:1 ellipses. Range rings are pre-rasterized pixel ellipses (see [§7](#7-vfx)), not anti-aliased vector shapes.

### Sprite anchoring

Every sprite definition declares an **anchor**: the pixel that sits on the ground point (the foot point). The renderer sets the sprite's display origin to the anchor in whole pixels (`setDisplayOrigin(ax, ay)`), never as a fraction.

| Thing | Ground point | Typical canvas | Anchor convention |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ground tile | top vertex of tile | 32×16 (+8 per elevation level) | top-left at `(sx − 16, sy)` |
| Tower | tile center | up to 32×48 | bottom-center of the base's footprint diamond |
| Enemy (small / medium / large) | its grid position | 10–16 / 16–24 / 24–32 px | between the feet, 1 px above the bottom row |
| Boss | its grid position | up to 64×64 | between the feet |
| Flying enemy | ground position (shadow) | as above | sprite drawn 12 px above the anchor, with a shadow blob at the anchor |
| Allied unit (for example Skitterkin swarmlings sent out by a tower) | its grid position | 8–12 px | between the feet |
| Projectile | its grid position | 2–8 px | center |
| Prop (single tile) | tile center | up to 32×48 | as towers |

### Depth sorting

The world is drawn in **fixed layers** (Phaser `Layer`s), bottom to top:

| # | Layer | Contents | Sorting |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `backdrop` | Sky gradient, stars, far parallax | none |
| 2 | `ground` | Ground tiles, cliffs, water | static, by `(i + j)` then `i` |
| 3 | `decal` | Path markings, buildable-tile markers, range rings, shadows, scorch marks | insertion order |
| 4 | `world` | Towers, ground enemies, props, tall terrain | **`depth = footY + kindBias + (uid & 0xff) / 1e4`** |
| 5 | `air` | Flying enemies, lobbed projectiles at the top of their arc | same formula |
| 6 | `vfx` | Projectiles, impacts, particles, glow sprites (additive) | insertion order |
| 7 | `lighting` | Act III/finale darkness overlay with light punch-outs | none |
| 8 | `overlay` | Health bars, tells, floating credits, selection brackets | insertion order |

- `footY` is the anchor's screen y **on the ground plane** (no elevation offset). `kindBias`: prop 0.1, enemy 0.2, tower 0.3, so at equal y, towers draw in front.
- The `uid` term breaks ties deterministically so sprites never flicker.
- **Multi-tile props** sort by their front-most (largest `footY`) tile. If a large prop clips wrong, split it into per-tile slices.
- **Elevation:** the playfield has at most **one raised level**: **high ground** tiles (buildable, +range; [design/maps.md](../design/maps.md)). A high-ground tile is a block (diamond top plus 8 px cliff faces) drawn in the **`world` layer** with `kindBias` 0.05, so things in front of it draw over its cliff faces and things behind it are hidden. A tower on high ground draws its anchor `ZH` (8 px) higher but sorts by the tile's ground-plane `footY`. Decorative cliffs at the map edges stay in the `ground` layer. Playfields with more than one raised level are out of scope.

## 6. Code-generated pixel art format

Per D001, sprites are **palette-indexed character grids** in TypeScript data files. At boot they're rasterized into RGBA buffers, packed into a texture atlas, and registered with Phaser. Nothing about the format is Phaser-specific.

### Files

| Path | Contents |
|---|---|
| `src/content/palettes/master.ts` | The master palette (§2), as `name → hex` |
| `src/content/palettes/factions.ts`, `env.ts` | Faction slot palettes (§4) and environment ramps (§3) |
| `src/content/sprites/<category>/<id>.ts` | One sprite definition per file (towers, enemies, projectiles, props, icons, portraits, ui) |
| `src/content/sprites/index.ts` | Registry of all sprites |
| `src/render/sprites/rasterize.ts` | **Pure** grid → RGBA buffer, with outline, mirror, patch, and glow passes. No DOM, no Phaser, so it's unit-tested in Node. |
| `src/render/sprites/atlas.ts` | Packs buffers into atlas canvases (shelf packer, max 2048×2048) |
| `src/render/sprites/CodeSpriteSource.ts` | Registers atlas textures, frames, and animations with Phaser |

### The standard legend

Every sprite uses this legend unless it adds its own entries. Each character names a **slot**, and a palette maps slots to master colors, so one grid can be re-skinned by swapping the palette.

| Char | Slot | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `.` | — | transparent |
| `0`–`4` | `p0`–`p4` | primary ramp (0 darkest) |
| `5`–`9` | `s0`–`s4` | secondary ramp |
| `a`–`e` | `a0`–`a4` | accent ramp |
| `+` | `e0` | emissive glow (outer) |
| `*` | `e1` | emissive core |
| `x` | `outline` | hand-placed outline pixel |
| `k` | `eye` | pupils, the darkest detail |
| `w` | `hi` | specular glint |
| `_` | `shadow` | ground shadow (`ink.0` at 50% alpha) |
| `A`–`Z` | custom | per-sprite legend entries mapping directly to master colors (use sparingly) |

### Format rules

- **Grids are validated:** every row must have exactly `w` characters, there must be `h` rows, and every character must be in the legend. Errors name the sprite, frame, row, and column.
- **Facings:** actors that move have 4 diagonal facings (SE, SW, NE, NW). Author SE and NE; SW and NW are `mirrorOf`. Tower heads that aim may author up to 8 facings (4 authored + mirrors); the minimum is SE and NE.
- **No runtime rotation of pixel art.** Rotating a sprite blurs or jags it unpredictably. Exceptions: projectiles of 4 px or smaller, and particles.
- **Composition:** a tower is a `base` sprite plus a `head` sprite (the head is drawn at an offset declared in the base, `mount: { x, y }`). Tier visuals are separate frames or patches of the same sprite (`t1`, `t2`, `t3`, `spec_a`, `spec_b`).
- **Procedural helpers are allowed.** Sprite files are TypeScript, so a definition can be computed (`mirrorH`, `stamp`, `recolor`, and noise fills for tiles). Ground tiles are mostly generated from the tile mask, the environment ramps, and a seeded noise pattern, not hand-gridded one by one.
- **Animation budget:** idle 2 frames at 2–4 fps, walk 2–4 frames at 6–10 fps, fire 2–3 frames, death 3–5 frames. Walk playback rate scales with the enemy's current speed, so a slowed enemy visibly walks slower.

### What the baker produces

For every sprite × palette variant × frame:

1. **Resolve** patches and mirrors into full grids.
2. **Rasterize** into an RGBA buffer through the palette (pure function, tested).
3. **Outline pass** (`outline: 'auto'`): every transparent pixel that is 4-adjacent to an opaque one becomes the palette's `outline` color. (`'auto8'` uses 8-adjacency for chunkier outlines.)
4. **Derived variants**, baked automatically for every actor:
   - `selected`: outline replaced with `white.1` (hover and selection).
   - `jammed`: every color mapped by luminance onto the `steel` ramp (towers under a jam, D001 #4 and [design/enemies.md §4](../design/enemies.md#4-tower-disabling-jamming)).
   - `@glow`: if `glow` is set, emissive pixels dilated by `radius` with stepped alpha (60%, 35%, 15%; never smooth gradients), drawn additively.
5. **Pack** into atlas pages and register with Phaser.

**Hit flashes are not baked.** They use Phaser 4's tint fill mode at runtime (`setTint(0xffffff)` plus `setTintMode(Phaser.TintModes.FILL)` for 2 frames), which works the same for hand-made sheets.

### Naming contract (what makes the renderer swappable)

Render code never touches grids. It asks a `SpriteSource` for frames by **stable names**:

```
frame key:  <spriteId>#<variant>:<frame>     e.g. enemy.example_mite#default:walk_se_0
anim key:   <spriteId>#<variant>:<anim>      e.g. enemy.example_mite#skitterkin.ally:walk_se
glow frame: <spriteId>#<variant>:<frame>@glow
```

```ts
interface SpriteSource {
  load(scene: Phaser.Scene): Promise<void>;     // registers textures, frames, anims
  frame(spriteId: SpriteId, frame: string, variant?: string): { texture: string; frame: string };
  anim(spriteId: SpriteId, anim: string, variant?: string): string;
  meta(spriteId: SpriteId): { w: number; h: number; anchor: { x: number; y: number } };
}
```

`CodeSpriteSource` is the only implementation for now. A future `SheetSpriteSource` would load a PNG atlas plus JSON (for example an Aseprite export) whose frame names follow the same contract, with anchors coming from the same sprite registry. Swapping is then one line in `BootScene`, and sprites can be migrated one at a time because a composite source can try sheets first and fall back to code.

The baked RGBA buffers are also available to the DOM UI (portraits and icons) as canvases, so the HUD and Phaser share one art pipeline.

## 7. VFX

All VFX follow the pixel rules: integer positions, palette colors only, and **stepped** alpha (100/60/35/15%) or dithering instead of smooth gradients.

### Projectiles

Projectiles are the brightest things on screen. Every projectile has a **white or `*e1` core** and a faction-colored edge, so it reads against any act's ground.

Each **damage type** ([design/enemies.md §2](../design/enemies.md#2-combat-resolution)) has its own *shape* of delivery, so the player can tell types apart without relying on color:

| Damage type | Shape | Look |
|---|---|---|
| **Kinetic** | Straight, hard-edged | 2×1 or 3×1 tracer streaks, rivets, harpoons with a 1 px tether line; 1-frame muzzle flash; `steel`/`white` with a warm core |
| **Explosive** | Lobbed arc, then a burst | Shell drawn in the `air` layer at the top of its arc, with a ground shadow that shrinks toward the apex and a 2–3 px smoke-puff trail; round burst sized to the splash radius |
| **Energy** | Glowing, electric | 3×3 to 5×5 bolts with a `@glow` halo; beams (1–2 px line with a 1 px white core, flickering between 2 widths at 15 Hz); chain arcs (a jagged polyline regenerated every 2 frames with cosmetic RNG); `teal`/`blue` |
| **Psionic** | Ripples, no solid projectile | Concentric 1 px rings or a wavering line that bends space; target sprite jitters 1 px for 2 frames on hit; `violet`/`pink` |

Faction flavor goes on top: Aelari shots are faceted shards with a 1-frame sparkle, and Vrell shots are mismatched scrap.

### Impacts, hits, and deaths

- **Hit flash:** 2 frames of white tint fill on the target. Never longer: hits must not hide the sprite.
- **Impact burst:** a 3–5 frame sprite, sized by damage class (small 5×5, medium 9×9, large 15×15).
- **Death tone ramp (a story dial):** Skitterkin deaths in Act I are slapstick: a "poof" of fluff and confetti pixels. Over Act II the confetti thins and a small stardust fleck lingers. By Act III there is no confetti, just a short, quiet fade of a still body. The variant is chosen by the map's act; the rules are in [design/enemies.md §8](../design/enemies.md#8-death-and-feedback-by-act).
- **Tidybots (Acts I–II):** after a wave resolves, 1–3 Tidybots hover in from the map edge, vacuum up the remains with a cheerful 2-frame chime light, and leave. In A2M4 they're seen unloading at the plant's intake. They never appear in Act III, and bodies stay on the path for a moment before fading.
- **Laid to rest (finale):** the unit's pixels flip to `stardust.sung` gold from the outline inward over 4 frames, then 6–10 gold motes rise slowly (about 1 px per frame) and fade in stepped alpha. **Scattered:** the unit unravels into `stardust.unsung` violet motes that drift back along its route, then pull together where it re-forms.
- **License revoked:** each powered-down tower flashes a red-stamped "REVOKED" plate (`red.3` on `white.0`) for 1 s, then folds down into its base and vanishes. The same stamp marks revoked slots in the loadout and HUD.
- **Song aura:** a soft warm-gold ring (stepped alpha, 1 px dashed edge in `gold.4`) around the choir leader, pulsing gently in time with the hymn. It is also a light stamp in the darkness overlay.
- **Screen shake:** at most 2 px, integer offsets, only for bosses, core hits, and big explosions. It's off in reduced-motion mode.

### Glow and bloom

There are three tiers, cheapest first. The game must look right with only tier 1.

1. **Baked emissives (always on).** Emissive pixels use the brightest ramp steps plus white cores. Most of the "glow" is painted in.
2. **Glow sprites (default on).** `@glow` frames (stepped-alpha halos) drawn additively in the `vfx` layer, under projectiles and emissive actors.
3. **Camera bloom (setting: Off / Low / High; default Low on desktop).** A Phaser 4 bloom filter on the world camera (`Phaser.Actions.AddEffectBloom`). Because the camera renders at the 640×360 internal resolution, the bloom stays chunky and pixel-consistent. It's skipped automatically if frame time exceeds budget.

**Darkness and lighting overlay (from A2M6 through the finale).** A low-resolution light map (640×360 `DynamicTexture`, redrawn each frame) is filled with the act's `shade` color and punched out with stepped-alpha light stamps at emissive sources (projectiles, tower emissives, alarm lights, the core). It's applied with multiply blend in the `lighting` layer. Actors' outlines and tells always draw above it in `overlay`, so darkness never hides gameplay information.

### Particles

Particles are pooled 1×1 and 2×2 palette pixels (Phaser particle emitters with code-baked textures), with lifetimes of at most 0.8 s and a global cap of 512 alive.

### Range rings and placement

- **Range rings** are pre-rasterized 1 px dashed pixel ellipses (2:1), baked per radius in 0.25-tile steps, drawn in the `decal` layer: `white.0` when valid, `red.3` when the placement is invalid.
- The **placement ghost** is the tower sprite at 60% alpha over the target tile, plus the tile mask highlight.

## 8. Readability and enemy-ability tells

**Rule: color is never the only signal.** Every ability, status, and state has a **shape** (glyph or silhouette change) and usually **motion**, plus color. Test it by viewing a screenshot in grayscale and with deuteranopia, protanopia, and tritanopia simulation; it must still read.

### Tile readability

Tile types and their rules are defined in [design/maps.md](../design/maps.md). Each one gets a distinct **shape**, not just a color:

| Tile | Look |
|---|---|
| **Pad** (buildable) | A constructed plate with corner brackets. It should never look like plain ground. |
| **High ground** (buildable) | A raised block with 8 px cliff faces and a pad on top (§5). |
| **Power node** (buildable) | A pad with emissive conduit lines that pulse slowly (`e0`/`e1` of the act accent). |
| **Path** | A worn track with tread marks, with luminance contrast of at least 1.5:1 against pads in every environment palette (tested). |
| **Air lane** | A dotted line of 1 px dashes in the `decal` layer, drifting slowly in the direction of travel. |
| **Rift lane** (Hollow) | Cracked ground with `acid` flecks and inverted-outline edges, visibly "not a path". |
| **Blocked / decor** | Clutter (rocks, crates, flora, domes), so it reads as neither pad nor path. |

## 9. UI style

The in-map HUD, menus, and dialogue are a **DOM overlay** sized to the 640×360 game-pixel space and scaled with CSS `zoom` by the same integer factor as the canvas (rationale in [architecture.md](../tech/architecture.md#ui-dom-overlay)). In-world UI (range rings, health bars, tells, floating credits) is drawn in Phaser.

### Look: a sci-fi tactical HUD

- **Panels:** `ink.1` at 92% opacity with a 1 px border in the act accent color and **45° chamfered corners** (2–3 px cuts; CSS `clip-path`). Optional 1 px scanline texture at 6% opacity.
- **Layout: HUD in the corners.** An isometric map leaves the four screen corners empty (§5), so: top-left has core integrity (or Evacuated) and credits; top-right has the wave counter, wave preview, call-wave, and speed and pause; bottom-left has the two commander ability slots (with the companion's portrait); bottom-right has the 4 loadout build slots (16×16 icons and costs). A contextual upgrade/sell/targeting/hold-fire panel opens next to the selected tower, on the side away from the map center. The playfield stays unobstructed.
- **Numbers** use tabular figures; counters tick up and don't jump.
- **Hit targets:** at least 16×16 game px (32 CSS px at 2×). Nothing important lives only on hover, so touch can be added later (D001 #8).

### Act themes

The UI theme is a set of CSS variables switched by `data-act` on the UI root, so a theme change is one attribute. HUD copy changes per act are owned by [story/style-guide.md](../story/style-guide.md).

| Act | Accent | Secondary | Feel |
|---|---|---|---|
| I | `teal.3` | `pink.3` | Cheerful corporate: star ratings, rounded badges, brand chevrons |
| II | `orange.3` | `violet.3` | Same frames, quieter: fewer decorations |
| III | `red.3` | `violet.4` | Emergency: alarm-red borders, no stars, sparse |
| Finale | `gold.3` | `teal.4` | Clean, warm light |

### Fonts: the one binary-asset exception

DOM text uses two **SIL Open Font License** pixel fonts, self-hosted through `@fontsource` packages (bundled by Vite, no external requests), with their license files included in the build:

| Font | Use | Size (game px) |
|---|---|---|
| **Silkscreen** | Headings, labels, HUD numbers, buttons | 8 (or 16) |
| **Pixelify Sans** | Dialogue, codex, body text | 8–10, chosen at T106 for crispness |

Fonts are used only at sizes where their pixel grid lines up with the game-pixel grid (verified at T106). In-world Phaser text (floating "+12", tier pips) uses a **code-generated 3×5 bitmap font** defined as glyph grids, like any other sprite.

### Dialogue presentation

- **Scenes** (pause the game): a bottom panel with a 48×48 portrait framed in the speaker's faction accent, a name plate, a typewriter text reveal (about 40 chars/s, click to finish), and a 2-frame mouth flap while text types.
- **Barks and radio** (build phase, don't pause): a compact comms panel at the top-left with the portrait and 1–2 lines, auto-dismissed. The format and the rules for when each is used are in [story/style-guide.md](../story/style-guide.md).
- **Silence lines:** the speaker's frame shows its `dark` variant (Bricks after A3M3) with an empty text box for the line's duration. No typing sound.
- **Portrait-less speakers:** `pa` lines show a small speaker icon and a PA-styled name plate; `tfa` and form lines render on an off-white form panel (`white.0` on `ink.1` text, numbered clauses, a stamp box); `narrator` lines are centered captions with no panel; `hollow` lines have no panel and no name plate, drawn in `hollow.3` with distortion (letters dropped, glyphs offset by 1 px, re-rolled every 6 frames; plain grey in reduced-motion mode).
- **Memos** use a Halcyon letterhead panel (`white.0` with `teal.3` chevrons and the Hally sun), with small print collapsed under a "Small print" toggle. In Act III the letterhead loses its color (`halcyon.worn`) and, after A3M2, the sun.
- **Forced single input** (SEAL, "Who's there?"): the HUD dims to 40% and goes inert; one large chamfered button (Silkscreen 16, at least 96×24 game px) sits low and centered in the act accent color, pulsing slowly (well under 3 Hz). No other prompt, timer, or hint is drawn.
- **Name tags** (Bricks's turret names): a 1 px-bordered `ink.1` plate above the tower in the `overlay` layer, with the name in the 3×5 bitmap font (16 characters fit in 64 px).
- **Callsign plate:** the HUD's top-left shows "JPT-1 · {callsign}" in Silkscreen 8 above the meter.

## Open questions

1. **More than one raised level.** §5 supports a single high-ground level. If design/maps.md wants stacked levels or ramps that enemies walk up, depth sorting needs a z-aware rule.
2. **Font crispness.** Silkscreen and Pixelify Sans need to be verified at 8–10 game px under CSS `zoom`. The fallback is a code-generated bitmap font rendered to canvas for all UI text.
3. **Finale room.** If the finale outgrows 22×14 ([design/maps.md, open question 1](../design/maps.md#open-questions)), choose between panning for that map alone and a 24×12-style aspect that keeps `cols + rows ≤ 36`.
4. **Lighting overlay cost.** Redrawing a 640×360 light map each frame should be cheap, but it needs a measurement on low-end integrated GPUs before A2M6 work.
5. **Hand-authored or generated ground tiles.** This doc assumes mostly procedural ground tiles. If they look too uniform, add hand-gridded variants per environment palette.
6. **Dawn silhouette.** The visor going up at the First Dawn is a full-screen scene illustration (larger than a portrait). Is one hand-gridded 640×360 scene within the code-art budget, or should it be a composed scene from props and a large silhouette sprite?
7. **Violet overlap.** Unsung stardust (`violet`) shares a hue with the Aelari accent and Act III crystal. Does the Hollow's scattered-mote violet need a slightly different ramp step so players don't read Aelari shards as Hollow?
