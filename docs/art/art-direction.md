# Art Direction

How Stardust looks: the palette, faction visual language, portraits, VFX, readability, and UI style. The technical spec that makes the look reproducible in code (isometric math, anchoring, depth sorting, the sprite format, the baker) is in [tech-spec.md](tech-spec.md). For *what* the factions, characters, and places are, see [story/races.md](../story/races.md), [story/characters.md](../story/characters.md), and [design/maps.md](../design/maps.md). This doc covers how they **look**.

Binding constraint: **D001 #1.** All art is generated by code from palette-indexed grids. The one exception to "no binary art assets" is listed in [§9 UI style](#9-ui-style).

---

## 1. The look in one paragraph

A sun-bleached sci-fi frontier seen from a 2:1 isometric camera, drawn in chunky, hand-placed pixels. Corporate Everglow hardware is clean, white, and teal, like frontier supply depots repainted by a cheerful brand team. Starship Troopers supplies the propaganda gloss and the bug-hunt framing. Everything that matters to play **glows**: saturated emissives, white-hot projectile cores, and bright ability tells, all set against **deep, inky darks** that get deeper every act. Act I reads like a candy-colored brochure. Act III looks like the brochure on fire at night.

### Principles

1. **Value first, hue second.** Every scene has three value bands: environment (low to mid), actors (mid to high, with dark outlines), and emissives and projectiles (highest, with white cores). If a screenshot turned to grayscale is still readable, the art is correct.
2. **Darks are colored, never gray.** The darkest ink, `ink.0`, is violet-black. Shadows shift hue toward violet or blue, and highlights shift toward yellow (hue-shifted ramps).
3. **Saturation is earned.** Large areas (ground, sky) stay softer. Full saturation is reserved for actors, emissives, and UI accents.
4. **Shape tells the truth.** Every gameplay-relevant state has a shape or motion cue as well as a color (see [§8](#8-readability-and-enemy-ability-tells)).
5. **The palette is a story dial.** The palette darkens gradually across maps, not with a switch at each act boundary. Actors keep their faction palettes; the world, the light, and the UI theme around them change.
6. **Warm gold means remembered; cold violet means forgotten.** This color language is canon ([story/bible.md](../story/bible.md#what-stardust-really-is)): sung stardust, the dead at rest, and anything laid to rest are warm pink-gold; unsung stardust, the vats, drill spoil, and the Hollow's scattered pieces are cold violet. Never swap them for decoration.
7. **No mixels.** Every on-screen pixel is the same size. Nothing is scaled by a non-integer, and nothing pixel-art is scaled at all inside the game view (portraits and icons are authored at their display size).

## 2. Master palette

**Every color in the game comes from the master palette.** Its hex values live in one place, [`src/content/palettes/master.ts`](../../src/content/palettes/master.ts): change colors there, never here. Sprite palettes, environment ramps, and UI themes reference colors by name (for example `teal.3`), never by raw hex. A content test enforces this ([conventions.md](../tech/conventions.md#testing)). This section names the ramps and what each is for.

Ramps run from `0` (darkest) to `4` (lightest) and are hue-shifted: shadows lean violet or blue, and highlights lean warm.

### Neutrals

| Ramp | Steps | Use |
|---|---|---|
| `ink` | 0–4 | Outlines, the deepest shadows, UI panels, letterbox. `ink.0` is violet-black, never gray |
| `steel` | 0–4 | Metal, Everglow hardware, UI chrome |
| `white` | 0–1 | Highlights, projectile cores, Everglow paint (`white.1` is pure white) |

### Hues

Nine saturated five-step ramps: `red`, `coral`, `orange`, `gold`, `green`, `teal`, `blue`, `violet`, `pink`. Their faction and UI roles are in [§4](#4-faction-visual-language) and [§9](#9-ui-style).

### Materials and skin

| Ramp | Use |
|---|---|
| `skin.a`, `skin.b`, `skin.c` | Human skin: light to medium, medium to deep, deep |
| `brass` | Vrell hardware |
| `patina` | Vrell oxidized metal |
| `grime` | Dirt, scavenged plating, mining scars |
| `hollow` | The Hollow's drained body color |
| `acid` | **Reserved for the Hollow** (see the hue reservation below) |

**Hue reservation.** Saturated yellow-green (hue 60–85°, HSV saturation above 60%, value above 50%) belongs **only** to the Hollow. No other faction, environment, or UI element may use it. When players see that color, they should feel that something is wrong before they can say why. A palette test enforces the reservation.

### Stardust shimmer

The mineral that gives the game its name is drawn with **palette cycles** (3 colors rotating every 150 ms on its emissive pixels). There are two kinds, plus the currency icon:

| Material | Cycle | Where |
|---|---|---|
| `stardust.sung` | `pink.3` → `gold.3` → `gold.4` | Natural seams, the Deep Halls, Bricks's Tumble IV stone, the Returned's sacks once warmed, anything laid to rest. Never changes by act |
| `stardust.unsung` | `violet.3` → `violet.4` → `white.1` | Second Harvest's vats and output, drill spoil, the Ascendant's cargo, Sterling's snow globe, the Hollow's scattered motes. Never changes by act |

**The Stardust currency icon** follows where the Stardust comes from ([design/progression.md §1](../design/progression.md#1-currencies)). In Acts I–II it is Everglow's processed product, sold as candy, and its cycle cools as the truth comes out. From A3M1 the warrens *give* it as sung stones, so the currency becomes trust and the icon changes shape as well as color:

| Phase | Icon shape | Cycle | Feel |
|---|---|---|---|
| Act I | Candy-cut gem | `pink.3` → `gold.3` → `gold.4` | Sparkly pink-gold candy (the brochure) |
| Act II | Candy-cut gem | `pink.3` → `orange.3` → `violet.4` | Warm, with a cold edge creeping in |
| Act III | Gift stone ([§6](#6-story-and-world-sprites)) | `stardust.sung` | Warm gold, given rather than harvested |
| Finale | Gift stone | `gold.3` → `pink.3` → `gold.4` | Warm gold: remembered, not undone |

## 3. Environment palettes by act

Each map picks an **environment palette** (`env` field in its map data). The palette is a set of named ramps that the procedural tile generator and props use. Maps within an act step gradually through the palette variants listed here (for example, Act II maps move from `act2.gold` to `act2.dusk`), so the darkening is continuous.

The light is **diegetic**: one Tumble day lasts about 38 standard days, so the acts are the Long Afternoon, the Long Dusk, the Long Night, and the First Dawn ([story/bible.md §3](../story/bible.md#tumble-iv)). The sun really sets at the end of A2M6 and rises once, in the finale's last phase. Two small moons hang in the sky backdrop in every act (they are the only sky light in Act III).

Actors (towers, enemies, projectiles) **keep their faction palettes in every act**. Only the world, sky, lighting overlay, and UI theme change.

### Act I: the Long Afternoon (`act1`)

Deep, saturated greens, pink clay, a dusty-rose sand track, and a cyan sky. Shadows are lavender, not black, but still clearly dark. The ground sits a value band **below** the actors (principle 1): body luminance runs grass ≈ 0.20 < path ≈ 0.31 < every enemy, ally, and tower (≈ 0.37–0.42), so a Skitterkin on the road reads in grayscale by its value, not only its outline. A palette test holds the order (grass < path, path/grass ≥ 1.3:1, each actor ≥ 1.2:1 over the path). Grass tiles carry two soft mid-dark clumps each (a solid `p1` heart with a dithered rim), and grass beside the road carries a **verge** (`tile.verge`, an overlay per shared edge): clumps of `p1` 1–3 px deep with a dithered rim and a few `p0` roots at the road's lip. Together they give the playfield mid-darks between the lit ground and the deep apron, and the road a darker edge.

Ramps (`act1.*`): `grass` (deep green), `clay` (pink: cliffs, soil), `path` (the dusty-rose sand track), `water` (4 steps), `sky` (the cyan backdrop gradient, 3 steps), and `shade` (lavender) / `light` (warm cream). Flora accents: `pink.3`, `pink.4`, `gold.4`. Everglow billboards and domes: `white.0` and `teal.3`.

### The apron (every act)

No map floats in the void. `GroundView` draws a 4-tile **apron** of non-playable ground around the grid (`tile.apron`): ring 1 is the env's ground one ramp step darker, and rings 2–4 dither it to `ink.0` with a 4×4 ordered pattern that thickens ring by ring (4, 9, then 14 of 16 cells), so the map's edge sinks into the deep dark instead of stopping at a hard diamond. Ring 1 carries the colony's perimeter **fence** (`prop.fence`, Everglow steel posts and wire), with gaps beside border spawns and where the road leaves. The **road runs on** out of every border core tile, across all four rings (the road out through the gate). Ring 2 carries sparse brush and rocks plus the act's dressing, always on the north edges so it never hides the playfield or the HUD's top-center banner. Act I's dressing is the colony: an Evie billboard and lettuce patches on the north-west, Dome Row domes on the north-east. The apron is outside the grid, so picking never reaches it. Placement rules: `apronCells` in `src/render/views/groundTiles.ts`.

### Act II: the Long Dusk (`act2.gold`, `act2.dusk`)

Sun-baked gold grass, rust soil, ember highlights. By the end of the act the sky is violet and the shadows are deep plum. Grey **mining scars** (`grime` ramp) spread across revisited maps. **A2M6 (Sundown)** is the one map where the light changes during play: it starts on `act2.dusk`, and the lighting overlay ([§7](#glow-and-bloom)) fades in across its waves until the last wave is fought in full Long Night darkness.

Ramps: `act2.grass` (dry gold), `act2.clay` (rust), and `act2.path` are shared by both palettes. Each has its own `sky` (3 steps) and `shade` / `light`: `act2.gold` has a sunset-orange sky, plum shade, and peach light; `act2.dusk` has a violet-to-rose sky, near-black plum shade, and lilac light. Accents: `violet.3`, `orange.3`, and embers `orange.4`.

### Act III: the Long Night (`act3`)

Near-black ground, cold-violet crystal growths (unsung stardust spreading from the mines), and alarm-red Everglow emergency lighting. Darkness is now the default, and light comes only from emissives and the two moons (see the lighting overlay in [§7](#7-vfx)).

Ramps (`act3.*`): `ground` (near-black to deep violet), `crystal` (cold violet up to white), `path` (4 steps; its top step repeats in the tile palette), `alarm` (`red.1`, two alarm reds of its own, `red.4`), `sky` (black, 2 steps), and `shade` (black) / `light` (pale violet) / `emergency` (alarm red).

### Finale: dawn through the dark (`finale`)

The finale follows its three phases ([design/maps.md §5.5](../design/maps.md#55-the-leaving-song-finale-rules)):
- **Phases 1–2 (the Descent, the Unsung):** still night, on the Act III ground, with one new warm source: the **choir's song aura is a warm-gold light** punched into the darkness overlay, moving with the choir. The Halls' natural seams glow `stardust.sung`; the Deep Bore glows `stardust.unsung`. The light does not brighten per wave.
- **Phase 3 (Dawn):** when the countdown reaches 0, the first sunlight in nineteen days rakes in from the east edge over about 8 s, lifting the darkness overlay and switching to the dawn ramps below. Highlights go gold and cyan. Sunrise is the reward, and it comes from the song, not from clearing waves.

Ramps (`finale.*`): `ground` (dark violet warming to peach), `sky` (the dawn gradient, 5 steps: night violet through rose and orange to gold), and `shade` / `light`; it borrows Act III's path and crystal ramps. The `hope` accents are `gold.3`, `teal.4`, and `white.1`.

Every environment ramp is an entry in the master palette, namespaced by act (`act1.grass.2`), in [`master.ts`](../../src/content/palettes/master.ts).

## 4. Faction visual language

Each faction's palette fills the same **slots**: primary ramp `p`, secondary ramp `s`, accent ramp `a`, emissives `e0`/`e1`, `eye`, `hi`, and `outline` (see [tech-spec.md § The standard legend](tech-spec.md#the-standard-legend)). Swapping one palette for another re-skins any sprite. Lore lives in [story/races.md](../story/races.md).

| Faction | Shape language | `p` | `s` | `a` | `e0` / `e1` | `outline` |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Everglow** (clean corporate: the human towers and Everglow Security) | Rounded rectangles, panel lines, chevrons, logo plates, symmetry | `steel` + `white` | `teal` | `orange` (safety) | `teal.3` / `teal.4` | `ink.0` |
| **Skitterkin** (warm, organic, cute) | Round bodies, big eyes, tufts, stubby legs, a slight bounce in every animation | `coral` | `gold` (butter bellies) | `green` (moss tufts) | `gold.3` / `gold.4` (bioluminescent spots) | `ink.0` |
| **Aelari** (chorded and woven, jewel-toned, humming) | **Woven, never plated:** bodies and constructs are open lattices of glass thread, like stained-glass weaving. Taut 1 px strands cross at bright knot-nodes, with see-through gaps between them; silhouettes are tall and near-symmetric | `blue` (sapphire) | `teal` (emerald) | `pink` (ruby) | `teal.4` / `white.1` | `blue.0` (colored outline, which makes them look lit from inside) |
| **Vrell** (patchwork, brass, scavenged) | Asymmetry, bolted plates, mismatched parts, exposed pipes, one oversized limb | `brass` | `orange` 0–2 (rust) | `patina` | `orange.3` / `orange.4` (welding glow) | `ink.0` |
| **The Hollow** (wrong, drained, unmourned) | Wrong geometry: silhouettes of what its pieces were (mostly Skitterkin, some Aelari, a few unrecognizable) drawn wrong, shapes that don't match their animation, missing pixels, limbs that pop between frames, no eyes or too many | `hollow` | `hollow` | `acid` | `acid.2` / `acid.3` | **`hollow.4` (inverted)** |

### Faction rules

- **Inverted outlines belong to the Hollow alone.** Every other actor has a dark outline. Hollow sprites have a *pale* outline around dark interiors. That makes them readable as "wrong" whatever the viewer's color vision, because it's a value cue, not a hue cue.
- **Hollow glitch:** Hollow sprites drop 1–3 random pixels per frame (render-side cosmetic RNG), and every 2–4 s they show one frame of horizontal offset.
- **Skitterkin animations are cute by default:** a squash-and-stretch bounce on the walk cycle, blinks on idle. When Skitterkin sprites are reused as allied towers ([research §4](../research/emotional-storytelling.md)), use the **same sprite** with the `skitterkin.ally` palette variant (identical `p`/`s`, with `a` swapped to `teal`, a nod to the alliance) so the player recognizes the creatures they used to shoot.
- **Aelari threads hum and fray.** Strands carry a slow 1 px vibration (alternate frames shift one strand pixel, 2–4 fps) so constructs read as sounding, not solid. When an Aelari unit is damaged, loose 1 px thread-ends fray out from its edges (a patch frame per damage band) instead of chipping or cracking. Interior gaps are real transparent pixels, so the ground shows through the lattice: no other faction has holes in its silhouette.
- **Everglow hardware wears the brand:** the logo plate (a 3×3 teal chevron) appears on every Everglow tower base. Later acts can swap in vandalized or faded variants via palette (`everglow.worn`: `white.0` → `grime.4`, `teal.3` → `teal.2`).
- **Jailbroken Everglow towers** (after A3M4, [design/towers.md §9](../design/towers.md#9-rules-for-story-driven-tower-changes)) use `everglow.jailbroken`: `everglow.worn` plus the logo plate patched over with a scratched `grime.3` cross and one `brass` Vrell patch (a palette variant plus a 3×3 patch frame, so every Everglow tower gets it for free).
- **Hollow pieces scatter violet, rest gold.** A destroyed Hollow unit unravels into `stardust.unsung` motes that drift away. In the finale, a unit laid to rest turns `stardust.sung` gold from the outline in and its motes **rise** ([§7](#impacts-hits-and-deaths)). This is the only place Hollow sprites ever show warm color.

## 5. Portraits and icons

Portraits and icons are authored at their display size and shown 1:1 in game pixels. The sprite format and how they're baked are in [tech-spec.md §2](tech-spec.md#2-code-generated-pixel-art-format).

### Sizes

| Asset | Size | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Dialogue portrait | **48×48** | Base frame plus expression patches (`neutral`, `happy`, `worried`, `angry`, `sad`, and 2 mouth frames for talking). Shown 1:1 in game pixels. |
| AI or comms speakers | 48×48 | A screen-frame portrait with a 2-frame scanline flicker (QUILL, Prim) |
| Commander ability icons | 16×16 | Authored 14×14 plus outline, like tower icons, on no plinth. `icon.ability.<id>` (Orbital Strike: the Everglow uplink satellite firing its teal beam into an orange reticle) |
| Tower and build icons | 16×16 | Readable silhouette, faction palette. Front view on a small faction plinth (Everglow: the steel slab with the teal and orange chevrons). Authored 14×14; the auto outline makes 16×16. `icon.tower.<id>` |
| Enemy icons (wave preview) | 16×16 | The enemy's own body builder, front-facing (Skitterkin: `enemies/skitter.ts`). Authored 14×14 plus outline. `icon.enemy.<id>` |
| Status and tell glyphs | 8×8 | See §8. `icon.tell.<id>` (`icon.tell.fast`, `icon.tell.armored`, `icon.tell.carrier`). Authored 6×6 plus outline |
| Currency icons | 8×8 | Credits (gold chip, `icon.credits`), Stardust (shimmer cycle), Intel (teal data shard) |
| HUD glyphs | 8×8 | The meter's core orb (`icon.core`) and the locked-slot padlock (`icon.lock`). Glyphs are authored 6×6 plus outline |

## 7. VFX

All VFX follow the pixel rules: integer positions, palette colors only, and **stepped** alpha (100/60/35/15%) or dithering instead of smooth gradients.

### Projectiles

Projectiles are the brightest things on screen. Every projectile has a **white or `*e1` core** and a faction-colored edge, so it reads against any act's ground.

Each **damage type** ([design/enemies.md §2](../design/enemies.md#2-combat-resolution)) has its own *shape* of delivery, so the player can tell types apart without relying on color:

| Damage type | Shape | Look |
|---|---|---|
| **Kinetic** | Straight, hard-edged | 2×1 or 3×1 tracer streaks, rivets, harpoons with a 1 px tether line; 1-frame muzzle flash; `steel`/`white` with a warm core |
| **Explosive** | Lobbed arc, then a burst | Shell drawn in the `air` layer at the top of its arc, with a ground shadow that shrinks toward the apex and a 2–3 px smoke-puff trail; round burst sized to the splash radius |
| **Energy** | Glowing, electric | 3×3 to 5×5 bolts with a `@glow` halo; beams (1–2 px line with a 1 px white core, flickering between 2 widths at 15 Hz); chain arcs (a jagged polyline regenerated every 2 frames with cosmetic RNG); `teal`/`blue` |
| **Psionic** | Ripples, no solid projectile | Concentric 1 px rings or a wavering line that bends space; target sprite jitters 1 px for 2 frames on hit; `violet`/`pink` |

Faction flavor goes on top: Aelari shots are thrown threads (a taut 1 px jewel-toned strand that snaps from tower to target, with a 1-frame knot sparkle at the hit), and Vrell shots are mismatched scrap.

### Impacts, hits, and deaths

- **Hit flash:** 2 frames of white tint fill on the target. Never longer: hits must not hide the sprite.
- **Impact burst:** a 3–5 frame sprite, sized by damage class: small 5×5, medium about 11×11 (`fx.bomblet_burst`), large about 19×19 (`fx.explosion`). Its first frame is the biggest and brightest (a white-hot star with spikes), so the hit lands on the frame it happens; then fireball, ring, smoke.
- **Death tone ramp (a story dial):** Skitterkin deaths in Act I are slapstick: a "poof" of fluff and confetti pixels. Over Act II the confetti thins and a small stardust fleck lingers. By Act III there is no confetti, just a short, quiet fade of a still body. The variant is chosen by the map's act; the rules are in [design/enemies.md §8](../design/enemies.md#8-death-and-feedback-by-act).
- **Tidybots (Acts I–II):** after a wave resolves, 1–3 Tidybots hover in from the map edge, vacuum up the remains with a cheerful 2-frame chime light, and leave. In A2M4 they're seen unloading at the plant's intake. They never appear in Act III, and bodies stay on the path for a moment before fading.
- **Laid to rest (finale):** the unit's pixels flip to `stardust.sung` gold from the outline inward over 4 frames, then 6–10 gold motes rise slowly (about 1 px per frame) and fade in stepped alpha. **Ishael's mote (Dawn):** as the light comes up, one gold mote rises beside Ves'thara's sprite on its own, larger (2×2), slower (about 1 px every 2 frames), and last to fade. Nothing labels it. **Scattered:** the unit unravels into `stardust.unsung` violet motes that drift back along its route, then pull together where it re-forms.
- **License revoked:** each powered-down tower flashes a red-stamped "REVOKED" plate (`red.3` on `white.0`) for 1 s, then folds down into its base and vanishes. The same stamp marks revoked slots in the loadout and HUD.
- **Song aura:** a soft warm-gold ring (stepped alpha, 1 px dashed edge in `gold.4`) around the choir leader, pulsing gently in time with the hymn. It is also a light stamp in the darkness overlay.
- **Screen shake:** at most 2 px, integer offsets, only for bosses, core hits, and big explosions (the Orbital Strike: 240 ms). It's off in reduced-motion mode (`prefers-reduced-motion`).

### Attack and ability effects

The numbers are `render/vfx/fxTiming.ts`; FxView draws them ([architecture.md § Rendering](../tech/architecture.md#rendering)).

- **Hitscan tracer (Rivet):** the `projectile.rivet_tracer` streak (warm tail, white head toward the target) flies from the muzzle to the target in about 90 ms. The hit itself landed the tick the tower fired; the streak is only the read.
- **Pulse ring (Sprinkler):** a solid 1 px `blue.4` ellipse grows from the tower to its pulse radius in quarter-tile steps over about 360 ms, fading down the stepped-alpha ladder, drawn in `decal` under the enemies it chills.
- **Shell burst (Thumper):** `fx.explosion` at the impact point, over a solid `orange.3` ring at the firing tier's splash radius for 200 ms, so the player sees what the shell covered. **Cluster Thumper**'s bomblets are `projectile.thumper_bomblet` (a 4 px orange ball with a white glint, tumbling) and each pops a small `fx.bomblet_burst`, so the cluster reads as one big burst ringed by little ones.
- **Orbital Strike:** the `fx.orbital_reticle` (its dashed ellipse sized to the 1.5-tile radius, dashes marching) sits on the target for the whole 1 s telegraph and holds while paused; at 60% alpha it also follows the pointer while the player aims. On impact a beam drops from the top of the screen (teal edges around a white core, 8 → 6 → 4 → 2 px over 200 ms, with a flat `teal.4` and white flash where it meets the ground), the ground flashes out to the strike's radius (a filled `teal.4` ellipse at 60% with a `white.1` edge, fading in steps over 240 ms), seven `fx.explosion` bursts roll outward (the center, then a ring of six at 0.9 tiles, 50 and 100 ms late) with two more just off the center for a big core, a dithered `ink.0` scorch (0.75 tiles) fades over 1.5 s, and the screen shakes.
- **Orbital Strike Mk II** (radius 2 tiles): the baked reticle fits only 1.5 tiles, so a wider strike's reticle is a marching dashed `teal.3` ring (3 on, 2 off, `ink.0` under-stroke) at its real radius.
- **Suppressing Fire:** while aimed, hovering a path tile previews the stretch it would cover (every ground route through the tile, 3 tiles each) as `orange.4` hazard stripes (3 px diagonal bands at 60% inside a solid 1 px tile edge); any other tile shows the red invalid highlight. Once cast, the covered tiles carry `orange.3` hazard stripes at 50% for the 5 s, drawn in `overlay` over the enemies they pin (D059), flickering 100% ↔ 60% every 125 ms (steady under reduced motion) and stepping down to 35% and 15% over the last half second, while heavy streaks (the `projectile.rivet_tracer` streak at 2× length and 3 px thick) rake random spots on the strip (one every 35 ms, up to 0.35 tiles off the center line), each landing with a quarter-tile `orange.4` spark ring. The streaks fly from Bricks's muzzle when he is on the map, and each one flashes his muzzle toward where it lands; otherwise they come in from up-screen. Everything holds still while paused.
- **Bricks at his post** (`ally.bricks`) breathes in `idle_<facing>` (2 fps) turned toward his target, and each shot plays `fire_<facing>` (the muzzle flash, then back to idle) with a tracer streak from his cannon's muzzle to the target, like a Rivet's.
- **Link tethers:** an active link ([design/towers.md §6.1](../design/towers.md#61-rules)) shows as `fx.link_tether`: a 1 px arc between the two towers, bowed 6 px up so it clears their heads, with a 1 px `ink.0` under-stroke and beads marching from one tower to the other (4 frames at 6 fps). Shape and color both name the link: **Crossfire** is a solid `orange.3` line with `orange.4` beads; **Sitting Ducks** is a dashed `blue.3` line (2 on, 1 off) with white beads. Drawn over the towers in `vfx`, its ends lifted 9 px to the heads so the arc never cuts through the bases (D059). It shimmers at 4 Hz for its first second, then pulses 100% ↔ 60% on the shared 1 Hz clock (steady under reduced motion).
- **A link forms (D059):** a ring in the link's color (`orange.3` / `blue.3`) grows to 0.75 tiles from both towers, and the link's name floats up between them in the 3×5 font's capitals for 1.6 s ("CROSSFIRE!" in `orange.4`, "SITTING DUCKS!" in `blue.4`), with a two-note chime.
- **Specialize (D059):** the upgrade's white fill and `teal.4` ring, plus a wider `gold.3` ring (1.25 tiles) and its own sound, so the tier-4 choice lands bigger than an upgrade.
- **The sack burst (D059):** `fx.sack_burst` plays where the Hauler fell, and its three young run *back up the route toward the warren* (about 2.6 tiles/s, staggered 90 ms, on lanes 4 px apart, each turned the way it runs) for 1.5 s, fading in steps over the last half.
- **The playfield pointer (D059, D061):** when the radio names a tower (Bricks's "That one's Denise."), a 9×9 `gold.3` arrow with an `ink.0` edge bobs 2 px (every quarter second; still under reduced motion) just over its turret head, in `overlay`, for as long as the line shows (3 s at least), and a `gold.3` ring pulses from its tile every second meanwhile.
- **Feedback juice (D026):** an enemy death pops `fx.explosion` (Act I's slapstick poof until the death-tone ramp lands). A new tower drops in from 4 px above (1 px per 2 frames) inside a `steel.4` dust ring; an upgrade fills the tower white for 2 frames inside a growing `teal.4` ring. Credits float as numbers in the 3×5 font with a 1 px `ink.0` outline, rising 10 px over 0.9 s: "−cost" in `gold.3` on a build or upgrade, "+refund" in `gold.4` on a sale.
- **A leak:** the core fills white for 2 frames, a `red.3` ring grows from it to 1 tile, the camera shakes 1 px for 120 ms (off under reduced motion), and "−N" floats up in `red.3`. At integrity 5 or less the core's heart turns red: its glow halo is drawn in `red.3` and the orb pulses `red.3` ↔ `red.4` at 1 Hz (a steady `red.4` under reduced motion). Both overlays are cut from the core's `@glow` frame (the orb is its 60% step, the halo its outer steps), so they fit the sprite exactly.
- **The core never hides a tower:** while a tower stands on a pad the core's sprite covers (the same screen column, two or more diagonals behind it, under the core's top), the core and its overlays draw at 60% (D028).
- **After the map ends** the battle freezes for 1.5 s before the results card (D028), so the last leak's ring, shake, and radio line play out.

### Glow and bloom

There are three tiers, cheapest first. The game must look right with only tier 1.

1. **Baked emissives (always on).** Emissive pixels use the brightest ramp steps plus white cores. Most of the "glow" is painted in.
2. **Glow sprites (default on).** `@glow` frames (stepped-alpha halos) drawn additively in the `vfx` layer, under projectiles and emissive actors.
3. **Camera bloom (setting: Off / Low / High; default Low on desktop).** A Phaser 4 bloom filter on the world camera (`Phaser.Actions.AddEffectBloom`). Because the camera renders at the 640×360 internal resolution, the bloom stays chunky and pixel-consistent. It's skipped automatically if frame time exceeds budget.

**Darkness and lighting overlay (from A2M6 through the finale).** A low-resolution light map (640×360 `DynamicTexture`, redrawn each frame) is filled with the act's `shade` color and punched out with stepped-alpha light stamps at emissive sources (projectiles, tower emissives, alarm lights, the core). On A2M6 the **Prism Spire's beam** is a large light source: its stamp covers its light radius, and towers inside it keep full range, so what the player sees lit is exactly what the sim treats as lit ([architecture.md § Sim public API](../tech/architecture.md#sim-public-api)). It's applied with multiply blend in the `lighting` layer. Actors' outlines and tells always draw above it in `overlay`, so darkness never hides gameplay information.

### Particles

Particles are pooled 1×1 and 2×2 palette pixels (Phaser particle emitters with code-baked textures), with lifetimes of at most 0.8 s and a global cap of 512 alive.

### Range rings and placement

- **Range rings** are pre-rasterized 1 px dashed pixel ellipses (2:1), baked per radius in 0.25-tile steps, drawn in the `decal` layer: `white.0` when valid, `red.3` when the placement is invalid, with a 1 px `ink.0` under-stroke so they read on light ground.
- **Onboarding:** until the first build, every free pad blinks the `teal.3` tile highlight at 1 Hz (steady under reduced motion). From W2's countdown until the first upgrade (while a tower stands), the same highlight blinks under every built tower instead (the upgrade teach, D028). Every 1 Hz pulse, on the canvas and in the HUD, runs on the page clock, so they all blink in phase.
- The **placement ghost** is the tower sprite at 60% alpha over the target tile, plus the tile mask highlight. Over high ground or a power node it also carries the bonus it would get, in the 3×5 font's capitals in `teal.4` with an `ink.0` outline, over its head ("+25% RANGE", "+20% RATE"; D059).
- The **keyboard tile cursor** (T126) is four `gold.3` corner brackets 1 px outside the tile's diamond (about 6 px of outline at each side vertex, 4 at the top and bottom), each with a 1 px `ink.0` outer stroke, in the `overlay` layer so a tower on the tile never hides them (`render/vfx/cursorPixels.ts`). Gold and the open edges keep it apart from the tile-mask highlights (hover `white.0`, selected `teal.3`, invalid `red.3`), from a pad's own plate brackets (which sit inside the tile), and from the clickable-object outline (dashed `gold.4` around an object, not a tile). It's steady: it marks where Enter acts, so it never blinks. While it shows, the hover highlight, ghost, ring, and reticle draw on its tile.

## 8. Readability and enemy-ability tells

**Rule: color is never the only signal.** Every ability, status, and state has a **shape** (glyph or silhouette change) and usually **motion**, plus color. Test it by viewing a screenshot in grayscale and with deuteranopia, protanopia, and tritanopia simulation; it must still read.

### Tile readability

Tile types and their rules are defined in [design/maps.md](../design/maps.md). Each one gets a distinct **shape**, not just a color:

| Tile | Look |
|---|---|
| **Pad** (buildable) | A constructed plate with corner brackets. It should never look like plain ground. |
| **High ground** (buildable) | A raised block with 8 px cliff faces and a pad on top ([tech-spec.md § Depth sorting](tech-spec.md#depth-sorting)). The cliffs are the act's clay (`a`), lit left and shaded right, with two strata bands and grass lipping over the edge. |
| **Power node** (buildable) | A pad with emissive conduit lines that pulse slowly (`e0`/`e1` of the act accent): four conduits run in from the tile's corners to a white-hot socket at the plate's heart, a bead flowing inward along each (4 frames at 4 fps). |
| **Path** | A worn track with tread marks, with luminance contrast of at least 1.5:1 against pads in every environment palette (tested). |
| **Air lane** | A dotted line of 1 px dashes in the `decal` layer, drifting slowly in the direction of travel. |
| **Rift lane** (Hollow) | Cracked ground with `acid` flecks and inverted-outline edges, visibly "not a path". |
| **Blocked / decor** | Clutter (rocks, crates, flora, domes), so it reads as neither pad nor path. A map's **decor markers** draw their story prop on their tile instead, on any map: `rig3` → `prop.rig`, `housing` → `prop.housing`, `scarecrow` → `prop.scarecrow` (`DECOR_MARKERS` in `src/render/views/groundTiles.ts`); other markers (a Challenge's power node, an ally post) draw nothing of their own. |

## 9. UI style

The in-map HUD, menus, and dialogue are a **DOM overlay** sized to the 640×360 game-pixel space and scaled with CSS `zoom` by the same integer factor as the canvas (rationale in [architecture.md](../tech/architecture.md#ui-dom-overlay)). In-world UI (range rings, health bars, tells, floating credits) is drawn in Phaser.

### Look: a sci-fi tactical HUD

- **Panels:** `ink.1` at 92% opacity with a 1 px border in the act accent color and **45° chamfered corners** (2–3 px cuts; CSS `clip-path`). Optional 1 px scanline texture at 6% opacity.
- **Layout: HUD in the corners.** An isometric map leaves the four screen corners empty ([tech-spec.md § Tile and screen](tech-spec.md#tile-and-screen)), so: top-left has core integrity (or Evacuated) and credits; top-right has the wave counter, wave preview, call-wave, and speed and pause; bottom-left has the two commander ability slots (with the companion's portrait), then Hold Fire (all) and, once it unlocks, Spare runners (all); bottom-right has the 4 loadout build slots (16×16 icons and costs; a newly issued tower's slot wears a small `teal.3` "NEW" tag with `ink.0` text and edge, QUILL's color, centered on the slot's top edge, for its first battle, D061). A contextual upgrade/sell/targeting/hold-fire panel opens next to the selected tower, on the side away from the map center unless that covers another tower: it then tries the other side, then above or below, so it never hides the selected tower and covers as few neighbours as it can (the exact rule: [architecture.md § UI: DOM overlay](../tech/architecture.md#ui-dom-overlay), *the panel rule*). The playfield stays unobstructed.
- **Numbers** use tabular figures; counters tick up and don't jump (credits close any gap in about 250 ms; reduced motion jumps). Core integrity turns `red.3` at 5 or below.
- **HUD details (T106):** panels cut 3 px, buttons 2 px. The 1 px edge is a separate ring drawn over the 92% fill (an even-odd `clip-path`), so the fill never shows the edge color through it. Buttons are `ink.2` with a `steel.1` edge; hover and the armed or pressed state take the act accent edge (pressed: over `--accent-dim`); keyboard focus turns the edge `white.1`. Hotkeys show as small `steel.3` key badges on their controls. Unaffordable costs are `red.3` with the icon at 45% opacity; a locked slot shows the padlock in place of its cost. Notices (a rejected command) sit top center in a panel edged in the act's secondary color for about 2 s.
- **Feedback (D026):** a leak flashes the status panel's edge `red.3` for 300 ms, and at integrity 5 or less a blinking `red.3` box with an `ink.0` "!" sits beside the meter's label (a shape, not only the red number). "Do this now" controls pulse their accent edge and fill at 1 Hz: build slot 1 until the first build, Start once a tower stands, and Call while an early call pays with nothing alive; a ready ability slot flashes its edge `white.1` three times as each countdown starts. A "WAVE n" banner in Silkscreen 16, accent-colored with a 1 px `ink.0` outline, shows top center for 1.2 s as a wave starts. While paused, a 15% `ink.0` scrim covers the playfield and a pinned "PAUSED" notice sits bottom center. **The upgrade teach (D028; once per save, D061):** from W2's countdown until the first upgrade, a hint panel (`teal.3` edge, the key badge U) sits bottom center above the PAUSED slot while no tower is selected, and the selected tower's Upgrade button pulses. Pulses and blinks stop under reduced motion.
- **Tower panel, medals, and intel (T202d):** the panel's bonus lines (where a stat's bonus comes from) are `teal.4`; the tier-4 choice is two `ink.2` cards (name in `white.1`, price in `gold.3`, description in `steel.3`), and a locked pair shows the padlock in place of the price with its unlock note in `steel.3`, never red. ### Fonts: the one binary-asset exception

DOM text uses two **SIL Open Font License** pixel fonts, self-hosted through `@fontsource` packages (bundled by Vite, no external requests), with their license files included in the build:

| Font | Use | Size (game px) |
|---|---|---|
| **Silkscreen** | Headings, labels, HUD numbers, buttons, and body text (menu copy, shelf rows, the results debrief, the loadout screen) | 8 (or 16) |
| **Pixelify Sans** | Reserved for dialogue (T302) | Not settled: see Open question 1 |

Fonts are used only at sizes where their pixel grid lines up with the game-pixel grid. T106 measured this in Chrome under CSS `zoom`: Silkscreen at 8 and 16 is crisp (under 2% of its edge pixels antialiased), so the HUD uses only Silkscreen. **Body text is Silkscreen too (T219):** Pixelify Sans draws "5" like "S" and its "fi" and "fl" ligatures like "A" at every size from 8 to 16 ("radius 1.S", "-1S%", "Ales"), so no size fixes it. Silkscreen is caps-only, which suits Everglow's labels; it draws "&" like "$", so the UI's text pipeline reads every "&" as "and" (`ui/silk.ts`, D059), and the R&D tab is "Research"; lowercase body text waits on the dialogue font. CSS tokens in `src/ui/theme.css`: `--font-heading` and `--font-body` (both Silkscreen) and `--font-dialogue` (Pixelify Sans, not used yet). The file also references each package's `LICENSE` so the build ships them. In-world Phaser text (floating "+12", tier pips) uses a **code-generated 3×5 bitmap font** defined as glyph grids, like any other sprite.

## Open questions

1. **Body font crispness.** Silkscreen is verified crisp (T106). Pixelify Sans is not: in Chrome its glyphs land on the pixel grid at no size from 8 to 40 px (at 8–10 game px under a 3× zoom, 45–55% of its edge pixels are antialiased; at 1×, 90%+), so dialogue in it reads slightly soft. Its "5" also reads as "S" and its "fi"/"fl" ligatures as "A" at every size, which moved menu body text to Silkscreen 8 (T219); dialogue needs ligatures off and a fix for the "5" if it keeps Pixelify. Options when dialogue lands (T302): accept the softness at 10 or 12, use Silkscreen 8 as the menus now do (caps only, less readable in long lines), or the code-generated bitmap font rendered to canvas.
2. **Lighting overlay cost.** Redrawing a 640×360 light map each frame should be cheap, but it needs a measurement on low-end integrated GPUs before A2M6 work.
3. **Hand-authored or generated ground tiles.** This doc assumes mostly procedural ground tiles. If they look too uniform, add hand-gridded variants per environment palette.
4. **Dawn silhouette.** The visor going up at the First Dawn is a full-screen scene illustration (larger than a portrait). Is one hand-gridded 640×360 scene within the code-art budget, or should it be a composed scene from props and a large silhouette sprite?
5. **Woven Aelari at 16 px.** A glass-thread lattice with see-through gaps needs enough pixels to read. Small Aelari units may need a denser weave (fewer gaps) than towers and bosses; check at T104's sprite gallery.

Technical open questions (raised levels, finale room) are in [tech-spec.md](tech-spec.md#open-questions).
