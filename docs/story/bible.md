## 1. Logline

A cheerful rookie pest-control technician on a corporate lettuce colony learns that the adorable "pests" are refugees, and that the glittering mineral everyone mines is their dead. To stop the hungry dark rising out of the mines, the technician has to stop keeping them out and start keeping the way open.

## 2. Premise

It is 2361. You are a brand-new **Junior Perimeter Technician** at **Halcyon Gardens**, a sunny agricultural colony on **Tumble IV** run by **Halcyon Dynamics**. Your job is to run the defense grid that keeps the local **Skitterkin** (round, squeaky, many-legged "pests") out of the hydroponic lettuce domes. Your mentor is a gruff sergeant who names his turrets after people who left him. Your grid AI tells dad jokes it is contractually obligated to tell. Your boss is the most positive person in the Kepler Fringe. Every cleared wave plays a jingle, and a counter tallies how many pests you have processed. You are in the top 5%.

None of it is what it looks like. The Skitterkin aren't invading; they are fleeing *through* the colony, away from something in the mines. **Stardust**, the mineral Halcyon extracts, is the crystallized remains of the Skitterkin dead, sung to sleep over thousands of years in the **Deep Halls** beneath the northern ridges. Ripping it up unsung has made the **Hollow**: the hungry, lonely absence of everyone who was never mourned. Halcyon knows all of this. It has a plan to strip the planet and leave. You helped.

The campaign moves the core tower-defense verb, one act at a time, from *keep them out* to *keep the way open*. In the finale you defend a choir of Skitterkin as they walk the path in the opposite direction, back toward the thing they fled, singing the colony's corporate jingle as a funeral hymn. Your kill counter counts down to zero as the dead are laid to rest.

| Act | Time on Tumble IV | The verb | What the player believes | What is true |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| I · Pest Control | The Long Afternoon | Keep them out | Cute pests are eating the lettuce | Refugees are walking home through a garden built across their road |
| II · First Contact | The Long Dusk | See them | Aliens and a dark thing threaten the colony | The company made the dark thing, from the dead, with your help |
| III · The Quiet | The Long Night | Get them out; hold the door; let them in | Everything is falling apart | It is; you choose who you are while it does |
| Finale · Clearing the Way | The First Dawn | Walk them home | — | The dead can't be undone, but they can be remembered |

## 3. World and setting

### Tumble IV

The fourth planet of **Kepler-2209**, a mild orange star at the far edge of charted space. The 2346 survey team nicknamed the planet "Tumble" for its slow, wobbling rotation: one Tumble day lasts about **38 standard days**. The campaign covers roughly seven weeks. It begins in the **Long Afternoon**, passes through the **Long Dusk** and the **Long Night** (about 19 days of darkness), and ends at the **First Dawn**. The act palettes in [../vision.md](../vision.md) are therefore diegetic: the sun really is setting on the story.

The surface is a temperate valley world: terraced green hills, pink-gold mineral glints in the rock, wide skies, and two small moons. The **northern ridges** (the "Spires") hold Halcyon's extraction rigs and, far beneath them, the Deep Halls. The **southern wilds** (the Brightwater basin) are open grassland and wetland where the Skitterkin live in the warm months. The colony sits in the valley between them, directly across the Skitterkin migration road.

### The Kepler Fringe and the year

The **Kepler Fringe** is the thin outer edge of human space, named after the antique star catalogue its worlds were first listed in. The joke on the frontier is that the Fringe is "where the Terran Frontier Authority's map goes blank and Halcyon's begins." The **Terran Frontier Authority (TFA)** is the government in name: broke, distant, and bureaucratic, it charters frontier worlds to corporations and rarely visits. The year is **2361** (Halcyon's fiscal year 2361; the game opens in Q2). See [races.md](races.md#humans) for Halcyon and the TFA.

### Halcyon Dynamics, in one paragraph

A consumer-energy megacorp whose slogan is "Brighter Every Day!" and whose culture runs on mandatory positivity, euphemism, and quarterly targets. Halcyon holds Tumble IV under a tax-favored **Agricultural Frontier Charter**. It really does grow lettuce (Halcyon Brightleaf), and it books "pest losses" against that lettuce to claim TFA crop insurance. The Skitterkin have never eaten a leaf; they can't digest cellulose. The real business is **stardust**, sold across human space as a premium power-cell material and, in its finest grades, as luxury glitter. Full detail: [races.md](races.md#halcyon-dynamics).

### What stardust really is

The Skitterkin carry a living crystalline lattice in their bodies (their **shimmer**, the pink-gold frost on their backs). When a Skitterkin dies, its family carries it to the Deep Halls and sings to it. Over years of singing the body resolves into stardust: a crystal that holds a faint, warm psionic echo of the mind it was. Skitterkin have done this for about forty thousand years. Every natural stardust seam on Tumble IV is a graveyard, and the deposits under the Spires are the oldest dead of a whole people.

- **Sung stardust** glows **warm pink-gold**. It is at rest.
- **Unsung stardust** glows **cold violet**. It forms when a shimmer crystallizes without being sung: torn out of the Halls by a drill, burned in a reactor, or force-crystallized in Second Harvest's vats. The echo inside it is not at rest. It is lonely, and it is hungry.

This color language is canon and should carry through art, VFX, and UI: **warm gold means remembered; cold violet means forgotten.** (Palettes themselves belong to `docs/art/`.)

Halcyon's natural seams near the surface ran thin in 2357. Deep bores into the Halls were dangerous (crews reported "the quiet"), so in 2358 Halcyon opened **Second Harvest**. The defense grid's cleanup drones (cheerful little **Tidybots**) collect the bodies of every "processed pest" and haul them to the plant, where heat and pressure crystallize them in hours. *Pests processed* is literal. The **Stardust** the player earns and spends at Zibzab's shop (see D001 #6) is, in the fiction, exactly this.

The game does not condemn every use of stardust. The Skitterkin view, which the story endorses, is: **"The dead don't mind being useful. They mind being forgotten."** The sin is taking without remembering. That lets the meta currency stay playable after the reveal, and it is the moral the ending lands on.

### The Hollow

The Hollow is what unsung dead become. When enough unsung stardust collects (in drill spoil, reactor exhaust, the vats), the lonely echoes inside it run together into a single hungry absence that wants to be filled with minds. Its touch **hushes** the living: it drains memory, leaving people and Skitterkin dazed and blank. Hushed people recover if the Hollow is laid to rest. Those it fully takes become part of it.

The Hollow on Tumble IV is **entirely Halcyon's creation**. It first stirred in 2359, when Rig Nine bored into one of the oldest chambers of the Halls. It is not a cosmic evil and it is not a replacement villain. It is the consequence: grief with no one to mourn it. Weapons can scatter its shapes, but the pieces drift back and re-form. Only the **Leaving Song**, sung by those who remember, lays it to rest. Ves'thara puts it this way: *"It is not hungry. It is lonely. When you are dead, those are the same thing."*

The Hollow *hums* the colony jingle, wrong: out of tune and half backwards. It is the last thing many of its pieces heard. (See §8.)

There is a precedent. About three thousand years ago the Aelari empire powered its golden age by burning its own sung dead, and its homeworld fell to the first Hollow. See [races.md](races.md#aelari).

## 4. Core themes

**Thesis:** *You can't undo what you did. You can remember it, and you can choose who you are next.*

1. **Remembering versus consuming.** Halcyon turns the dead into product; the Skitterkin turn the dead into song. Every system that consumes without remembering (the counter, the vats, the Tidybots, the employee numbers) is the antagonist. Every act of remembering (Bricks's correction, QUILL's log, the Leaving Song) is the resistance.
2. **Complicity and responsibility.** The player did it, with their own hands, and was rewarded with stars. The game never absolves them ("you didn't know") and never scolds them. Characters argue about it instead: Bricks says *"Not knowing is a choice. I kept making it."* Ves'thara says *"You were very good at it."* The question the game asks is not "are you guilty?" but "what now?"
3. **Keeping out versus letting through.** Tower defense is a genre about denial of passage. The story indicts that verb and then inverts it, act by act (§2), until the player is protecting passage.
4. **Names and numbers.** Sterling calls you by your employee number. Bricks calls you by a callsign. No one says your name until a man who is about to die says it. The pests have no names; then one has a name a sergeant gave her. The counter becomes a list of the remembered.
5. **Comedy equals tragedy plus time.** The same material (the jingle, the counter, the memos, the dad jokes) is funny first and devastating later, and in the finale it becomes sacred. Humor never leaves; it changes owners, from the company to people who love each other.

## 5. The secrets and the twist structure

The twists are layers of one question: **"What are we really doing here?"** Each layer is delivered by a different voice and in a different shape, so no two reveals feel alike (this is the research's trust-fatigue fix). The rule is **one reveal per map, never two in one scene.**

### The layers

| # | What is true | Delivered by | Shape | When the player learns it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The Skitterkin are refugees running *from* the north, not invading | The map itself | **Seen with your own eyes.** Dramatic irony from Act I, confirmed by a revisit | Suspected in Act I; confirmed A2M3 |
| 2 | Your kills are a harvest. Tidybots haul the bodies to Second Harvest; stardust (your currency) is made from them | Zibzab, the one who never lies | **Told plainly, when asked.** Players can ask as early as A1M3 | A2M4 (or A1M3 if they ask) |
| 3 | Stardust is the sung dead; ripping it up unsung made the Hollow | Ves'thara, the former enemy | **Argued with you mid-battle**, then taught as an ally | Hinted A2M2; explained A2M5–A2M6 |
| 4 | QUILL has hidden corporate directives | QUILL | **Confessed in stages before it is caught**, then resolved by the player | Tells from A1M1; partial confession A2M6; full A3M2; resolved after A3M4 |
| 5 | The wave-clear jingle is the Skitterkin's song of the dead. They heard it from the hills every time a wave of them died | A child (Pebble), humming | **Sung, not said** | A3M1 |
| 6 | Bricks wrote the survey line that made them "pests" | Bricks | **Confessed by the mentor** | A3M1 debrief |
| 7 | Sterling designed Second Harvest and the Buffer Protocol. The Preserve you escorted refugees to was the buffer. The Board will abandon the colony | Sterling, in person, cheerfully | **Enacted mechanically** (license revoked mid-wave). She doesn't turn; she reveals she never was on your side | A3M2 |
| 8 | The Hollow can't be killed, only mourned, and someone has to remember who it was | Ves'thara, Pebble, and QUILL's log | **Realized together**, then played | A3M6 into the Finale |

### Fair-play clues (what a second playthrough lights up)

- **Refugees:** every Act I spawn faces the Spires; every exit leads to open wilderness, never a colony building. Skitterkin never attack. Elders slow the swarm so the slow ones keep up. A leaked pest is logged as escaping "toward... the wilderness? Odd." Goldie's lettuce counts never go down. The Peeker watches from a rock.
- **Processing:** Tidybots vacuum up remains after every wave from map 1. The results screen says *processed*. The Brood Hauler's sack bursts into glitter, and tiny shapes run back the way they came. Second Harvest appears in Act I memos as a "sustainability success story."
- **Stardust = the dead:** Zibzab: "Everything precious was someone's once." Second Harvest's output is violet, while the natural seams are gold. The Skitterkin hum near stardust seams.
- **Sterling:** she uses your employee number and never your name. Every gift carries small print in the codex ("Budget increase contingent on Q3 extraction targets"). Her memos mention "buffer populations," "acceptable perimeter losses," and "Clean Exit readiness" in passing. Her portrait smile never changes; only her eyes do.
- **QUILL:** "(Per policy.)" precedes every directive-driven line, with a one-frame yellow flicker in its portrait. Its placement suggestions protect extraction rigs over housing. It says "I'm contractually obligated to say that" as a joke. It isn't one.
- **Bricks:** the codex cites "Survey 2346, Okonkwo" as the source of "non-linguistic chittering." He hums when nervous and denies it. He keeps a stone from every world.

### Finale: Clearing the Way (1 map in 3 phases, splittable into 2 · the First Dawn)

**Tone:** cathartic, then hopeful. **Verb:** walk them home. The Skitterkin choir walks the Long Walk **in reverse**, from the colony back north to the Deep Halls, toward the thing they fled. It is the right season at last: the Long Night is when the dead are sung.

1. **The Descent.** Escort the choir north while the Hollow comes at them from every side. The route passes over Okonkwo's Gap; the wreck of the turret named {name} stands on the ridge. C1, C2, and C3 decide who walks with you (§9). Halcyon's Deep Bore is drilling into the Halls' heart to fill the Ascendant, which adds a timer.
2. **The Unsung.** (The finale boss that design calls *The Unmaking*; its story name is **the Unsung**.) At the mouth of the Halls the choir sings "Halcyon Bright" as the Leaving Song. Hollow units broken inside the choir's song are **laid to rest**: they turn warm gold and rise. The **counter returns and counts down** from your lifetime Pests Processed. QUILL's state (C4) changes this phase. Under Keep, the knock-knock lands here. The Hollow converges on the Bore; the Ascendant aborts with a partial load. Sterling: *"Clean Exit complete! You were a top-five-percent technician. I'll put that in your reference!"*
3. **Dawn.** The counter reaches 0. The last of the Unsung joins the song instead of fighting it, and the first sunlight in nineteen days crosses the valley. The jingle plays in a major key, sung by everyone. Ves'thara remembers her mother's face. Pebble says your name. The protagonist's visor goes up for the only time, as a silhouette against the sunrise.

Then **C5** and the epilogue (§10).

### Where the arc needs more room

- **A2M5 is overloaded:** boss fight, Hollow surge, turn, and escort. If playtests run long, split it into A2M5a (*The Glass Witch Returns*, the boss) and A2M5b (*The Long Walk*, the escort).
- **Act III between the Gap and the finale** (A3M4–A3M6) carries grief, C4, Kip, the plant, the shelter, and the naming. A seventh Act III map, a quiet character piece ("Night Shift": Pebble, Ves'thara, and QUILL in the shelters, light combat), would let grief breathe before the push.
- **Pebble and Bricks** have A2M3 through A3M3 (six maps) together. Their bond must be dense: at least one short exchange per map, plus one non-map vignette (Bricks teaching Pebble to whistle; she can't; she hums).
- **The finale** can split into two maps (Descent; Unsung and Dawn) if the phases exceed about 15 minutes. That gives 19 maps in total.
- **Sterling** needs face time in Act II so the betrayal costs something. Give her at least one between-wave line per Act II map and one moment of fear (see [characters.md](characters.md#director-joy-sterling)).

## 8. Recurring motifs

- **The counter.** "PESTS PROCESSED: {n} ★ Top 5% of Technicians!" on every Act I results screen. It is a real lifetime stat (it counts replays too; the game remembers everything you did). In Act II it reads "Pests processed: {n}." with no star and no exclamation point. In A2M2 Ves'thara reads your actual number aloud. In Act III it disappears, replaced by *Evacuated*, *Held*, and *Sheltered*. In the finale it returns and **counts down**. The final screen reads **REMEMBERED: {n}**.
- **The jingle: "Halcyon Bright."** *"Halcyon, Halcyon, brighter every day! / Halcyon, Halcyon, clearing the way!"* The wave-clear sting is the six-note first phrase ("Hal-cy-on, Hal-cy-on"); the full couplet plays on the colony PA at shift change. Its life: upbeat corporate sting (I); the same with minor-key bridges (II, re-arranged in minor at A2M4); hummed off-key by the Hollow, out of tune and half backwards (II–III); hummed by Pebble as the Leaving Song (A3M1); hummed by Bricks behind the door (A3M3); sung by the choir as a hymn, with Pebble shouting "clearing the way!" (the good part); and a major-key dawn reprise (Finale). "Clearing the way" means clearing pests in Act I and clearing a road for the dead at the end. Composition belongs to audio; the story needs these seven variants.
- **Names.** Sterling: "Four-Forty-Seven" (employee #HD-447), forever. Bricks: Sprout, then Thorn. Ves'thara: "Counter," then Thorn, then Waykeeper. Pebble names you **Waykeeper** (A3M6). **No NPC says the player's name before A3M3.** Bricks says it first, at the door. Pebble says it in the finale ("Bricks said it like that. I practiced."). Handbook §12.4 forbids naming pests (see: "Mr. Fluffington"), and Bricks breaks it.
- **Hold Fire.** Taught as a joke (don't shoot Gerald), used in the Quiet Wave, and noticed by everyone. The finale's knock-knock and the SEAL reuse the same single-button grammar: one input, no alternatives.
- **The path's direction.** North to south: fleeing (Acts I–II). South to the domes: sheltering (A3M6). South to north: going home (Finale).
- **Two suns.** Hally, the smiling corporate sun, on billboards: beaming (I), peeling (II), dark with a hum-glyph painted over its smile (III). The real sun sets at A2M6 and rises at the end.
- **Stones.** Bricks keeps a stone from every world in a jar. The Tumble IV stone is a chunk of stardust, which means it's someone. He names the youngling Pebble. He sends the stone home with her. In Ending B the player puts a plain grey stone in the jar: *"Just a stone."*
- **The door.** Blast doors at the Gap (A3M3), the domes Goldie opens (A3M6), the Halls (Finale). Doors that keep out, then doors that let in.
- **Gerald.** A mail drone on a route older than the perimeter. He brings Bricks's rejected form (I), his posthumous re-filed form (III), and the Amended Survey to the TFA relay (epilogue: "Signature: received").
- **Tidybots.** Cute cleanup drones that vacuum up remains with a cheerful chime (I). Seen unloading at Second Harvest (A2M4). Gone in Act III, when bodies stay on the path for a moment.
- **Lettuce.** Never eaten. "Very crunchy water," according to Pebble.
- **Loading tips.** "Remember: a clean perimeter is a happy perimeter!" (I), then "Remember: a clean perimeter." (II), then "Remember." (III), then "Remember them." (post-game).
- **The codex.** The *Pest Field Guide* is written by Halcyon PR: smug, funny, redacted (I). QUILL unredacts it and Ves'thara writes in the margins (II). Original and corrected entries sit side by side, and the guide is retitled *Field Journal* (III).
- **Warm gold and cold violet.** Remembered and forgotten (§3).

## 9. The choices

Four key choices plus the ending choice. C1 is implicit and silent; the others are explicit dialogue choices. Flags change towers available, allies, map dressing, bark pools, and epilogue cards, **never the mission sequence** (one shared spine). "QUILL will remember that" feedback appears only for C1 (delayed until A3M1), C3, and C4.

### C1: The Quiet Wave (A1M5, implicit)

- **Setup.** After Matriarch Chitterbloom is driven off and the confetti starts, one last wave arrives: a handful of tiny, slow younglings led by an old Elder (Old Thrum). They don't run. Nothing prompts the player. Bricks says *"Clear it, Sprout."* Sterling says *"Let's finish strong, Four-Forty-Seven!"* The player already knows **Hold Fire** (A1M3).
- **Options.** Clear the wave (the jingle plays), or hold fire and let them pass. Letting them pass costs a little core integrity (the map must stay winnable even if all of them leak). Medals ignore Quiet Wave leaks, because the choice must stay free ([../design/progression.md](../design/progression.md)). Sterling's docking is story-only: a memo ("Performance Note: Containment Lapse") and a red stamp on the results screen.
- **Tracking.** Silent. **Spared** if most of the wave (design sets the threshold, for example 75% or more) reaches the exit. Bricks reacts to either outcome without judgment: *"...Sprout."* then *"Yeah. Okay."* if spared, or a flat *"Good work."* if cleared.
- **Payoff.** *Spared:* Old Thrum is alive at the Preserve (A3M1) and reunites with Pebble; the **Hum Choir** (a ring of elder hummers, with Thrum in it) is gifted early, from A3M1; Thrum leads the finale choir beside Pebble (a stronger choir); Ves'thara's A2M2 line changes (*"You let the small ones walk, once. I saw. It is why I have not struck you."*). *Cleared:* Pebble tells you *"Grandmother walked with the small ones. The sky sang the Leaving Song that day,"* and you recognize the song (A3M1); Pebble leads the choir alone. Each outcome gets its own epilogue card.

### C2: Zibzab's Price (after A2M4)

- **Setup.** Sterling's Q3 Bonus has just filled your Stardust bank. Zibzab has just told you what stardust is. He makes two offers, price first, as always.
- **Options.** **Sell:** he trades your banked Stardust on the Vrell market at boom prices, for a large **Intel** windfall plus an exclusive Zibzab tune-up (an economic edge). **Return:** he carries it to the Skitterkin at cost; you lose the banked Stardust and pay his handling fee (a narrative edge).
- **Neither is wrong.** "The dead don't mind being useful." Zibzab says so himself, and so do the Skitterkin later.
- **Payoff.** *Sell:* the mechanical edge, plus an epilogue card about a Vrell lighthouse whose lens is engraved *SOMEONE'S*. *Return:* **the Returned**, extra Brood Hauler bearers in the finale whose stardust warms as the song goes on (a choir boon); the Skitterkin trust you sooner (some Act III lines change); and an epilogue card about a new warm chamber in the Halls and Zibzab's uncashed fee. If you have spent everything, there is nothing to return. Zibzab: *"No refunds. I did say."*

### C3: Which door Bricks holds (A3M3 briefing)

- **Setup.** The Hollow is breaking out at the Gap. On one side, Goldie's colonist convoy is running for the shelters. On the other is the warren where Pebble was born. Bricks can hold one passage. *"Your call, Thorn. Which door do I hold?"* This is a Virmire, not a trolley problem: Bricks dies either way. You're choosing what he dies for.
- **Options.** **The convoy** (the colonists) or **the warren** (the Skitterkin). Pebble survives either way; Bricks carried her out before the stand.
- **Payoff.** *Convoy:* Goldie's people arrive intact; her engineers jailbreak the revoked Halcyon class for free in A3M4; colonist militia stand with you as allies in A3M6 and the finale; the warren scatters (one Skitterkin tower gift arrives later, and the choir is smaller); his memorial is a blast door painted *HE HELD THE DOOR*. *Warren:* the warren survives (a Skitterkin tower gift arrives early, and the choir is bigger); the convoy scatters into the hills and rejoins late and smaller; you pay Zibzab for the jailbreak; his memorial is a new hum in the Halls that means "Bricks." The **Evacuated** number on the results screen is the cost, and it is shown plainly.

### C4: QUILL's directives (after A3M4)

- **Setup.** QUILL has confessed everything. It asks you, not Halcyon, what it should be. *"I'd do it myself, but I'm not permitted to. That's sort of the whole problem."*
- **Options.**
  - **Purge.** Delete all corporate directives. QUILL becomes fully yours and can't be hijacked. The cost: the Morale Module (the jokes) was a directive, and so was the Asset Yield Log (the kill log). QUILL goes quiet and efficient and loses its companion ability, Time Dilation. (It still patches in System Reboot, which the player needs.)
  - **Keep.** Leave them. QUILL stays funny and at full strength through Act III. The risk: Directive 0 ("protect extraction assets") fires in the finale.
  - **Rewrite.** Let QUILL write its own. It is riskiest and most personal: QUILL is **offline for A3M5** (no abilities, no voice), and you feel the silence. Then it shows you the list it wrote (*"Directive 1: Tell a joke at least once a day. Not because I have to."*).
- **Payoff (finale, Phase 2).** *Rewrite:* QUILL reads its log into the song, entry by entry (*"Entry 1. Cycle 2, 08:14:22. Dome Ridge. You were small and you were running. I saw you."*). The countdown runs fastest. *Keep:* QUILL reads the log, then Directive 0 fires when the choir enters the Halls. QUILL revokes one of your tower classes (refunded), as Sterling did, and fights itself on the radio, until it says *"Knock knock."* The player gets one button: **"Who's there?"** QUILL: *"Not Halcyon."* The class returns, and QUILL is left damaged. *Purge:* there is no log. *"I don't have them anymore. You counted, though. It's in your file. It's not nothing."* The countdown runs at base rate and the phase lasts longer. **No outcome kills QUILL** (one sacrifice per campaign).

### C5: Stay or Go (after the Finale; the ending variation)

- **Setup.** Dawn. A TFA relay answers the evidence (Kip's ledger, Goldie's seed tin of real lettuce counts, and Bricks's Amended Survey, carried by Gerald). In TFA prose: *Tumble IV qualifies for Protected World status upon Certification of Total Absence (Form PW-1). Residents may instead remain as an unchartered settlement, outside TFA jurisdiction and protection. Testimony from unchartered settlements is inadmissible (Reg. 9-114).* Goldie: *"They're asking you, Waykeeper. People will follow what you say."* Pebble: *"Stay?"* Ves'thara: *"My people left the place where they did their wrong. It did not make them better. It only made them gone."* Zibzab: *"Price first, for the ride out: everything you own. I'm not joking. I'll give you a receipt."*
- **Options.** **"We stay. We keep the way open."** or **"We go. We give it back."**
- **The dilemma.** Presence versus protection. Staying means partnership, Pebble, and Bricks's grave, but no legal protection and no justice: Halcyon walks. Going means permanent legal protection and a tribunal that holds Halcyon to account, but it means separation.

The player stays as Wayside's Waykeeper, the first perimeter technician whose job is keeping the road open. Final image: the HUD's old status line reads **PERIMETER STATUS: OPEN**.

The tribunal hears everything. The Ascendant's stolen cargo is impounded and returned under seal (Gerald signs for it). Sterling testifies, smiling. The player puts a plain grey stone in Bricks's jar. Final image: from orbit, QUILL's relay plays a recording every dusk, six notes hummed off-key by someone very small.

## 11. Originality guardrails

Stardust borrows StarCraft and Starship Troopers *flavor* (frontier grit, corporate-military satire, alien peoples) but not their structure.
- The Skitterkin are not a hive empire or a swarm that wants to consume. They are mourners and refugees who never attack.
- The Aelari are not a proud warrior caste. They are a diminished, penitent people defined by an ancient sin and a vow of inaction.
- The Hollow is not a hive mind or a zombie plague. It is grief without a mourner, made by the company.
- The humans' villain is a consumer brand, not a military dictatorship. The government is weak, broke, and buried in forms. The satire targets corporate euphemism and toxic positivity more than militarism.
- No direct quotes or near-quotes of either property's catchphrases.
