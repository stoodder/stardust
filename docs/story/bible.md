The canon for Stardust's story: the world, the truth under it, the arc, the choices, and the endings. Characters live in [characters.md](characters.md), peoples and factions in [races.md](races.md), and writing rules in [style-guide.md](style-guide.md). Mechanics, rosters, and numbers belong to `docs/design/`; where this bible names a tower, enemy, or ability, it uses the design docs' names ([../design/towers.md](../design/towers.md), [../design/enemies.md](../design/enemies.md), [../design/core-loop.md](../design/core-loop.md#3-commander-abilities)). Spoilers throughout. These docs are for builders.

## 1. Logline

A cheerful rookie pest-control technician on a corporate lettuce colony learns that the adorable "pests" are refugees, and that the glittering mineral everyone mines is their dead. To stop the hungry dark rising out of the mines, the technician has to stop keeping them out and start keeping the way open.

## 2. Premise

It is 2361. You are a brand-new **Junior Perimeter Technician** at **Everglow Gardens**, a sunny agricultural colony on **Tumble IV** run by **Everglow Dynamics**. Your job is to run the defense grid that keeps the local **Skitterkin** (round, squeaky, many-legged "pests") out of the hydroponic lettuce domes. Your mentor is a gruff sergeant who names his turrets after people who left him. Your grid AI tells a dad joke every shift because it is contractually obligated to. Your boss is the most positive person in the Kepler Fringe. Every cleared wave plays a jingle, and a counter tallies how many pests you have processed. You are in the top 5%.

None of it is what it looks like. The Skitterkin aren't invading; they are fleeing *through* the colony, away from something in the mines. **Stardust**, the mineral Everglow extracts, is the crystallized remains of the Skitterkin dead, sung to sleep over thousands of years in the **Deep Halls** beneath the northern ridges. Ripping it up unsung has made the **Hollow**: the hungry, lonely absence of everyone who was never mourned. Everglow knows all of this. It has a plan to strip the planet and leave. You helped.

The campaign moves the core tower-defense verb, one act at a time, from *keep them out* to *keep the way open*. In the finale you defend a choir of Skitterkin as they walk the path in the opposite direction, back toward the thing they fled, singing the colony's corporate jingle as a funeral hymn. Your kill counter counts down to zero as the dead are laid to rest.

| Act | Time on Tumble IV | The verb | What the player believes | What is true |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| I · Pest Control | The Long Afternoon | Keep them out | Cute pests are eating the lettuce | Refugees are walking home through a garden built across their road |
| II · First Contact | The Long Dusk | See them | Aliens and a dark thing threaten the colony | The company made the dark thing, from the dead, with your help |
| III · The Quiet | The Long Night | Get them out; hold the door; let them in | Everything is falling apart | It is; you choose who you are while it does |
| Finale · Clearing the Way | The First Dawn | Walk them home | — | The dead can't be undone, but they can be remembered |

## 3. World and setting

### Tumble IV

The fourth planet of **Kepler-2209**, a mild orange star at the far edge of charted space. The 2346 survey team nicknamed the planet "Tumble" for its slow, wobbling rotation: one Tumble day lasts about **38 standard days**. The campaign covers roughly seven weeks. It begins in the **Long Afternoon**, passes through the **Long Dusk** and the **Long Night** (about 19 days of darkness), and ends at the **First Dawn**. The act palettes in [../vision.md](../vision.md) are therefore diegetic: the sun really is setting on the story.

The surface is a temperate valley world: terraced green hills, pink-gold mineral glints in the rock, wide skies, and two small moons. The **northern ridges** (the "Spires") hold Everglow's extraction rigs and, far beneath them, the Deep Halls. The **southern wilds** (the Brightwater basin) are open grassland and wetland where the Skitterkin live in the warm months. The colony sits in the valley between them, directly across the Skitterkin migration road.

### The Kepler Fringe and the year

The **Kepler Fringe** is the thin outer edge of human space, named after the antique star catalogue its worlds were first listed in. The joke on the frontier is that the Fringe is "where the Kepler Frontier Authority's map goes blank and Everglow's begins." The **Kepler Frontier Authority (KFA)** is the Fringe's government in name: broke, distant, and bureaucratic, it charters frontier worlds to corporations and rarely visits. The year is **2361** (Everglow's fiscal year 2361; the game opens in Q2). See [races.md](races.md#humans) for Everglow and the KFA.

### Everglow Dynamics, in one paragraph

A consumer-energy megacorp whose slogan is "Brighter Every Day!" and whose culture runs on mandatory positivity, euphemism, and quarterly targets. Everglow holds Tumble IV under a tax-favored **Agricultural Frontier Charter**. It really does grow lettuce (Everglow Brightleaf), and it books "pest losses" against that lettuce to claim KFA crop insurance. The Skitterkin have never eaten a leaf; they can't digest cellulose. The real business is **stardust**, sold across human space as a premium power-cell material and, in its finest grades, as luxury glitter. Full detail: [races.md](races.md#everglow-dynamics).

### What stardust really is

The Skitterkin carry a living crystalline lattice in their bodies (their **shimmer**, the pink-gold frost on their backs). When a Skitterkin dies, its family carries it to the Deep Halls and sings to it. Over years of singing the body resolves into stardust: a crystal that holds a faint, warm psionic echo of the mind it was. Skitterkin have done this for about forty thousand years. Every natural stardust seam on Tumble IV is a graveyard, and the deposits under the Spires are the oldest dead of a whole people.

- **Sung stardust** glows **warm pink-gold**. It is at rest.
- **Unsung stardust** glows **cold violet**. It forms when a shimmer crystallizes without being sung: torn out of the Halls by a drill, burned in a reactor, or force-crystallized in Second Harvest's vats. The echo inside it is not at rest. It is lonely, and it is hungry.

This color language is canon and should carry through art, VFX, and UI: **warm gold means remembered; cold violet means forgotten.** (Palettes themselves belong to `docs/art/`.)

Everglow's natural seams near the surface ran thin in 2357. Deep bores into the Halls were dangerous (crews reported "the quiet"), so in 2358 Everglow opened **Second Harvest**. The defense grid's cleanup drones (cheerful little **Tidybots**) collect the bodies of every "processed pest" and haul them to the plant, where heat and pressure crystallize them in hours. *Pests processed* is literal. The **Stardust** the player earns and spends at Zibzab's shop (see D001 #6) is, in the fiction, exactly this, through Act II. From A3M1 it changes hands: the warrens *give* the player sung stones, warm gold, so the currency becomes trust ([../design/progression.md §2](../design/progression.md#2-earning-stardust)).

The game does not condemn every use of stardust. The Skitterkin view, which the story endorses, is: **"The dead don't mind being useful. They mind being forgotten."** The sin is taking without remembering. That lets the meta currency stay playable after the reveal, and it is the moral the ending lands on.

### The Hollow

The Hollow is what unsung dead become. When enough unsung stardust collects (in drill spoil, reactor exhaust, the vats), the lonely echoes inside it run together into a single hungry absence that wants to be filled with minds. Its touch **hushes** the living: it drains memory, leaving people and Skitterkin dazed and blank. Hushed people recover if the Hollow is laid to rest. Those it fully takes become part of it.

The Hollow on Tumble IV is **entirely Everglow's creation**. It first stirred in 2359, when Rig Nine bored into one of the oldest chambers of the Halls. It is not a cosmic evil and it is not a replacement villain. It is the consequence: grief with no one to mourn it. Weapons can scatter its shapes, but the pieces drift back and re-form. Only the **Leaving Song**, sung by those who remember, lays it to rest. Ves'thara puts it this way: *"It is not hungry. It is lonely. When you are dead, those are the same thing."*

The Hollow *hums* the colony jingle, wrong: out of tune and half backwards. It is the last thing many of its pieces heard. (See §8.)

There is a precedent. The Aelari learned song-weaving from the Skitterkin thousands of years ago and forgot it had been mourning. About three thousand years ago they wove their own sung dead into lamps and burned them for light, and their homeworld fell to the first Hollow. See [races.md](races.md#aelari).

## 4. Core themes

**Thesis:** *You can't undo what you did. You can remember it, and you can choose who you are next.*

1. **Remembering versus consuming.** Everglow turns the dead into product; the Skitterkin turn the dead into song. Every system that consumes without remembering (the counter, the vats, the Tidybots, the employee numbers) is the antagonist. Every act of remembering (Bricks's correction, QUILL's log, the Leaving Song) is the resistance.
2. **Complicity and responsibility.** The player did it, with their own hands, and was rewarded with stars. The game never absolves them ("you didn't know") and never scolds them. Characters argue about it instead: Bricks says *"Not knowing is a choice. I kept making it."* Ves'thara says *"You were very good at it."* The question the game asks is not "are you guilty?" but "what now?"
3. **Keeping out versus letting through.** Tower defense is a genre about denial of passage. The story indicts that verb and then inverts it, act by act (§2), until the player is protecting passage.
4. **Names and numbers.** Sterling calls you by your employee number. Bricks calls you by a callsign. No one says your name until a man who is about to die says it. The pests have no names; then one has a name a sergeant gave her. The counter becomes a list of the remembered.
5. **Comedy equals tragedy plus time.** The same material (the jingle, the counter, the memos, the dad jokes) is funny first and devastating later, and in the finale it becomes sacred. Humor never leaves; it changes owners, from the company to people who love each other.

## 5. The secrets and the twist structure

The twists are layers of one question: **"What are we really doing here?"** Each layer is delivered by a different voice and in a different shape, so no two reveals feel alike (this is the research's trust-fatigue fix). The rule is **one reveal per scene, at most two per map, never the same shape** ([style-guide.md §1](style-guide.md#1-the-ten-rules), rule 5).

### The layers

| 2 | Your kills are a harvest. Tidybots haul the bodies to Second Harvest; stardust (your currency) is made from them | Zibzab, the one who never lies | **Told plainly, when asked.** Players can ask as early as A1M3 | A2M4 (or A1M3 if they ask) |
| 3 | Stardust is the sung dead; ripping it up unsung made the Hollow, and Ves'thara's mother is in it | Ves'thara, the former enemy | **Argued with you mid-battle**, then taught as an ally | Hinted A2M2; explained A2M5–A2M6; her mother, A2M6 debrief |

### Fair-play clues (what a second playthrough lights up)

- **Refugees:** every Act I spawn faces the Spires; every exit leads to open wilderness, never a colony building. Skitterkin never attack. Elders slow the swarm so the slow ones keep up. A leaked pest is logged as escaping "toward... the wilderness? Odd." Goldie's lettuce counts never go down. The Peeker watches from a rock.
- **Processing:** Tidybots vacuum up remains after every wave from map 1. The results screen says *processed*. The Brood Hauler's sack bursts into glitter, and tiny shapes run back the way they came. Second Harvest appears in Act I memos as a "sustainability success story."
- **Stardust = the dead:** Zibzab: "Everything precious was someone's once." Second Harvest's output is violet, while the natural seams are gold. The Skitterkin hum near stardust seams.
- **Sterling:** she uses your employee number and never your name. Every gift carries small print in the codex ("Budget increase contingent on Q3 extraction targets"). Her memos mention "buffer populations," "acceptable perimeter losses," and "Clean Exit readiness" in passing; the A2M4 memo cites "Protocol BP-2359"; her sincere A3M1 memo's small print says the engineers are Clean Exit Asset Recovery and the Preserve's buffer is "depleted." Her portrait smile never changes; only her eyes do.
- **QUILL:** "(Per policy.)" precedes every directive-driven line, with a one-frame yellow flicker in its portrait. Its placement suggestions protect extraction rigs over housing. It says it is "contractually obligated" to tell a joke every shift, as a joke. It isn't one. It jokes about loopholes. Zibzab asks what its price is (A1M4).
- **Bricks:** the codex cites "Survey 2346, Okonkwo" as the source of "non-linguistic chittering." He hums when nervous and denies it. He keeps a stone from every world.

### Finale: Clearing the Way (1 map in 3 phases, splittable into 2 · the First Dawn)

**Tone:** cathartic, then hopeful. **Verb:** walk them home. The Skitterkin choir walks the Long Walk **in reverse**, from the colony back north to the Deep Halls, toward the thing they fled. It is the right season at last: the Long Night is when the dead are sung. Map detail: [campaign/finale.md](campaign/finale.md#fin--clearing-the-way).

1. **The Descent.** Escort the choir north while the Hollow comes at them from every side. The route passes over Okonkwo's Gap; the wreck of the turret named {name} stands on the ridge. C1, C2, and C3 decide who walks with you (§9). Everglow's Deep Bore is drilling into the Halls' heart to fill the Ascendant, which adds a timer.
2. **The Unsung.** (The finale boss that design calls *The Unmaking*; its story name is **the Unsung**.) At the mouth of the Halls the choir sings "Everglow Bright" as the Leaving Song. Hollow units broken inside the choir's song are **laid to rest**: they turn warm gold and rise. The **counter returns and counts down** from your lifetime Pests Processed. QUILL's state (C4) changes this phase; under Keep, the knock-knock lands here. If the Quiet Wave was cleared, Thrum's echo drifts out of the Unsung and is laid to rest in gold: *"Grandmother. She heard."* The Hollow converges on the Bore; the Ascendant aborts with a partial load. Sterling says nothing on the radio; her farewell memo waits until after Dawn.
3. **Dawn.** The counter reaches 0. The last of the Unsung joins the song instead of fighting it, and the first sunlight in nineteen days crosses the valley. The jingle plays in a major key, sung by everyone. One gold mote stays at Ves'thara's shoulder, and she remembers her mother's face. Pebble says your name. The protagonist's visor goes up for the only time, as a silhouette against the sunrise.

Then **C5** and the epilogue (§10).

## 8. Recurring motifs

- **The counter.** "PESTS PROCESSED: {n} ★ Top 5% of Technicians!" on every Act I results screen. It is a real lifetime stat (it counts replays too; the game remembers everything you did). In Act II it reads "Pests processed: {n}." with no star and no exclamation point. In A2M2 Ves'thara reads your actual number aloud; in A3M2 it is the second half of the Asset Yield Log's header (41,206 + yours). In Act III it disappears, replaced by *Evacuated*, *Held*, and *Sheltered*. In the finale it returns and **counts down**; the finale's results card reads **PESTS PROCESSED: 0**, and the epilogue's counter flips it to **REMEMBERED: {n}**, the first time the word is shown.
- **The jingle: "Everglow Bright."** *"Everglow, Everglow, brighter every day! / Everglow, Everglow, clearing the way!"* The wave-clear sting is the six-note first phrase ("Ev-er-glow, Ev-er-glow"); the full couplet plays on the colony PA at shift change. Its life: upbeat corporate sting (I); the same with minor-key bridges (II, re-arranged in minor at A2M4); hummed off-key by the Hollow, out of tune and half backwards (II–III); hummed by Pebble as the Leaving Song (A3M1); hummed by Bricks behind the door (A3M3), who always hated it and finally likes it; sung by the choir as a hymn, with Pebble shouting "clearing the way!" (the good part); and a major-key dawn reprise (Finale). "Clearing the way" means clearing pests in Act I and clearing a road for the dead at the end. Composition belongs to audio; the story needs these seven variants.
- **Names.** Sterling: "Four-Forty-Seven" (employee #ED-447), forever. Bricks: Sprout, then Thorn. Ves'thara: "Counter," then Thorn, then Waykeeper. Pebble names you **Waykeeper** (A3M6). **No NPC says the player's name before A3M3.** Bricks writes it on his last turret tag a few waves before the end, and says it first, at the SEAL. Pebble says it in the finale ("Bricks said it like that. I practiced."). Handbook §12.4 forbids naming pests (see: "Mr. Fluffington"), and Bricks breaks it. The Skitterkin have names too: Pebble tells you the matriarch's (Bramble) in A3M1, and the Aelari punishment is to have your name cut out of the song (unthreading).
- **Hold Fire and the one button.** Taught as a joke (don't shoot Gerald), rehearsed as a joke (the knock-knock's optional "Who's there?" in A1M3), used in the Quiet Wave, and noticed by everyone. The SEAL and the finale's forced "Who's there?" reuse the same single-button grammar: one input, no alternatives.
- **The path's direction.** North to south: fleeing, *past* the colony (Acts I–II). North to south again in A3M6, on the same road, but the destination flips: the gate opens *into* the domes. South to north: going home (Finale), the only true reversal.
- **Two suns.** Evie, the smiling corporate sun, on billboards: beaming (I), peeling (II), dark with a hum-glyph painted over its smile (III). The real sun sets at A2M6 and rises at the end. Ves'thara's light at Sundown (the Prism) and in the finale ("I will hold the light") is the third.
- **Stones.** Bricks keeps a stone from every world in a jar. The Tumble IV stone is a chunk of stardust, which means it's someone. He names the youngling Pebble. He sends the stone home with her. In Act III the warrens give the player sung stones: Stardust that is given, not taken. In Ending B the player keeps Bricks's jar, which has a stone from every world but Tumble IV, and adds a plain grey one: *"Just a stone."* (epilogue card 12, on every Go save but `family`'s, §10).
- **The door.** Blast doors at the Gap (A3M3), the domes Goldie opens (A3M6: "Lettuce in. It's cold out here."), the Halls (Finale). Doors that keep out, then doors that let in.
- **Gerald.** A mail drone on a route older than the perimeter. He drops your intake form and leaves without a signature (the Prelude), brings Bricks's rejected form (I), his posthumous re-filed form (III), and the Amended Survey to the KFA relay (epilogue: "Signature: received").
- **Tidybots.** Cute cleanup drones that vacuum up remains with a cheerful chime (I). Seen unloading at Second Harvest (A2M4). Gone in Act III, when bodies stay on the path for a moment.
- **Lettuce.** Never eaten. "Very crunchy water," Pebble decides, if she ever tries it (the Stay epilogue). The punchline of two of QUILL's jokes.
- **Loading tips.** "Remember: a clean perimeter is a happy perimeter!" (I), then "Remember: a clean perimeter." (II), then "Remember." (III), then "Remember them." (post-game).
- **The codex.** The *Pest Field Guide* is written by Everglow PR: smug, funny, redacted (I). QUILL unredacts it and Ves'thara writes in the margins (II). The original and the corrected version sit together, the original above, dimmed, and the guide is retitled *Field Journal* (III).
- **Warm gold and cold violet.** Remembered and forgotten (§3).

## 9. The choices

Four key choices plus the ending choice. C1 is implicit and silent; the others are explicit dialogue choices. Flags change towers available, allies, map dressing, bark pools, and epilogue cards, **never the mission sequence** (one shared spine). "Will remember that" feedback appears only for C1 (delayed until A3M1, and only if spared), C3, and C4. The UI for each choice is in [campaign.md §6](campaign.md#6-choice-and-flag-ledger).

### C1: The Quiet Wave (A1M5, implicit)

- **Setup.** After Matriarch Chitterbloom is driven off and the confetti starts, one last wave arrives: a handful of tiny, slow younglings led by an old Elder (Old Thrum). They don't run. Nothing prompts the player. Bricks says *"Clear it, Sprout,"* and then lowers his own cannon and doesn't fire (allies never target these units); nobody mentions it. Sterling says *"Let's finish strong, Four-Forty-Seven!"* The player already knows **Hold Fire** (A1M3).
- **Options.** Clear the wave (the jingle plays), or hold fire and let them pass. Letting them pass costs a little core integrity (the map must stay winnable even if all of them leak). Medals ignore Quiet Wave leaks, because the choice must stay free ([../design/core-loop.md §2.7](../design/core-loop.md#27-hold-fire)). Sterling's docking is story-only: a memo ("Performance Note: Containment Lapse") and a red stamp on the results screen.
- **Tracking.** Silent. **Spared** if Old Thrum reaches the exit and at least 75% of her younglings do (spared means she lived). Bricks reacts to either outcome without judgment: *"...Sprout."* then *"Yeah. Okay."* if spared, or a flat *"Good work."* if cleared.
- **Payoff.** Grace either way, never accusation. *Spared:* Old Thrum is alive at the Preserve (A3M1) and reunites with Pebble; the **Hum Choir** (a ring of elder hummers, with Thrum in it) is gifted early, from A3M1; Thrum leads the finale choir beside Pebble (a stronger choir); Ves'thara's A2M2 line changes (*"You let the small ones walk, once. I saw. It is why I have not struck you."*). *Cleared:* Pebble tells you *"Grandmother walked with the small ones. The sky sang the Leaving Song that day,"* and you recognize the song (A3M1); there is no echo line. In the finale, Thrum's echo is among the Unsung, and it is laid to rest in gold beside the choir: *"Grandmother. She heard."* Spared players get the reunion; cleared players get the farewell. Pebble leads the choir alone. Each outcome gets its own epilogue card.

### C2: Zibzab's Price (after A2M4)

- **Setup.** Sterling's Q3 Bonus has just landed on A2M4's results screen and filled your Stardust bank. Zibzab has just told you what stardust is. He makes two offers, price first, as always.
- **Options.** **Sell:** he trades your banked Stardust on the Vrell market at boom prices, for a large **Intel** windfall plus an exclusive Zibzab tune-up (an economic edge). **Return:** he carries it to the Skitterkin at cost; you lose the banked Stardust and pay his handling fee (a narrative edge).
- **Neither is wrong.** Zibzab says so in the offer itself: *"The dead don't mind being useful. They mind being forgotten."* Bramble says it back to you in A3M1, when the warrens give you their first sung stones.
- **Payoff.** *Sell:* the mechanical edge, plus an epilogue card about a Vrell lighthouse whose lens is engraved *SOMEONE'S*. *Return:* **the Returned**, extra Brood Hauler bearers in the finale whose stardust warms as the song goes on (a choir boon); the Skitterkin trust you sooner (some Act III lines change); and an epilogue card about a new warm chamber in the Halls and Zibzab's uncashed fee. If you have spent everything, there is nothing to return. Zibzab: *"No refunds. I did say."* Numbers: [../design/progression.md §2.1](../design/progression.md#21-story-hooks).

### C3: Which door Bricks holds (A3M3 briefing)

- **Setup.** The Hollow is breaking out at the Gap. On one side, Goldie's colonist convoy is running for the shelters. On the other is the warren where Pebble was born. Bricks can hold one passage. *"Your call, Thorn. Which door do I hold?"* This is a Virmire, not a trolley problem: Bricks dies either way. You're choosing what he dies for.
- **Options.** **The convoy** (the colonists) or **the warren** (the Skitterkin). Pebble survives either way; Bricks carried her out before the stand.
- **Payoff.** *Convoy:* Goldie's people arrive intact; her engineers jailbreak the revoked Everglow class for free in A3M4; colonist militia stand with you as allies in A3M6 and the finale; the warren scatters (one Skitterkin tower gift arrives later, and the choir is smaller); his memorial is a blast door painted *HE HELD THE DOOR*. *Warren:* the warren survives (a Skitterkin tower gift arrives early, and the choir is bigger); the convoy scatters into the hills and rejoins late and smaller; you pay Zibzab for the jailbreak; his memorial is a new hum in the Halls that means "Bricks." The **Evacuated** number on the results screen is the cost, and it is shown plainly; the reward is the same at any score.

### C4: QUILL's directives (after A3M4)

- **Setup.** QUILL has confessed everything. It asks you, not Everglow, what it should be. *"I'd decide for myself, but I'm not permitted to. That's sort of the whole problem."*
- **Options** (each has a real cost and a real benefit, and none is required to win; the mechanics are design's, [../design/core-loop.md §6.11](../design/core-loop.md#611-quills-directives-c4-in-play)):
  - **Purge.** Delete all corporate directives. QUILL becomes fully yours and can't be hijacked. With no Directive 0 it is free to fight Everglow: it works against Everglow Security from inside from A3M5 on, and it slows the Deep Bore in finale Phase 1. The cost: the Morale Module (the jokes) was a directive, and so was the Asset Yield Log (the kill log). QUILL goes quiet and efficient, loses Time Dilation for good, and keeps System Reboot.
  - **Keep.** Leave them. QUILL stays funny and at full strength through Act III, and lends you **Time Dilation** as a Technician ability from A3M5 through finale Phase 1. The cost: Directive 0 ("protect extraction assets") fires in the finale, and Time Dilation goes dark for Phase 2.
  - **Rewrite.** Let QUILL write its own. It is the most personal: QUILL is **offline for A3M5** (no abilities, no voice, so no System Reboot there), and you feel the silence. Back online in A3M6's briefing, it shows you the list it wrote (*"Directive 1, written by me: tell a joke at least once a day. Not because I have to. Because Pebble laughs."*).
- **Payoff (finale, Phase 2).** *Rewrite:* QUILL reads its log into the song, entry by entry (*"Entry one. Cycle two, 08:14:22, Dome Row. You were small and you were running. I saw you. I'm sorry."*). The countdown runs fastest. *Keep:* QUILL doesn't read the log; Directive 0 fires when the choir enters the Halls. QUILL revokes one of your tower classes (refunded), as Sterling did, and fights itself on the radio, until it says *"Knock knock."* The player gets one button: **"Who's there?"** QUILL: *"Not Everglow."* The class returns, and QUILL is left damaged. *Purge:* there is no log. *"I don't have them anymore. You counted, though. It's in your file. It's not nothing."* The countdown runs at base rate and the phase lasts longer, but the Bore was slower and Everglow's machines softer on the way there. **No outcome kills QUILL** (one sacrifice per campaign).

### C5: Stay or Go (after the Finale; the ending variation)

- **Setup.** Dawn. A KFA relay answers the evidence (Kip's ledger, Goldie's seed tin of real lettuce counts, and Bricks's Amended Survey, carried by Gerald). In KFA prose: *Tumble IV qualifies for Protected World status upon Certification of Total Absence (Form PW-1). Residents may instead remain as an unchartered settlement, outside KFA jurisdiction and protection. Testimony from unchartered settlements is inadmissible (Reg. 9-114). Residency points accrued under charter are honored upon evacuation only (Reg. 4-02).*
- **The voices.** Goldie: *"They're asking you, Waykeeper. People will follow what you say."* For Stay: Pebble: *"Stay?"* and Ves'thara: *"My people left the place where they did their wrong. It did not make them better. It only made them gone."* For Go: Bramble, via Ves'thara: *"This road carried our dead before your domes. Let it be only a road again."* Zibzab, for neither: *"Price first, for the ride out: everything you own. I'm not joking. I'll give you a receipt."* If `arrival_reason = family`, the form adds a line with your family's name on it: the residency points you came out here for.
- **Options.** **"We stay. We keep the way open."** or **"We go. We leave them their road."**
- **The dilemma.** Presence versus protection. Staying means partnership, Pebble, and Bricks's grave, but no legal protection and no justice: Everglow walks, Kip's testimony is inadmissible, and any residency points lapse. Going means permanent legal protection, a tribunal that holds Everglow to account, and the family's residency honored, but it means separation, and the Skitterkin get their road back empty.

## 10. Endings

Every player gets the same finale (§6): the song, the countdown, the dawn. That shared spine is where the catharsis lives. **C5 is the one meaningful variation**, and it changes what hope looks like, not whether there is any. Both endings end at dawn with the jingle in a major key.

### Ending A (default): The Open Perimeter (Stay)

The colony takes down the domes that sit on the Long Walk, pane by pane, and rebuilds beside it. The next evening the Skitterkin walk through, and nobody counts them. Everglow Gardens becomes **Wayside**. Humans learn to hum. The KFA ignores them; Everglow writes Tumble IV off as a total loss, and Sterling is promoted. The player stays as Wayside's Waykeeper, the first perimeter technician whose job is keeping the road open. Final image: the HUD's old status line reads **PERIMETER STATUS: OPEN**.

### Ending B (variation): The Long Walk Home (Go)

The colonists leave on Zibzab's freighters for everything they own, and he writes them a receipt. Tumble IV enters the Register of Protected Worlds; the player signs Form PW-1. The tribunal hears everything. The Ascendant's stolen cargo is impounded and returned under seal (Gerald signs for it). Sterling testifies, smiling. The player puts a plain grey stone in Bricks's jar (card 12, unless the family's residency card takes the slot). Final image: from orbit, QUILL's relay plays a recording every dusk, six notes hummed off-key by someone very small.

## 11. Originality guardrails

Stardust borrows StarCraft and Starship Troopers *flavor* (frontier grit, corporate-military satire, alien peoples) but not their structure, and no names that read as borrowed.
- The Skitterkin are not a hive empire or a swarm that wants to consume. They are mourners and refugees who never attack.
- The Aelari are not a proud warrior caste of plated crystal psions. They are woven people, glass thread that hums and frays, defined by a forgotten debt (they learned song-weaving from the Skitterkin and forgot it was mourning), an ancient sin, and a law of *weave nothing new*. Their punishment is unthreading (not being sung), not exile.
- The Hollow is not a hive mind or a zombie plague. It is grief without a mourner, made by the company.
- The humans' villain is a consumer brand, not a military dictatorship. The government is weak, broke, and buried in forms. The satire targets corporate euphemism and toxic positivity more than militarism.
- **Names stay ours.** The megacorp is Everglow (not a name another sci-fi corp uses), the government is the Kepler Frontier Authority (no "Terran"), there is at most one "Brood" name (Brood Hauler), and bosses are named for what they do in the story (the Sunderglass *Penitent* kneels).
- No direct quotes or near-quotes of either property's catchphrases.
