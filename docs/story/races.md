# Races and Factions

The peoples of Stardust: culture, biology or tech, history with stardust and the Hollow, visual language, speech, and role in story and gameplay. The world and its secrets are in [bible.md](bible.md); individual characters are in [characters.md](characters.md). Palettes, shape language, and sprite specs belong to [../art/art-direction.md](../art/art-direction.md#4-faction-visual-language); visual notes here are short and only say what the art must *communicate*. Unit and tower rosters belong to [../design/enemies.md](../design/enemies.md) and [../design/towers.md](../design/towers.md).

| People | Story role | As enemies | As towers |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Humans: Everglow Dynamics** | The moral center of the story's wrong | **Everglow Security** machines, Act III | **Everglow** towers (starting kit; license revoked A3M2) |

| **The Hollow** | The consequence: grief with no mourner | Act II late to the finale | Never |

## Humans

Humanity in 2361 is spread thin across hundreds of worlds. The rich **Core** worlds are governed tightly. The **Kepler Fringe** at the edge is governed mostly on paper. Humans are the only people in the story who fight with machines instead of minds or songs, and the only people who count things instead of remembering them.

### Everglow Dynamics

- **What it is.** A consumer-energy megacorp, headquartered on Everglow Prime, whose products ("Brighter Every Day!") run on stardust: premium power cells, luxury glitter, and "glow" cosmetics. Its mascot is **Evie**, a beaming cartoon sun on every billboard, memo, and tower base plate.
- **Culture.** Mandatory positivity. Quarterly everything. The Employee Handbook covers smiling (Smile Audits measure "mandated sincerity"), Mandatory Fun Fridays ("attendance optional (required)"), and naming pests (forbidden, §12.4, "see: Mr. Fluffington"). Problems are opportunities; people are headcount; deaths are "unscheduled departures." The company doesn't think of itself as cruel. It doesn't think of itself at all. It optimizes.
- **Tech.** Clean, white-and-teal, rounded, branded, warrantied. AI-managed grids (QUILL), drones for everything (Tidybots, mail drones, compliance drones), and extraction rigs that bore kilometers deep. Everglow doesn't risk staff; it sends product.
- **History with stardust.** Everglow co-funded Survey 2346, took the charter in 2349, extracted for twelve years, opened Second Harvest in 2358, and learned what the Hollow is from its own science team in 2359. It wrote it up as a risk register. Its response was the Buffer Protocol and Clean Exit (see [bible.md §3](bible.md#3-world-and-setting)).
- **Role in story.** **The moral center of the wrong.** Not the Hollow and not the Aelari: the corp. Every reveal traces back to an Everglow decision made in a cheerful meeting. Director Joy Sterling is its face.
- **Role in gameplay.** **Everglow towers** are the starting kit: issued, reliable, and readable. A class is revoked mid-wave in A3M2 and restored as jailbroken firmware in A3M4. **Everglow Security** (remote drones and walkers, never human pilots, so the player never guns down people) enters as an enemy faction in A3M2, when Clean Exit sends Compliance Drones to repossess the colony core. Sterling's boss platform appears in A3M5. In-world, Auditor units carry Compliance Officer Prim's chassis.
- **Visual language.** Friendly rounded rectangles, safety stripes, logo plates, and symmetry. Everything clean, until it isn't: billboards and base plates fade and get defaced across the acts.
- **How they speak.** Corporate cheer: exclamation points, "super," "love that," "let's circle back," "per policy." Euphemism is the dialect. Their memo format is in the [style guide](style-guide.md#61-everglow-memos).

**The Everglow euphemism dictionary** (canon; use it consistently):

|---|---|
| Pests | Skitterkin |
| Processed | Killed and collected |
| Throughput / yield | Bodies through the vats |
| Reclamation | Turning the dead into stardust |
| Cargo | Young |

### The Kepler Frontier Authority (KFA)

- **What it is.** The human government of the Kepler Fringe, in name. It is broke and distant, and it charters worlds to corporations because it can't afford to run them. Its motto, "Order to the Edge," is printed on forms that take eleven weeks to process. It recruits frontier workers with **Core Residency points**: a few years of colony work earns your family the right to live somewhere safe. That is why many colonists, and possibly the protagonist, are here.
- **Tech and presence.** Almost none on Tumble IV. There is a relay beacon, a stack of forms, and the occasional memo. The KFA never appears in person; it is a voice in paperwork.
- **History with stardust.** It accepted Survey 2346's "non-sapient fauna" classification. It misfiled the Aelari petition in 2355. It pays Everglow's crop-insurance claims for lettuce that no one ate.
- **Role in story.** **The law that matters at the end.** Under its **Sapient Worlds Act**, Bricks's Amended Survey reclassifies the Skitterkin as sapient. Kip's ledger and Goldie's seed tin prove the fraud. Then the KFA's rigid rules create C5: a Protected World requires **Certification of Total Absence (Form PW-1)**, and testimony from unchartered settlements is inadmissible (Reg. 9-114). The KFA is satire about bureaucracy, but it isn't a villain. When the forms finally move, they do the right thing.
- **How they speak.** In forms: numbered regulations, passive voice, and "the undersigned."

### The colonists

About 340 people: lettuce farmers, technicians, mechanics, cooks, and their kids, mostly contract workers earning residency points. They're funny, tired, and decent, and they believed the memos because believing the memos was part of the job. Goldie is their face. They are the people at risk in the buildings, and in Act III they become the people who decide to open the domes. In gameplay they appear as convoy and evacuee context, and under C3 Convoy as militia allies.

## Skitterkin

*Everglow's name. What they call themselves is a hummed chord humans can't pronounce; the Aelari render it "the Singers." In Act III the codex adds that note, but everyone keeps saying "Skitterkin," because the player knows that word.*

- **Biology.** Round, fuzzy, six-legged, about knee-high as adults and teacup-sized as younglings, with big dark eyes. They have soft butter-colored bellies and moss-like tufts. Across their backs runs the **shimmer**: a living crystalline lattice, faint and pink-gold in the young and brighter with age. It is how they think and how they sing. They hum constantly, in harmony, and the hum is their language. They eat roots, fungi, and mineral licks. **They can't digest cellulose, so they have never eaten the lettuce.** They don't fight. When cornered they run, or scatter, or huddle around their young. The first line of [../design/enemies.md §5.1](../design/enemies.md#51-skitterkin-body-organic) is canon: they never attack anything.
- **Culture.** Matrilineal warrens led by matriarchs, with elders as memory-keepers. Their names are hums; humans hear them through a translator as a word or two (Pebble, Old Thrum, Bramble). Their whole culture centers on **the Remembering**: when one of them dies, the family carries the body along **the Long Walk** to the **Deep Halls** and sings to it until, years later, it becomes stardust and rests. They don't count individuals; they remember them. ("We don't count. Counting is for things you might lose.") They have no word for "enemy." The closest is "the hungry quiet," their name for the Hollow, which they call "the ones who weren't sung." On the use of the dead, they are generous: **"The dead don't mind being useful. They mind being forgotten."**
- **Tech.** None, in the human sense. They build woven-grass burrows, moss mounds, and little lanterns of glowing fungus. Their one great work is the Halls, which is a song more than a building. They are also, without knowing it, the first teachers of the Aelari: song-weaving began here (see Aelari).
- **History with stardust.** Stardust is their dead: about forty thousand years of them. The **Long Walk** has always run north to the Halls at dusk and south to the Brightwater basin at dawn. Everglow built the colony across it. Since 2359 they have been fleeing *south, out of season*, away from the Hollow rising in the Halls, through the colony, carrying their young and whatever of their dead they could rescue from the drill spoil.
- **The Leaving Song.** From the hills above the colony they heard a six-note phrase play from the sky (the PA's wave-clear jingle) every time a large group of them died at once. Over two years it became the song they sing for the dead. Pebble hums it in A3M1. See [bible.md §8](bible.md#8-recurring-motifs).
- **Visual language.** Round bodies, big eyes, tufts, stubby legs, and a bounce in every animation; cute by default. In Act II, the same sprites glance backward, panicked. As allies they use the same sprites with the `skitterkin.ally` variant, so the player recognizes the creatures they shot.
- **How they speak.** Translated (by QUILL's translator, by Ves'thara, or in Pebble's learned English), Skitterkin speech is present tense, concrete, and gentle, with odd beautiful phrasings: "the sky sings when we leave," "tall ones" (humans), "small ones" (young), "leaving ones" (the dying and the dead). Raw speech in text is rendered as hums and squeaks in parentheses: `(hum, low)`, `(squeak)`.
- **Role in story.** The refugees, the mourners, and the heart. Act I's "pests," Act II's revelation, and Act III's people. In the finale they walk the path the other way, home.
- **Role in gameplay.** An **enemy faction in Acts I–II** that never attacks: Nibblers, Zoomers, Chonks, Puffwings, Bunchlings, Diggers, Elders, Brood Haulers, and the Act I boss **Matriarch Chitterbloom** (Everglow's codename; her name is **Bramble**, which Pebble tells you in A3M1), who is driven off, never killed. From Act II they appear in mixed waves fleeing ahead of the Hollow (they leak for nothing; sparing them is free). From A2M5 they are **escorted refugees**. From A3M1 they are a **tower faction**: Scuttle Den, Hum Choir, Gloop Spitter, and Sproing Pad, which are always given, never sold, and the warrens give the player sung stones (Act III's Stardust). Bramble grants the **Underfoot** ability. In the finale they are the choir.
- **Story answers to design's open questions.** Hollow units **should not** consume Skitterkin tower units; keep the promise that "their units never die" ([../design/towers.md §8](../design/towers.md#8-presentation-and-personality)). The game's grief is about what the player did, not about punishing them for trusting their allies. Bramble becoming an ally is **confirmed** (A3M1).

## Aelari

- **Biology.** Tall, slender people who are **woven, not plated**: each body is a lattice of fine glass threads over a slow inner light, like stained glass that breathes. Every thread hums, so an Aelari's voice is a chord and a crowd of them sounds like an instrument tuning. They are long-lived (centuries) and psionic in the way a struck string is: they speak to each other by resonance, mind to mind, and they can draw song into glass thread and weave it into shape. Injury frays them; age dulls the threads; grief makes them go slack and quiet. Like the Skitterkin they carry a shimmer, and their dead can become stardust if sung.
- **History.** About nine thousand years ago Aelari travelers reached Tumble IV, heard the Skitterkin singing their dead into crystal, and carried the art home as **song-weaving**: drawing song into glass and weaving it. Over the millennia they forgot where it came from and what it was for. It became their great art, then their industry. During **the Brightening**, about three thousand years ago, their homeworld **Ilun** wove its own sung dead into lamps and burned them for light, and Ilun fell to the first Hollow. About twelve hundred years ago a band of survivors followed the oldest pattern in their oldest hymn, a star-route nobody had believed, back to Tumble IV, and found the Skitterkin still singing. They *relearned mourning from the people they had learned weaving from.* The elder race's art came from the "pests." Tumble IV became a holy place to them. A small **Watch** stayed to keep vigil over the Deep Halls, and a few Aelari dead were sung there too, including Ves'thara's mother, Ishael.
- **Culture.** Penitent, careful, and diminished. Every Aelari is a thread in a family **chord**, and every chord is woven into the **Long Weave**, the great tapestry in which the Aelari sing each other's dead. Their law since Ilun is the **Concord of Quiet Hands**: *mend; weave nothing new.* They repair old patterns and refuse to make new ones, because the last new thing they wove was the Brightening. Their caution is sincere and also a kind of complicity. When Everglow came, the Watch filed a petition, the KFA misfiled it, and the Concord would weave no answer. The Concord's sentence is not exile. It is **unthreading**: your thread is cut from the Long Weave, so when you die no one will sing you. Ves'thara wove war-constructs to break Everglow's rigs, and they unthreaded her for it (see [characters.md](characters.md#vesthara)). They value precision, patience, memory, and silence, and they find humans exhausting.
- **Tech.** **Lumenglass**: glass drawn into thread with song and woven into lattices, beams, and constructs that glide in formation and hum or chime when struck. They are piloted from a distance by resonance, so Aelari rarely risk their own bodies. Stardust is taboo to them; they never use it.
- **History with the Hollow.** They made the first one. They know exactly what it is and how it ends: only by mourning. That knowledge is Ves'thara's gift to the player in Act II.
- **Visual language.** Woven lattices of glass thread in jewel tones (sapphire and emerald, with ruby at the knots), with see-through gaps; tall, near-symmetric silhouettes; threads that hum (a slow vibration) and fray when hurt; colored outlines that make them look lit from inside. Never armor plates, never faceted crystal slabs. Specs: [../art/art-direction.md §4](../art/art-direction.md#4-faction-visual-language).
- **How they speak.** Formal, with **no contractions**. They name people by role ("Counter," "Old Soldier," "Trader"). Images of light, glass, thread, and memory. They don't raise their voices; when an Aelari is angry, the sentences get shorter.
- **Role in story.** The elder people who already made Everglow's mistake, and who forgot that their own art began as mourning. They are the warning (Ilun), the mirror (caution as complicity), and, through Ves'thara, the bridge: they translate the Skitterkin and explain the Hollow.
- **Role in gameplay.** An **enemy faction in Act II**: Ves'thara's lumenglass constructs (Glimmers, Facet Wardens, Veilwalkers, Lightwings, Blinkstriders, Aegis Cantors, and her **Sunderglass Penitent**, a woven giant that kneels), which target rigs and the plant, never homes. From A2M5 they are a **tower faction** attuned through Ves'thara: Prism Spire as a gift, then Mindspike Obelisk, Seer's Lantern, and Stasis Bloom in R&D. Her ability is **Stillness**.

## Vrell

- **Biology.** Squat, four-armed, hermit-crab-like mollusks with independent eyestalks. Each Vrell carries an enormous shell that is its home, its shop, its vault, and its status. A bigger shell means a better trader. Shells are grown, then extended with scavenged plating, so every Vrell is a walking patchwork of brass, rust, and other people's spare parts. They are slow on foot, have excellent hearing, and their memory for debts is extraordinary.
- **Culture.** A trading people spread through every frontier in the galaxy, bound by the **Fair Ledger**: a Vrell's word is a contract, and a lie is a *debt to the universe* that must be repaid. So Vrell don't lie. They don't volunteer things either; volunteering is giving it away. Their greeting and their ethic is **"Price first"**: always state the cost before the thing. They sell to anyone. Who they sell to is not a moral question to them. *Whether they told you the price* is.
- **Tech.** Jury-rigged, ingenious, mismatched, and never unreliable in practice. A Vrell repair looks like it will fall apart and won't.
- **History with stardust and the Hollow.** The Vrell are the biggest stardust traders in the Fringe and have always known what it is: "Everything precious was someone's once." They consider it neither sacred nor profane, just precious, and correctly priced only when you know where it came from. They have also lost a trading moon to a Hollow and know the signs. Vrell insurers won't cover Tumble IV. To them the Hollow is "the worst customer": it doesn't want anything; it only wants.
- **Visual language.** Asymmetry, bolted plates, exposed pipes, one oversized limb, and price tags on everything. Brass, rust, and patina, with warm welding glow.
- **How they speak.** "Price first." They say the cost before the clause, use contract phrasing, correct their own marketing in the same breath, and are relentlessly literal about honesty. They never say "free."
- **Role in story.** The **moral mirror**. Zibzab never lies, and in the end he is the only one who always told you the price. Everglow hid the price; the Vrell say it first.
- **Role in gameplay.** Never enemies (this answers [../design/enemies.md](../design/enemies.md) open question 5: Vrell don't fight, because fighting is bad for repeat business). A **tower faction** licensed with Stardust at Zibzab's shop (Salvage Crane, Zapcoil, Grapple Harpoon, Lookout Shack), plus the shop itself and the **Clearance Sale** ability.

## The Hollow

*Full lore is in [bible.md §3](bible.md#the-hollow). Note for design: the Hollow is not a "void/nanite intelligence." It is the unmourned dead, made by Everglow's mining. [../design/enemies.md §5.4](../design/enemies.md#54-the-hollow-body-void) should describe it that way; its mechanics (splitting, consuming, phasing, jamming) all fit as written.*

- **What it is.** What unsung dead become: lonely echoes torn out of the Halls or crystallized in the vats, running together into one hungry absence that wants to be filled with minds. It is not evil, and it is not a person. It is grief with no one to mourn it.
- **"Biology."** It has no bodies. It has shapes: silhouettes of what its pieces used to be (mostly Skitterkin, some Aelari, a few things no one recognizes), drawn wrong. When broken they scatter into **motes** that drift back and re-form. Its touch **hushes**: it drains memory, leaving the living dazed and blank. People hushed by it recover if it is laid to rest. Those it fully takes become part of it. The story's word for its tower-jamming is *hushing*: the Silencers make towers go quiet.
- **"Culture."** None. It hums, though. It hums the colony jingle wrong (out of tune, half backwards, broken), because it is the last thing many of its pieces heard. It also echoes fragments of other things it has consumed: snippets of the PA, of QUILL ("...logging..."), and of the hushed. It doesn't speak in dialogue. If it ever has a text line, the line is a fragment of someone else's.
- **History.** Ilun, three thousand years ago. On Tumble IV, Rig Nine in 2359, growing on drill spoil, reactor exhaust, and Second Harvest's vats, and strongest in the Long Night.
- **How it ends.** Not by force. Weapons scatter it; only the **Leaving Song**, sung by people who remember, lays it to rest. Laid-to-rest pieces turn warm gold and rise. That is the finale.
- **Visual language.** Wrong geometry: shapes that don't match their animation, missing pixels, limbs that pop between frames, no eyes or too many. Drained grey bodies, the sickly yellow-green hue reserved for the Hollow alone, and **inverted (pale) outlines** around dark interiors. Unsung stardust glows cold violet. When the Hollow is laid to rest, its color returns to warm gold. Music and SFX drop out near it; the silence is part of the effect.
- **Role in story.** **The consequence, not the villain.** It lets no one off the hook: every Hollow shape on the path is someone Everglow, and the player, failed to mourn.
- **Role in gameplay.** An **enemy faction from the start of Act II (first seen in A2M1) to the finale**: Motes, Fractures, Maws, Rift Moths, Undertows, and Silencers, with the **Gathering Maw** (A3M6) and the finale's **Unsung** (design's working name: *The Unmaking*). They attack anything that isn't a tower: fleeing Skitterkin, refugees, and Bricks at the Gap. They never damage towers; Silencers can only briefly jam them.
