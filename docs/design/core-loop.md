# Core Loop

How a play session flows, how a map plays minute to minute, how you win or lose, and how the in-map economy works.

> **Numbers:** every number in this doc is a **first-pass tuning value** unless it is labeled a **rule**. Tune freely within the rules. Tuning values live in data files, not engine code (see `/CLAUDE.md`, "Content is data"). Once the content files exist, they become the home for exact numbers, and this doc keeps roles, rules, and target ranges (see [../tech/architecture.md](../tech/architecture.md)).

Related docs: [towers.md](towers.md) (tower stats, upgrades, targeting, synergy links) · [enemies.md](enemies.md) (enemy roster and the damage/defense matrix) · [progression.md](progression.md) (Stardust, Intel, medals, shop, R&D) · [maps.md](maps.md) (tiles, paths, difficulty curve) · [../story/campaign.md](../story/campaign.md) (the order of maps, introductions, and unlocks; authoritative for ordering).

## 1. Session loop (between maps)

```
New game: Character creation (§6.1) ─► A1M1
Star map ─► Mission briefing ─► Loadout ─► Play the map ─► Results ─► Debrief ─► Base (inbox / shop / R&D / codex) ─► Star map
                 ▲                                │
                 └──────── Retry / change loadout ◄┘ (on a loss, or by choice)
After the finale: Ending choice (C5) ─► Epilogue cards, one at a time, before the counter and the credits (§6.8) ─► post-game star map
```

| Step | What the player does | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Star map** | Picks a mission node. | The main campaign path is linear; [campaign.md](../story/campaign.md) decides any side missions. Replayable nodes show earned medals and offer veteran modifiers ([progression.md §6](progression.md#6-veteran-modifiers-replays)). Act I plays from the **Shift board** (Everglow's duty roster: each shift's name, medals, and Start or Replay); once A1M5 is won, the star map (on screen, the **Sector map**) replaces it as the world widens (§1.1). **As built,** both: the Shift board through Act I, the star map from A1M5 won (T301b). |
| **Mission briefing** | Reads a short briefing (a memo, QUILL, or the companion NPC). Sees the **threat intel** strip: one icon for every defense trait on this map (the map's `threatIntel`, for example *Flying*, *Shielded*, *Cloaked*) and each required counter ("Required: Air"), always in view; the owned towers that answer it, and the medal conditions with the optional objectives, are one press away (T803e). Each run of one speaker's lines sits beside that speaker's face. | Rule: threat intel is always shown and always accurate. Losses should teach, never ambush (vision pillar 3). A map may hold back *one* surprise for its final wave, and the briefing hints at it ("QUILL: sensors show something big under the soil"). Which traits a map *requires* an answer to is a separate field ([maps.md §6.3](maps.md#63-one-new-thing-per-map)). |
| **Loadout** | Picks **up to 4 tower types**, a **Technician ability**, and **Field Protocols** (meta perks). Sees which synergy links the chosen towers can form. | Rule: the 4-tower limit is a hard rule (`/CLAUDE.md`). The loadout locks when the map starts. Restarting a map lets you change it. **Rule:** the loadout screen is shown only when there is a choice to make: the player owns more than 4 tower types, or has a real choice of Technician ability, Field Protocols, or companion (the finale pick list). Carried in equipped, the screen **benches** it for a protocol that acts there (the first other one the player owns, in the picker's order; it stays if they own none), and its row says so, "Benched: no core on this shift", until the player equips it again (D144). Every protocol row shows its whole effect and note, never clipped. |
| **Play the map** | Plays the waves (section 2). | Typically 6–20 minutes at 1x; see [maps.md §6](maps.md#6-difficulty-curve). |
| **Results** | Sees victory or defeat, medals earned, Stardust and Intel gained, and the act's counter line (§6.2). | On a loss, offers: Retry (same loadout), Retry from a checkpoint (§2.12, Act II on), Change loadout, Back to star map. Change loadout shows after a win too, wherever the map offers the loadout screen; it opens that map's loadout screen. A win can play full-screen scenes around the card, every time and skippable: A1M5's party before it, and its stinger as the card is left, whichever way (`content/dialogue/resultsFlow.ts`). |
| **Debrief** | Reads a short scene (≤ 6 lines) and, after some maps, makes a story choice (C2–C4, [../story/bible.md §9](../story/bible.md#9-the-choices)). | The story choice comes only after a victory (or a Holdout's scripted end). As built, the debrief's lines show on the Results card after a win or a loss: a loss gets a short supportive line, never a scolding ([D059](../decisions.md#d059--2026-11-09--m2-review-round-1-rulings)). Skippable on replays. A debrief's story choice is answered on the Results card: its options stand in for the way on until one is picked (A2M4's C2, price first: the stake, the whole bank, heads the choice, and each option shows what it pays under it, Sell's Intel windfall and the Zibzab Tune-up, Return's "Fee: an IOU"), then the card shows what it did and offers the way on ([../tech/meta-runtime.md § Story transactions](../tech/meta-runtime.md#story-transactions)). A stray key can't answer it: the focus starts on the stake, not an option, and the options wait 700 ms before they take a press. A choice never answered (the tab closed on the card) is offered at the base before anything else, priced at the bank then ([D138](../decisions.md#d138--2026-11-10--m5-review-round-1-rulings)). A2M6's debrief (Ishael) plays full-screen before its card instead of on it. |
| **Base** | Reads the **Everglow Inbox** (§6.4), spends Stardust at **Zibzab's shop**, Intel in **R&D**, reads the **codex** (§6.5), checks the **Pest Bounty Board** (§6.6, Acts I–II), talks to NPCs. | The base hub is the Perimeter Office (working name). Story scenes can play here. See [progression.md](progression.md). |

**The loadout screen as built.** It offers the towers, the Technician ability, and the Field Protocols (owned ones, up to the save's slots; one save-wide set, so a change carries to every map). The finale's companion pick joins with the finale (M7).
- It comes before the briefing, which opens over the battle as the map's `_brief` scene. The star map's node panel previews a map (§1.1) but never replaces its briefing.
- The order the player picks towers in is their build-slot order (keys 1–4; the Towers heading's tooltip says so).
- The header gives the kicker (shift and map) its own line; the threats and the counters the map needs sit beside the heading under it.
- Each tower shows its icon, role, cost (after the save's discounts), and which of the map's counters it answers, from which tier or spec.
- Under the towers, the Field Protocols toggle on and off; with one slot, picking another swaps it in, and Specialist picks its tower once equipped. A row is its name; an equipped one shows its effect too, and the others show theirs on a line under the list while hovered or focused (T803d).
- A side column shows the Technician's abilities, slot 2 (the map's companion: a line of its own only where no Technician card already says "Bricks brings it"), and the links: the formed ones in view right under the Technician list (up to three with their bonus under each name; more by name, and by name only under the finale's *Walking with you*, where the column has no room for the bonuses; D156), the whole list (bonuses, and what each still needs) one press away under **Every link**, which opens over the column (T803d).
- The footer, beside Lock in, gives a heads-up for any required counter the pick leaves unanswered ("Heads up: nothing here answers Air. Rivet, Sprinkler (Cloudburst), Skyhook or 7 more would."): the first three towers that would answer it, and how many more. It never blocks the pick.
- A run's rules show where they bite (T803d): Short-Handed's limit of 3 by the tower count (a fourth pick is refused, and says why); a challenge's faction lists its towers first and greys the rest, "Stays home"; Radio Silence and No Protocols as a line under their heading. The post-game maps (Endless, the challenges) offer the screen too.
- The last pick on each map is remembered; a map with none starts from the last pick anywhere. Retry keeps it, and the briefing's *Change loadout* and Results' *Change loadout* go back to the screen.

The rules and the code are in [../tech/meta-runtime.md § Meta](../tech/meta-runtime.md#meta-srcmeta).

### 1.1 The star map

The campaign screen from Act II on. On screen it is the **Sector map**: Everglow Perimeter Ops' survey chart of the valley, since every node is on Tumble IV; the code calls it the star map. Its look is [art-direction.md §10](../art/art-direction.md#10-the-star-map), its build is [architecture.md § UI: DOM overlay](../tech/architecture.md#ui-dom-overlay), and every string it shows is in [story/style-guide.md §2](../story/style-guide.md#2-voice-by-act).

**When it replaces the Shift board.** The campaign screen is the Shift board until A1M5 is won and the star map from then on, for good. The Shift board is Act I's view: it lists Act I's five shifts and one dimmed, padlocked sixth row that never names its map. Every way back to the campaign (Continue or Load from the Title, an import, the Base's and the loadout screen's way back, Results after a loss) reaches whichever of the two the save is on. The switch is a story beat: the stinger has just named Rig Nine, and the world widens.

**Nodes.** One node per campaign map, standing at its real place in the valley (the place is [campaign.md §2](../story/campaign.md#2-overview)'s biome column). **What the map shows.** Never a map the player can't see yet:
- **Cleared:** every won map, with its medals.
- **Next:** the *frontier*, the first map in campaign order that isn't won. It is *Next up* when its content is built, and a **teaser** (*Coming soon*) when it isn't: named, with its tagline, padlocked, and no Start.

Example: with A2M3 won and A2M4 not built, the frontier is the teaser *Second Harvest*, whose tagline ends "Ask Zibzab where stardust comes from."

The **"you are here" marker** stands on the latest cleared map. When a newer map has been won since the screen last showed, the marker walks the leg to it as the screen opens.

**Paths.** Dashed legs join the visible nodes in campaign order: the route the Technician has worked. The leg from the marker to the frontier is drawn in the act accent. No leg leads anywhere the player can't see.

**The valley.** The chart shows the colony (Dome Row along its south wall, Admin Plaza at its heart, the east and north gates), the Terraces on the north slope, the Preserve's fenced basin to the west, Reclamation Facility 2 under the north-east cliff, and the Spires across the north with the rigs, Rig Nine deepest in the north-east. Beyond the Spires, Everglow's survey is blank. **The Long Walk** is drawn as an old trail under Everglow's roads, from the north edge through both colony gates and out across the south edge: the twist the paths tell ([maps.md §7](maps.md#7-storytelling-with-the-map)), in one picture. Everglow labels it a surge corridor; from A2M5 on, the label is corrected to the Long Walk. The Deep Halls are not named on the chart until a map has named them: A3M4's mail does ("Pebble, c/o the Deep Halls"), so their label joins over the Halls' mouth once A3M4 is won. **The north edge darkens act by act** (maps.md §7, the Hollow's edge), even before the Hollow reaches the colony.

**Palette.** The chart and the screen's UI theme follow the furthest act reached: the frontier's phase (Act II's gold from A2M1, its dusk from A2M4, Act III's night from A3M1, and dawn after the finale; the steps are art-direction §10's).

**The node panel.** One node is always selected, and its panel is open beside it: the shift number and place (the number alone when the place is the map's name), the map's name and state (*Cleared* or *Coming soon*; the frontier's Start says *Next up* for it), and its **tagline** (the italic line under the map's heading in its act file, verbatim). A map whose content is built adds its **threat intel** (each trait's glyph and name, and what it requires an answer to, as the briefing's strip shows them; §1's *Mission briefing*), and, one press away under a **Medals** toggle beside the button, its three **medals** by name (earned or not) and any optional objectives (§2.13). Then the button: **Start** on the frontier, **Replay** on a cleared map (Act I is replayed from its nodes). Either goes the way the Shift board's Start did: the loadout screen when there is a choice to make, then the briefing over the battle. A teaser shows its name, *Coming soon*, and its tagline only. Veteran modifiers join the panel with [progression.md §6](progression.md#6-veteran-modifiers-replays).

**Around the chart.** The header (top left: the slot, *Sector map*, the Technician's line), the banks (top right), and the footer (bottom right: Title, Settings, Base) keep to the screen's corners, as the HUD does; the menu notice shows under the header. **The first time a save opens the star map**, the chart is revealed by a survey sweep and a one-time notice says Perimeter Ops' access was upgraded.

**Keyboard.**
- On arrival the frontier is selected and its panel's Start is focused, so Enter plays the next map at once, as on the Shift board. A teaser frontier focuses the teaser's node; after the finale, Endless's Start on the post-game plate (below).
- The nodes are one tab stop (only the selected node is in the tab order). **Left and Up** select the previous visible node in campaign order, **Right and Down** the next, **Home** the first and **End** the frontier; there is no wrap. Focus moves with the selection and the panel follows.
- **Enter** or **Space** on a node (or a click) selects it and focuses its panel's button. **Esc** in the panel returns focus to its node; on a node it does nothing.
- Tab then reaches the panel's button and the footer, in that order.

**Screen readers.** The chart is an image with a one-line description; the region labels are hidden from screen readers. The nodes are a labelled list of buttons, each named with its shift, map, place, state, and medals (a teaser adds its tagline), and the marker's node is marked as the current location. The panel is a region named by its map. Maps not shown are not in the page at all.

**Reduced motion.** The frontier's beacon holds still, the marker doesn't bob or walk (it is simply at its node), and the first-visit sweep is skipped (the notice still shows).

**After the finale (the post-game star map; T863).** Every node is cleared and there is no frontier, so the screen leads with what's new and keeps the valley calm:
- **The chart** is the `dawn` phase, marked by C5's answer ([art-direction.md §10](../art/art-direction.md#10-the-star-map)): under **Stay** the colony's label is corrected, "Everglow Gardens" struck through above **Wayside**; under **Go** it keeps its old name. The legs are gone (the route is walked; the Long Walk's trail stays), the marker stands on the finale, and the medal and veteran pips are quieter.
- **The post-game plate** ("QUILL's sim deck", under the header, over its line: none of it is real, and nobody is counted) lists **Endless** (*The Long Shift*, with the save's best run) and each challenge map built so far (a cleared one with its medal pips), one row each under its kind, then **Watch the epilogue** (C5 answered). Selecting a row opens its panel above the plate, in one column with it, clear of the west nodes (on screen above, but after the plate in Tab order, so the keyboard meets the deck's rows before the open panel's seed and Start; a pick focuses the panel's Start): Endless's holds its tagline, the **seed** (a number the player can type to share a run, or "New seed"), the seed's best, and Start; a challenge's holds its ground, its rule as its tagline, its threats and required counters, its medals and what they pay (Intel) one press away, and Start or Replay. Esc in a panel returns to its row; a campaign node's panel works as before.
- **Arrival** selects Endless and focuses its Start. **Watch the epilogue** plays the epilogue again with Esc allowed, then returns here.
- **An Endless run** plays its seed's generated waves ([maps.md §8](maps.md#8-post-game-maps)). The HUD's wave counter drops its "/N", and the preview adds the block's line: "Warm-up", or "Needs" and the block's counters, and in a block's last two waves "Then" and what the next block brings. The run ends on the **Endless score screen** in the results card's place: "{n} waves held.", the seed with its best (or "New best."), the save's best runs once they hold another seed (this seed's row marked, its best not said twice), and **Run it again** (the same seed), **New seed**, and the way back.

## 2. In-map loop

### 2.1 Phases

1. **Setup.** The map opens paused-in-spirit: no countdown runs until the player starts wave 1. The player gets the map's starting credits and builds freely. Dialogue may play.
2. **Wave.** Enemies spawn from spawn points along their ground paths or air lanes. Towers fire automatically. The player builds, upgrades, sells, retargets, and uses abilities.
3. **Countdown.** When a wave has *finished spawning*, the next wave's countdown starts (default **20 s**; Act I 25 s, Act III 15 s). When it reaches zero the next wave starts. The player can **call the wave early** at any point during the countdown (2.4). **Exception:** a wave that waits for the boss (`countdownAfter: 'bossEnded'`, A1M5's Quiet Wave) starts its countdown only once the boss fight has ended (the boss driven off, or gone), so it walks in after the fight, never during it.
4. **Repeat** until the last wave resolves. **The finale's Phase 2 waves** (W19–26) belong to their script phase (`inPhase`): each waits, never called, until Phase 2 begins, and once Dawn's phase passes them no wave starts again ([maps.md §5.5](maps.md#55-the-leaving-song-finale-rules)).
5. **End.** Victory when every wave has resolved (below), so every enemy of the last wave and of any earlier wave still on the field has been killed or has leaked, and core integrity is above 0. Defeat the instant core integrity hits 0; it wins over a victory on the same tick. **The finale is won by its script** (`winBy: 'script'`): 8 s into Dawn, never by its waves resolving, so Dawn holds the field ([maps.md §5.5](maps.md#55-the-leaving-song-finale-rules)).

Waves can overlap: the countdown starts once spawning ends, not when the wave is dead, so slow kills stack pressure. **Rule:** a wave is *resolved* when every enemy in it has died or leaked. The wave clear bonus (2.3) pays out on resolution.

### 2.2 Wave preview

Before each wave, a **wave preview** shows the incoming enemy icons, grouped by type with counts, and marks which spawn and lane each group uses (ground or air). Pulsing "incoming" flags sit at the active spawn points. New enemy types show a "NEW" badge and a one-line codex hint the first time they appear ("Shielded: Energy damage breaks shields fast"). A neutral unit isn't a pest: Gerald's row reads "MAIL" with the hold-fire glyph, never "NEW" or "AIR". On an Escort, a friendly "+6 walking" chip shows the refugees the next wave sends walking (the HUD detail is [../tech/architecture.md § UI](../tech/architecture.md#ui-dom-overlay)). **Rule:** the preview never lies.

**One scripted exception: QUILL lockout (A3M2).** After Sterling orders QUILL to lock you out, the preview shows "ACCESS RESTRICTED" instead of icons for the rest of the map. It hides; it never shows anything false. To keep the map fair, QUILL **leaks the key trait of every remaining wave** through a mandatory script-cue bark at the start of each countdown ("I am not permitted to tell you wave six is armored. Ahem."), and the briefing's threat intel (shown before the lockout) covers every trait on the map. The countdown and the call button stay; the leak line shows under "ACCESS RESTRICTED" (in the act's alarm red beside the padlock, over a dashed blank where the icons were), under QUILL's name in its teal, until its wave starts ([../tech/story-runtime.md § Map scripts](../tech/story-runtime.md#map-scripts-story-driven-sim-changes)).

### 2.3 Credits (the in-map currency)

Credits exist only inside a map and reset every map (D001).

| Source | Formula / value | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Starting credits | Per map data (`startCredits`). | Per-act ranges: [maps.md §6.1](maps.md#61-per-act-targets). |
| Kill bounty | Per enemy data (`bounty`). Nibbler 3, Chonk 12, bosses 0 (bosses pay through the wave bonus). | Paid when the enemy dies. Leaked enemies pay nothing. Fleeing Skitterkin pay only 1 ([enemies.md § Fleeing Skitterkin](enemies.md#fleeing-skitterkin)). |
| Wave clear bonus | `20 + 4 × waveNumber` (wave 1 = 24); ×3 for a boss wave (one that sends a boss). | Paid when the wave resolves, even if some of it leaked. A driven-off boss's wave resolves when she and her brood are off the map ([enemies.md §6.1](enemies.md#61-boss-scripting)). |
| Early wave call | `round(0.5 × nextWaveClearBonus × secondsRemaining / countdown)` | See 2.4. |
| Wreckage salvage | Per wreckage tile in map data (30–80). | See [maps.md §3](maps.md#3-tile-types). |
| Salvage Crane | Tower effect. | See [towers.md](towers.md#vrell-towers). |

| Sink | Rule |
|---|---|
| Build a tower | Pays its tier-1 cost ([towers.md §2](towers.md#2-roster-overview)). |
| Upgrade | Pays the next tier's cost. Tiers 1 to 3 are linear; tier 4 is a specialization choice ([towers.md §3](towers.md#3-upgrades-and-specializations)). |
| Sell | **Refund 70%** of everything spent on that tower (shop upgrades can raise this to 80%). **Misclick grace (rule):** a tower sold before it first fires and within **10 s** of sim time of being built (whichever ends first) refunds 100%. Paused time doesn't count toward the 10 s. For pulse and aura towers, "first fires" means the first pulse that affects an enemy. |

**Rule:** there is no interest on banked credits by default (one Vrell specialization adds a small amount; see [towers.md](towers.md#vrell-towers)).

### 2.4 Early wave call

During a countdown, the player can press the incoming flag (or a hotkey) to start the next wave now.

- **Bonus credits:** `round(0.5 × nextWaveClearBonus × secondsRemaining / countdown)`, where `nextWaveClearBonus` is the clear bonus of the wave being called (2.3; for a boss wave, the plain bonus, never the tripled one: the triple rewards the fight, not calling it early) and `countdown` is that countdown's full length. Skipping a whole countdown pays half that wave's clear bonus: calling wave 5 the instant its 20 s countdown starts pays `0.5 × 40 = +20`. The bonus scales with the map's own economy, so it can't dominate early maps.
- **Ability cooldowns:** every commander ability's remaining cooldown drops by `0.25 × secondsRemaining`.
- You can call several waves back to back; each call pays its own bonus.
- **Rule:** the player cannot call wave 1 early (there is no countdown before it). Starting wave 1 is free and pays nothing.
- **Rule:** the Quiet Wave (any group tagged `quiet_wave`) can never be called early: the call is refused and shows no bonus, so the player always sees it walk in before choosing (§2.7). A wave may also be marked `noCall` for the same treatment when its build phase is a scripted fight (A2M5's W6: the Penitent's 60 s; D138).
- **Rule (no degenerate rush):** the balance harness runs an `alwaysCallEarly` policy (par build, every wave called the instant its countdown starts). Its margin must be ≤ `min(bandTop, parMargin + 10)`: it may not leave the map's par band, and it may not beat the normal par run by more than 10 points ([maps.md §6.4](maps.md#64-balance-harness)).

### 2.5 Building, upgrading, selling

- Towers go only on **buildable tiles**: pads, high ground, and power nodes. **Never on paths** (hard rule). See [maps.md §3](maps.md#3-tile-types). How the player picks a tower and a tile is in §4.
- One tower per tile; every tower has a 1×1 footprint.
- Building, upgrading, selling, retargeting, and choosing specializations are all **allowed while paused** (accessibility, and it rewards planning over reflexes).
- A new tower **deploys** for 0.5 s before it can fire (a drop-pod or build animation). Upgrades apply instantly.

### 2.6 Targeting priorities

Every attacking tower has a targeting priority the player can change at any time: **First** (furthest along its route), **Last**, **Strong** (highest current HP + shield), **Close** (nearest). Defaults and tie-breaks are in [towers.md §5](towers.md#5-targeting). From A2M1 the **Spare runners** filter ([§2.7](#spare-runners)) can sit on top of any priority. The **Sproing Pad** is the exception: its leapers pick for themselves (a flyer, then a Marked foe, then a free one, then the first; [towers.md](towers.md)), so its panel's Target button is disabled and reads **Flyers first**, and T says so in a notice rather than cycling.

### 2.7 Hold Fire

The player can order any tower, or all towers at once, to **hold fire**. A holding tower keeps its auras and links but does not attack. Commander abilities still fire if the player uses them. **Rule:** Hold Fire must always be available, because the story depends on it: the player must be able to *choose* not to shoot.

- **One tower or all.** A tower's panel toggle holds that tower. The HUD's Hold Fire (all) toggle is a master switch: on, it holds every tower, and towers built while it's on start holding; off, it releases every tower, including ones held one by one. A released tower can fire at once (its reload kept running while it held).
- **What the player sees.** The tower panel's toggle reads "Hold fire", then "Holding fire" while on. Hold Fire (all) sits bottom-left beside the ability slots on every map, and reads "Hold", then "Held" while the master switch is on. A holding tower dims and wears a small pause plate above it, so it reads by shape, not by color alone ([../art/art-direction.md §8](../art/art-direction.md#status-visuals)). H with no tower selected says how: "Pick a turret first · Shift+H holds all". Each change clicks: down for on, up for off.
- **Taught in A1M3** by Gerald the mail drone, who flies along the path as a **neutral unit**: towers that aren't holding fire target him like an enemy (wasting their shots while real pests slip by), he takes no damage (shots plink off), and each hit plays a QUILL bark ("Do NOT shoot Gerald"). The HUD's Hold Fire (all) button pulses until the player uses it. **The second lesson** is one turret at a time: on Gerald's second pass, which Bricks asks to be held on the Rivets and Skyhooks only, the hint reads "Gerald is the mail. Hold fire: H one, Shift+H all" until a single turret holds fire ([../tech/architecture.md § UI](../tech/architecture.md#ui-dom-overlay)). Gerald never leaks, pays nothing, and can't make the map lost: at the end of the path he delivers and leaves. He flies the same way once more in A3M4.
  - **His layer (rule):** Gerald is an **air** unit whose route is the **ground path**, so the towers the player built along the path see him, but only those that hit air (Rivet and Skyhook in A1M3) can shoot him; Thumpers and Sprinklers can't.
  - **What counts as a hit (rule):** only a shot a tower *aimed at him*: a hitscan shot, or a shell or flak burst fired at him that lands with him inside. Area hits aimed at someone else (a flak burst at a Puffwing beside him, a pulse, a chain's jump) pass him by and never count, so the Return to Sender medal measures the player's choice to hold fire, not bad luck. A burst fired at him still hurts the pests around him.
- **Story tracking.** The sim records per run: total ticks with any tower holding fire, the ticks since any tower last fired at a Skitterkin while one was in range of a tower (for the "not firing" bark, §6.3), and, for every **wave tag** ([enemies.md §7](enemies.md#7-wave-design-rules)), how many tagged units spawned, leaked, and were killed by the player.
- **C1, the Quiet Wave (A1M5), is tracked silently.** The flag `spared_quiet_wave` is set at map end if **Old Thrum** (the `quiet_wave` unit that isn't a youngling) leaked **and at least 75%** of the younglings leaked (tuning value; the share, not the count, so it survives wave-size tuning): spared means Thrum lived, so holding fire only after she falls never counts. **The choice is seen before it's made:** the wave's countdown starts only after the Matriarch is driven off (§2.1), it can't be called early (§2.4), and no buildable tile's best tier-3 range reaches the first 4 tiles of its route (content validation `[quiet-wave-reach]`, which counts only the towers owned on the run that first wins the map, the only run that sets the flag: their best tier-3 range, and the best range aura a support tower beside the tile could add. The Lookout Shack, stocked after A1M5, and the Grapple Harpoon, stocked after A2M1, aren't counted on A1M5), so it walks into view before any tower could fire. Nothing on screen acknowledges it in A1M5 except Bricks's line and Sterling's story-only memo and results stamp ([../story/bible.md §9](../story/bible.md#c1-the-quiet-wave-a1m5-implicit)). **Rules:** (1) sparing it can never lose the map: a Quiet Wave leak never takes the core below 1 (the sim caps it at integrity − 1 before Emergency Bulkhead absorbs anything, and charges it after every other leak on the same tick, so a pest leaking alongside it can't turn sparing into a loss), for every player; the map must be winnable on Standard by the par build even if every Quiet Wave unit leaks, and the harness's Hold Fire variant must end with `spared_quiet_wave = true` ([maps.md §6.4](maps.md#64-balance-harness)); (2) Quiet Wave leaks never cost a medal (medal exemption below); (3) the wave's integrity cost is small (≤ 2, [enemies.md §5.1](enemies.md#51-skitterkin-body-organic)).
- **`held_fire_second_look` (A2M3)** is set at map end when **at least 50%** of the map's `fleeing` units leaked **and** Hold Fire was turned on at least once during the run.
- **Medal-exempt units (rule).** Units in groups tagged `quiet_wave` or `fleeing`, units with the `youngling` modifier ([enemies.md §5.1](enemies.md#51-skitterkin-body-organic)), and any group tagged `medal_exempt` are **medal-exempt**: their leaks cost only the integrity their data says (0 for younglings and fleeing units; Old Thrum's 2), never break Perfect Core, and no Challenge medal can reference them. Any other leak breaks Perfect Core the moment it happens, even one that costs no integrity (a leak cost of 0, or Emergency Bulkhead absorbing it): the medal means *nothing got through*. How the sim settles medals is in [../tech/architecture.md § Medals](../tech/architecture.md#medals).
- **Allies hold fire too (rule).** Scripted allies with an attack (Bricks's post, colonist militia) **never target** those same medal-exempt units ([maps.md §5.4](maps.md#54-allies-on-the-map)), so sparing them is always the player's choice alone. In A1M5, right after "Clear it, Sprout," Bricks visibly **lowers his cannon** for the Quiet Wave (a silent tell, no line).
- The same single-button grammar returns for the forced inputs in §2.11.

#### Spare runners

From **A2M1**, when the first fleeing Skitterkin appear, the player can tell towers to **spare runners**: a toggle in each attacking tower's panel, plus a global HUD toggle (hotkey **Alt+H**) that sets it on every tower.

- **Hidden until it unlocks** (with A1M5 won; [../tech/meta-runtime.md § Progress and gating](../tech/meta-runtime.md#progress-and-gating)): the panel toggle ("Spare runners", then "Sparing runners") shows only on towers with an attack, the HUD's Spare runners (all) button sits beside Hold Fire (all), and Alt+H does nothing before then.
- **Rule:** while it is on, a tower never **picks** a `fleeing`-tagged unit or a `youngling` unit as its primary target. It takes the best other target by its priority (§2.6), or waits if there is none. Its attacks are otherwise unchanged, so splash, chains, and multi-target hits can still catch those units. A pulse tower, which picks no target, waits while only spared units are in range, and still catches them when it pulses for another unit. Abilities are unaffected. The global toggle works like Hold Fire's: towers built while it's on start with it on.
- **Why (rule):** sparing must cost nothing in play, not only in the leak and medal rules. The balance harness proves it: the `spareFleeing` policy (the par build with the filter on for every tower) must end with a margin ≥ par margin − 5 ([maps.md §6.4](maps.md#64-balance-harness)). Fleeing units pay a bounty of only 1 ([enemies.md § Fleeing Skitterkin](enemies.md#fleeing-skitterkin)), so the filter gives up almost nothing.
- **Plain Hold Fire is unchanged.** It stops all fire, and it is still the only tool for C1 in A1M5, where the filter doesn't exist yet. The filter is taught in A2M1 by one short line in voice ([../story/campaign/act2.md § A2M1](../story/campaign/act2.md#a2m1--rig-nine)).
- The filter is a sim command (`spareRunners`, separate from Hold Fire), so it is in the command log and the harness can set it ([../tech/architecture.md § Commands](../tech/architecture.md#commands-input-into-the-sim)).

### 2.8 Speed and pause

- Speeds: **1x** and **2x**. Pause at any time (hotkey Space).
- **The pause menu** (Esc with nothing to cancel, or the HUD's Menu button) freezes play under **Resume**, **Retry shift** (the same map and loadout, minus the briefing), and **Quit** (to the base, or the Shift board before it opens). Leaving mid-map ends the run as a **loss** ([../tech/meta-runtime.md § Run end](../tech/meta-runtime.md#run-end)): its Pests Processed count, a map not yet won pays the defeat stipend for the waves resolved (no more than losing there would), and no medal or objective is recorded. No Results card shows.
- **Rule:** all timers (cooldowns, countdowns, effects) run on simulation time, so 2x halves real-world waits and pause freezes everything. The sim runs a fixed timestep; 2x doubles the rate at which the host's tick accumulator fills, so twice as many fixed ticks run per real second ([../tech/architecture.md § Game speed](../tech/architecture.md#game-speed-pause-and-the-host-loop)).
- **3x** unlocks once the finale is won (the post-game; T863): the HUD adds a 3x button, and F steps 1x → 2x → 3x → 1x (before then, 1x and 2x). Speed only changes how many fixed ticks run per real frame, so a run is the same at any speed (`app/hostSpeed.test.ts`).

### 2.9 Core integrity (lose condition)

- Each map has a **colony core** at the end of its paths. Core integrity starts at **20** (shop upgrades add up to +3; see [progression.md §4](progression.md#4-zibzabs-shop-stardust)).
- A leaking enemy subtracts its **leak cost** (`leak` in enemy data): 1 for small enemies, 2–4 for big ones, and **0** for fleeing Skitterkin and younglings (§2.7). **Rule:** a leaking boss drains all remaining integrity (Emergency Bulkhead can't absorb it); on an Escort map, a boss reaching the safe exit is a defeat ([maps.md §5.2](maps.md#52-objective-variants)).
- **Rule:** integrity never regenerates during a map (a Field Protocol may grant a one-time shield; see [progression.md §5](progression.md#5-field-protocols-meta-perks)).
- At 0 integrity the map is lost immediately. Per D001, a loss has no penalty beyond a retry; the player keeps a share of the Stardust earned ([progression.md §2](progression.md#2-earning-stardust)).
- **Meter labels are map data** (`meterLabel`). Defend maps show **Core integrity**. Escort maps show **Evacuated** (refugees reaching safety) or **Sheltered** (A3M6, refugees reaching the domes). Holdout shows **Evacuated**. The finale shows **Voices** (`meterLabel: 'voices'`, the singers still singing; §2.10). The results-screen counter line follows the same label (§6.2).

### 2.10 Objective variants

Most maps are **Defend** maps (survive every wave with integrity above 0). A few story maps swap the objective. Each variant is a map-data flag, and [maps.md §5](maps.md#5-map-modifiers-and-objective-variants) defines them:

- **Escort:** friendly refugees walk the path *toward safety* while Hollow units chase or intercept them. The meter is relabeled **Evacuated** (or **Sheltered**) and counts refugees who made it, of the map's total; the HUD shows it in the core meter's place, with "of {total}" under it and a **Lost** row: one pip per loss the map allows (`maxLost`), filled red as refugees are lost (words, "3/20", with no `maxLost` or more than 16), the blinking alarm box once a quarter of the allowed losses or fewer are left (the `low_integrity` bark's threshold), and a red flash on each refugee lost. When a map **pivots** into Escort, the relabel is a moment: first the old meter whole, as it was (the core's glyph, "Core" and its last integrity, never "Core 0"; no "of {total}", no pips) for 0.5 s, then every row of the Escort's at once, the new label blinking its accent edge three times (1.5 s). Both are held while a pausing scene is up (A2M5's pivot cue plays over the old meter), so the swap plays when control returns; under reduced motion nothing blinks: the old meter holds 2 s, then the new label shows in a steady accent for 2 s. All pips full is the loss: the map is lost when the refugees lost reach `maxLost`. An enemy reaching the safe exit costs a sheltered refugee, not integrity ([maps.md §5.2](maps.md#52-objective-variants) has the rules). An escort can keep an earlier map's route and change only where it leads: A3M6 walks A1M1's road north to south as before, but the gate at the end now opens into Dome 1 (the Skitterkin are welcome inside). **Only the finale reverses a path** (south to north, going home). A map can **pivot** into Escort mid-map (A2M5).
- **Holdout:** a deliberately unwinnable defense that ends on a **scripted event, not on integrity 0** (A3M3 ends on the SEAL). It is scored by Evacuated, always counts as completed, never blocks progress, and pays a flat reward ([progression.md §2](progression.md#2-earning-stardust)). As built (T605), it ends as a win on the SEAL's press, whatever the score, and an enemy at a door takes a sheltered refugee as on an Escort ([maps.md §5.2](maps.md#52-objective-variants)). **Its HUD (T631):** the Escort's meter in the core's place, "Evacuated" of the run's refugees (only the chosen door's groups: `state.refugeeTotal`, as every meter's total is), with **no Lost row**: a Holdout has no defeat, so nothing counts down to one. Each refugee lost still flashes the meter, and once the losses reach the low mark (`holdoutLow`, the `low_integrity` bark's: A3M3's 9 of 36) the blinking alarm box sits beside the label.
- **Protect the asset:** a second, smaller objective (for example the A2M2 extraction rigs or the A3M5 vats) with its own meter that shares the map's waves. Units on its branch routes leak into it instead of the core, by their leak value (a boss drains it); either meter at 0 loses the map. The HUD shows its meter under the core's, labelled with its name, flashing on each hit and showing the low-asset alarm from half; Results lists it beside Core integrity. Perfect Core on a Protect map means neither was damaged; where the asset is the bearers (A3M5) the medal is named plain **Perfect** ("Win with the core and the bearers unhurt."), as act3.md lists it. The bearers' meter wears the refugee glyph and, under its label, **"Carried n of total"**: the bearers home so far, of the run's (Results' "Carried" line, live). The rules are in [maps.md §5.2](maps.md#52-objective-variants).
- **Choir escort (finale):** an Escort whose refugees are the singing choir, plus the song rules (laid to rest, scattering, the countdown) in [maps.md §5.5](maps.md#55-the-leaving-song-finale-rules). **Its HUD (T832)** shows only what each phase needs, in the slots the HUD already has: **Voices** in the Escort meter's place (the singers singing "of" the run's choir, and a **Hushed** row of pips, one per hush the choir can take before its floor, so all full is the loss), the colony **Gate**'s integrity on the core's row under it, and HALTED on the meter while the choir is halted. One top-centre clock under the boss bar carries what runs against the player: the **Deep Bore**'s fill in Phase 1, then in Phase 2 the **countdown** ("Pests processed", falling as the song lays the Hollow to rest, its gold bar the rest progress). A wave held for its phase reads "Held", not a countdown; phase banners ("The Descent", then "The Halls" over "The Leaving Song") replace the wave banner, and Phase 2's add waves get none. Under Keep, Directive 0 darks Time Dilation's slot under a DIRECTIVE 0 tag (§6.11). At **Dawn** the HUD steps back to the name plate and the counter at 0. The look is [../art/art-direction.md §9](../art/art-direction.md#look-a-sci-fi-tactical-hud) (*Finale HUD*).

### 2.12 Checkpoints

From **Act II** on, a lost run can be resumed from a checkpoint instead of from wave 1.

- **When.** The game records a checkpoint as the **countdown to every 5th wave begins** (W5, W10, W15, …), so a retry lands in the build phase before that wave; how it is stored and restored is in [../tech/architecture.md § Checkpoints](../tech/architecture.md#checkpoints). On a loss, Results offers **"Retry from wave N"** for the latest checkpoint taken before the run's final tick, first and focused, beside Retry (from wave 1): a run lost on the very tick W10's countdown began offers W5, since W10's would replay straight into the loss (D134). The run resumes exactly as it stood: the towers, credits, core and asset meters, cooldowns, timers, and the earlier waves' enemies still walking. It opens **paused** ("Resumed before wave N. Unpause when ready. Perfect Core and the Challenge need a shift from wave 1.", naming the map's Perfect medal as its card does: "Perfect" on an Escort or the finale's choir; for 6 s, a two-line banner centred below the HUD's top button row, clear of the corner panels; "before", because the wave counter still shows the waves behind it), so the player can look and rebuild first. A retry whose replay can't reach its checkpoint still playing never strands the player: it lands on the campaign screen with "That checkpoint couldn't be restored. The shift is safe to play again from wave 1." Nothing said or heard before the checkpoint plays again (lines, barks, banners), and a later loss offers the latest checkpoint again.
- **Only after a loss (rule).** The pause menu's Retry stays a full retry from wave 1: a checkpoint recovers a lost run, it is not a way to rewind a wave that went badly.
- **Finale.** The finale also records a checkpoint at the **start of Phase 2** (towers, credits, Voices, and Deep Bore %), so a Phase 2 loss offers **"Retry from the Halls"** and never replays the Descent; its cue reads "Checkpoint · The Halls", and the retry opens "Resumed at the Halls." (D154).
- **The Long Walk survives a closed tab (finale only, D154).** The finale's latest checkpoint (a wave's or the Halls') is kept in the save as it is taken. Closed mid-walk, the Title offers **"Resume the Long Walk"** above Continue: the run picks up paused at that checkpoint ("The Long Walk resumes at the Halls. Unpause when ready."), as the **same run**, not a retry, so its medals are still to play for (unless it already was a retry). A save that changed since (a purchase, a setting) can't pick it up: the Shift board says so and the walk starts again from the gate. Any end of the finale run, abandoning it, or starting the finale fresh drops it. Every other map's checkpoints last only for the session.
- **Cost (rule):** a run that used a checkpoint can still earn **Victory**, the first-clear rewards, and its optional objectives (§2.13; story content, never a medal), but **forfeits Perfect Core and the Challenge medal** for that run. The HUD's medal tracker shows them forfeited from the resume, and the Results card says why.
- **Pests Processed:** each attempt counts its own kills (§6.2), so a retry counts only the kills since the checkpoint; the ones before it were filed by the attempt that reached it.
- **No farming (rule):** a defeat after a checkpoint retry pays defeat Stardust only for the waves resolved **in this attempt beyond the checkpoint**, and a defeat on a map already won pays **0** ([progression.md §2](progression.md#2-earning-stardust)).
- Act I and the A3M3 Holdout (which has no defeat) have no checkpoints.

### 2.13 Optional objectives

A few story maps carry an **optional objective** worth +1 Intel (Objective Intel, [progression.md §3](progression.md#3-medals-and-intel); 7 across the campaign). It is never moral and never required: it rewards playing a certain way, like a Challenge medal, and uses the same condition grammar (map data `objectives`, [maps.md §5.3](maps.md#53-map-scripts)).

- **Standing.** Each objective is **open** until it settles. It is **done** the moment its condition can no longer fail (a counter reaching its target), or at victory if it still holds then; it **fails** the moment it can no longer hold (Clean Audit's first late sell) or when the map is lost. The HUD's tracker, a box per objective beside the medals on the status panel's last row, shows each one's standing as it changes; its name and what completes it are its tooltip and one press away under the row's "+" (D153's declutter, T803b). An objective that follows a refugee group (`groupLost`: A3M1's The Matriarch's Young, Bramble's column) also shows how many of that group are on the road, in the "walking" chip's green ("+6"), while they walk (T631).
- **Completion actions.** An objective may run script actions the moment it is done (A3M5's "Cut the Feed" shrinks a later wave).
- **Payout.** A won run pays each done objective's Intel **once per save**, like a medal; a loss pays none. Results lists it under the medals.
- **Late sells** (A1M4's **Clean Audit**). A tower's build phase lasts until the next wave starts after it was built: in that countdown it can be sold and rebuilt freely. A sale once a wave has started since the build is a **late sell**. A misclick-grace refund (§2.3) is never a sale.

## 3. Commander abilities

Enemies can never attack towers, so these abilities are the player's direct way to act on the battlefield. They give agency, spectacle, and an answer to a crisis.

### 3.1 Rules

- **Two ability slots per map:**
  - **Slot 1, Technician:** the player's own ability, chosen in the loadout from those unlocked ([progression.md §7](progression.md#7-rd-tree-intel)).
  - **Slot 2, Companion:** the signature ability of the NPC who accompanies this mission, shown with their portrait. [campaign.md](../story/campaign.md) sets the companion per map. The companion also voices in-map barks and between-wave radio ([../story/style-guide.md §5.5](../story/style-guide.md#55-barks)).
- **No companion:** on a map with no companion the slot is **hidden**, not empty, and there is no fallback ability. The one exception is deliberate (next rule).
- **The finale pick list.** In the finale the player picks who walks with them in the loadout screen ("Walking with you", in slot 2's place): Ves'thara (Stillness, the default), Bramble (Underfoot), Zibzab (Clearance Sale), or QUILL (Time Dilation; not offered under C4 Purge). The pick is remembered for the finale (a remembered QUILL falls back to Ves'thara once Purged). The balance harness runs the par build with each companion ([maps.md §6.4](maps.md#64-balance-harness)).
- **Slot 1 never doubles slot 2 (T501).** The Technician can't equip the ability the companion brings: on the loadout its card dims and says why ("QUILL brings it", "Bricks brings it"), and picking that companion moves slot 1 to the first ability that doesn't clash.
- **The empty slot (A3M4).** After Bricks dies, A3M4's map data sets the companion to Bricks in the **`absent`** state: the slot is visible, his portrait is dark (the art's `dark` portrait variant), the button is disabled and has no label, and his radio and bark slots play authored **silence** ([../story/style-guide.md §5.6](../story/style-guide.md#56-special-line-kinds-authoring-conventions)).
- **Bricks has no hero unit.** His presence is the companion slot (Suppressing Fire, portrait, barks) and his turret name tags ([towers.md §8](towers.md#8-presentation-and-personality)). On A1M1, A1M5, A2M6, and the A3M3 Holdout he also appears as a **scripted ally unit** on the map: a person, not a tower, so the 4-tower rule is untouched ([maps.md §5.4](maps.md#54-allies-on-the-map)). The player never places or moves him.
- **Inheritance.** At the end of A3M4, **Suppressing Fire** becomes a Technician ability, equippable in slot 1 from A3M5. Under C4 Keep, **Time Dilation** is also equippable in slot 1 from A3M5 through finale Phase 1 (§6.11). The loadout's Technician card names whose it was ("Time Dilation · QUILL's", "Suppressing Fire · Bricks's").
- Abilities are **ready when wave 1 starts**, then go on cooldown after each use.
- Cooldowns run on sim time and shrink with early wave calls (2.4).
- Targeted abilities can be **aimed while paused**; they fire when play resumes (or instantly if cast during play).
- Abilities follow the same damage rules as towers ([enemies.md §2](enemies.md#2-combat-resolution)). Bosses take reduced control effects as defined there.

**Suppressing Fire's segment** is the clicked path tile and one tile either side of it along the route ([decisions.md D031](../decisions.md#d031--2026-11-09--m2-scope-the-m2-cut)), on every route through that tile, shifted inward at a spawn or core so it always covers 3 tiles. It must target a path tile. Its slow is Chilled 50% (the strongest chill wins, so it doesn't stack with a Sprinkler), and its damage lands in beam ticks every 0.25 s, so armor blunts it per second as it does a beam ([enemies.md §2.3](enemies.md#23-damage-pipeline)): it is a crowd-control tool, not an armor answer. It hits every ground unit on the segment, quiet-wave units included: it is the player's cast, not the ally's. Air units are never hit, even one flying the ground path (Gerald), and neither is a unit out of reach (burrowed, Phased, or cloaked and not Revealed): no slow, no damage.

**Time Dilation** slows the whole map, not just what is on it at the cast: a unit that spawns during the 6 s is slowed too, and so is one burrowed, Phased, or cloaked (it is map-wide, not aimed). Its slow is a Chilled 40%, so the strongest chill wins (a stronger Sprinkler or Suppressing Fire chill still applies), and a boss takes it at half strength by the boss rule ([enemies.md § 6](enemies.md#6-bosses)), which is the 20% in the table. Gerald and other neutral units aren't enemies, so it passes them by.

**Stillness** (T507; [`src/sim/abilities/stillness.ts`](../../src/sim/abilities/stillness.ts)) may land on any tile and is instant: on the cast's tick, every unit whose centre is within 2.5 tiles of the tile's centre, ground or air, goes into Stasis for 3 s by the Stasis rules ([enemies.md §2.4](enemies.md#24-status-effects): none on a unit already in Stasis or immune, 6 s of immunity after; a boss takes Chilled 50% instead). It never reaches a unit out of reach (burrowed, Phased, leaving, or cloaked and not Revealed) or a neutral unit. On screen a pale blue disc covers the circle and fades over the 3 s; aiming previews that circle (its pale blue ring over a faint disc at its real radius, held still on the hovered tile), not the strike's reticle.

**Underfoot** (T604; [`src/sim/abilities/underfoot.ts`](../../src/sim/abilities/underfoot.ts)) must target a tile of a ground route (a path or a rift lane), like Suppressing Fire; off the road the cast is refused and starts no cooldown. It lays a trap on that one tile for 6 s from the cast. Each tick, after the units move, every ground unit whose centre is on the tile springs it, once per unit per trap: 30 Kinetic (armor applies, as a strike's does), then Pinned 1 s by the Pin rules ([enemies.md §2.4](enemies.md#24-status-effects): none on a unit already pinned or immune; a boss takes Chilled 50% instead). It reaches a burrowed unit (a Pin holds it underground), but never a flyer (Gerald on the ground path included), a neutral unit, or a Phased, leaving, or cloaked-out one. On screen a moss-green ring holds on the tile and blinks through its last second, and each spring bursts a green ring from it. **Aiming it** (T631) takes ground-route tiles only, as Suppressing Fire's does: the hint reads "Pick a path tile", a ghost of the snare lies at 80% on the road tile under the pointer or the tile cursor, and off the road the hover turns red and shows none, so a click there never reaches the sim.

**Scanner Sweep** (T406) may land on any tile. It opens a 4-tile circle that lives 10 s from the cast and reveals every cloaked unit standing in it on each tick (a unit that walks out cloaks again a tick later; a detector's linger is the Lookout's, not the Sweep's). On screen it is a green ring at its real radius, pinged open, fading over its last second; aiming previews the circle. It never hurts anything.

**Clearance Sale** loads 3 charges at 40% off. Each **purchase** (a build, an upgrade, or a tier-4 specialization) that still costs credits spends one charge, whenever it happens, and the charges last until spent. The sale applies after every other price change (tune-ups, Specialist): 40% off the price you would have paid, rounded to the nearest credit. Rapid Deploy's free first build spends no charge. A tower's sell value follows what was actually paid, so a discounted tower refunds 70% of its discounted price. A new cast (only possible through Ready Room) replaces the charges left rather than adding to them. "Once per 4 waves" counts **wave starts after the cast**: cast during wave 1 and it is ready again as wave 5 starts. Zibzab's Companion Rapport (R&D, after A1M4; [progression.md §7.3](progression.md#73-command-abilities-and-protocols)) makes it 3 waves with its −15% (cooldowns in waves round to the nearest wave; Ability Coolant's −5% alone leaves it at 4).

**Revocations are the betrayal you feel.** Losing Orbital Strike and a tower class in A3M2 is attributable to Sterling on screen (her portrait in the cue, an Everglow "REVOKED" stamp), so the loss reads as hers, not as a bug.

**The armed-tower rule.** A battle starts with nothing armed. A build slot (click, or 1–4) arms its tower, and pressing it again disarms. A locked slot doesn't arm and says when it ships ("Thumper ships at wave 3"); an unaffordable one says "Not enough credits". With a tower armed, clicking a free pad builds it, and the slot **stays armed while another copy is affordable**, so several can go down in a row; clicking any other tile says "Can't build there" (on a standing wreck, "Click it (no tower armed) to send the drone") and keeps the slot armed. Selecting a tower, aiming an ability, Esc, or right-click disarms. Until the first build, slot 1 and the free pads pulse to show where to start.

**The tile cursor (keyboard-only play).** The first arrow key shows a cursor on the playfield (on the selected tower, else where it was last, else on the pad nearest the map's middle), and each arrow moves it to the nearest pad or tower in that direction on screen; while an ability is aimed it moves over every tile instead, so a strike can land on the road (only the path for Suppressing Fire; aiming it, E, snaps a showing cursor straight onto the nearest path tile). It also lands on a wreck, on a clickable world object while it's clickable, and, with nothing armed, on the Act I Peeker's rock. Enter does what a click on that tile would: build the armed tower on a free pad, select a tower, fire the aimed ability, send the drone, reach the clickable object, or poke the Peeker. The ghost, range ring, and reticle follow the cursor, and a screen reader hears the tile and what stands on it (a clickable object by its map name, "The Peeker's rock, column 10, row 7. Enter reaches for it."; the Act I rock as "A rock … Enter pokes it."), that a clickable object is within reach as it turns clickable, and what H, Shift+H, T, and a cast just did ("Rivet holding fire.", "Targeting: Last.", "Orbital Strike fired."; D059). Moving the mouse hides it. The exact rule is in [architecture.md § Rendering](../tech/architecture.md#rendering) (*Input*).

Every control is a hit target of at least **16×16 game px** (32×32 screen px at the 2× scale) so touch support can come later (D001). Key remapping is a backlog item.

## 5. Readability and feedback rules

- Every enemy ability has a **visual tell** before it fires and a **codex entry** naming its counter ([enemies.md](enemies.md)).
- Damage numbers are optional (off by default). A hit that does reduced damage because of armor or shields shows a distinct "plink" spark and sound, so the player learns "wrong tool".
- The tower panel shows live DPS, kills, and the active **synergy links** and tile bonuses. A **beam** reads as damage per second (its per-tick damage times its ticks, never damage × rate) with its ramp ("12 dmg/s · range 3 · ramp ×4 in 6 s"), and a live **Ramp** row fills from ×1 to the cap while a beam holds its target. At **Sundown** the stat line shows the darkened range, and a line under the bonuses says why: "-15% range (dark)" for an unlit tower, "Lit: full range" for a lit one while the map is dark. Spec cards name what sets them apart (D059): "3 beams", "echo 50% · 1.2 tiles", "Unnerves 1.5 s", "Mark +35% · no evading", "Marks all in 3.5 +15%", and what a choice gives up against tier 3 (Truthlight's "1 beam"; D138). At Sundown a tower the dark can't dim (no attack, as the Lookout and the Crane; or the Lantern, whose beam reaches its detection radius) shows no dark line. A **Jammed** tower's panel says so under its name, with the Jammed glyph and the whole seconds left ("Jammed 4 s"); after the jam ("Immune 3 s") or System Reboot ("Immune 6 s") it counts the seconds no jam can land ([enemies.md §4](enemies.md#4-tower-disabling-jamming)).
- Range is shown as an ellipse when placing or selecting a tower (range is measured on the logical grid and drawn in isometric projection; see [maps.md §1](maps.md#1-grid-and-coordinates)).
- QUILL comments on key events during play (first leak, boss phase, a new enemy) in the Defense Grid style (D001 influences), through the bark matrix (§6.3). The voice is set by [../story/style-guide.md](../story/style-guide.md).
- The HUD's top-left name plate shows **JPT-1 · {callsign}** (§6.1), so the callsign change is visible during play.

### 6.1 Character creation and callsigns

The reason only flavors a few lines.

### 6.2 Results screen and the lifetime counter

- **Pests Processed** is a **lifetime save stat**: +1 for every Skitterkin enemy killed by the player's towers or abilities, on every run, including replays, losses, and retries (the game remembers everything you did). Units gathered by a Maw, scurrying young from a Hauler's sack, and medal-exempt younglings that leak don't count. It's added when a run ends for any reason.
- **Per-act results line** (copy owned by the style guide, [§2](../story/style-guide.md#2-voice-by-act)): Act I `PESTS PROCESSED: {n} ★ Top 5% of Technicians!` (the "Top 5%" is always true, and it's a lie nobody checks); Act II `Pests processed: {n}.` (no star); Act III the counter is **hidden** and replaced by the map's meter line (`Evacuated: {n}.` on an Escort or the Holdout, `Sheltered: {n}.` on A3M6, `Carried: {n} of {total}.` on A3M5, its bearers home of those its flags walked, beside its Long Walk row), and a map with no meter (Defend, a Protect map with no bearers) shows **no line at all**: the count is simply gone (`Held.` is the Holdout card's headline, never a counter line); the finale's win headlines `PESTS PROCESSED: 0`, the counter as Dawn left it (its first `REMEMBERED: {n}` is the epilogue's flip, §6.8), and a post-game challenge (the finale's dials) shows `REMEMBERED: {n}` as its counter line. Act I results also show the cheerful star rating; Act III shows no stars at all. The line ticks up (art-direction §9); screen readers get the final line meanwhile, and once it lands the card holds that one line, never a second copy (D150).
- **Ves'thara reads the live number** in A2M2 through the `{pests_processed}` token, which resolves to the lifetime total plus the current run's kills so far.
- **The countdown:** in the finale's Phase 2 the counter returns at the lifetime total and **counts down to 0** as the Hollow is laid to rest; it first flips to **REMEMBERED: {n}** on the epilogue's counter card (§6.8). Its pacing rules are in [maps.md §5.5](maps.md#55-the-leaving-song-finale-rules). As built (`app/countdown.ts`): N is the lifetime count as Phase 2 begins (the save's, plus this attempt's Skitterkin not yet filed), the counter shows round(N × (1 − p)) with p the rest progress, and the epilogue's REMEMBERED: {n} is that N. The finale's card (on the dawn gradient C5 rises into, headlined PESTS PROCESSED: 0 in the counter's steel) lists **Voices held** ("{n} of {N}") in Core integrity's place: the fewest singers singing at once through the run, of its choir (Dawn wakes the hushed before the card, so the card counts what the walk held).
- **Story stamps:** a results screen can carry a story-only stamp (for example A1M5's red "Containment Lapse" when the Quiet Wave was spared). A stamp reads **this run** (`thisRun`), not the saved flag, so a replay that cleared the Quiet Wave shows none ([D091](../decisions.md#d091--2026-11-09--t316-results-the-scene-flow-the-pause-menu)). Stamps never change rewards. The stamps are content (`content/dialogue/resultsFlow.ts`).
- **An Escort's meter on Results.** A map that ended as an Escort, from the start or after a pivot (A2M5), or as a Holdout (A3M3), lists its meter beside the waves in Core integrity's place, as the HUD showed it last, with the refugees lost after it when any were ("Evacuated 25 / 36 · lost 11", the map's `meterLabel`; the total is the run's own, so a group a story flag kept off the road, Thrum's under `cleared` or the door C3 didn't pick, isn't counted). Core integrity isn't listed: an Escort from the start has no core, and a pivot's leaks before it are the Perfect medal's business. Where the act hides the counter (Act III), the meter's line is the counter line, except where it would only repeat this row ("Evacuated: 30." under "Evacuated 30 / 30"): then the row says it once and the card has no counter line (`counterRepeatsStat`, D143).
- **Story transactions on Results.** A2M4's Q3 Bonus is a payroll row (+400 Stardust) with its memo's subject under it; C2's trade is a row once answered (Sell's Intel and the Zibzab Tune-up's line, or Return's "Carried home"); A2M5's gift is "Given: Prism Spire" (A3M1's Hum Choir, on a spared first win, "Given: Hum Choir"). A companion's ability handed to the Technician is an aside: Gerald's mail (A3M4) is "Inherited: Suppressing Fire · Technician ability", and C4 Keep is a loan, "Lent by QUILL: Time Dilation · Technician ability", since QUILL is still there (D150). A3M2's Clean Exit, on its first win: the uplink's refund is a row ("Refunded · Orbital Strike Mk II", +2 Intel), and what was revoked is one line under the payroll ("Revoked: Rivet, Orbital Strike"), plain, never a verdict.
- **Gift stones (Act III and the finale).** From A3M1 the warrens give the Stardust ([progression.md §1](progression.md#1-currencies)): the card's payroll is headed "Given", and its Stardust line reads "Gift stones" beside the gift-stone icon (the same amounts and rules; a loss after a checkpoint retry adds "· Since wave {n}", a loss on a cleared map "· Walked before").

The Stardust line wears the act's Stardust icon (Everglow's gem in Acts I–II, the gift stone from Act III), and its note never repeats the waves ("Effort stipend", "· Since wave {n}" after a checkpoint retry); a replay win whose veteran modifiers beat the map's best bonus on file notes them, and only such a win pays them (D156; "Overtime bonus · Veteran +40%", and on a training shift "· Training shifts are unpaid!" after it, so a +0 explains itself). ### 6.9 The Peeker

- On every Act I map, a youngling hides behind a rock on a decor tile near a warren mouth ([maps.md §7](maps.md#7-storytelling-with-the-map)). It peeks out every 20–40 s of play (for 3 s), and it's shy: while an enemy is within 2 tiles of the rock, or the pointer or keyboard cursor is on it, it stays down, and a peek in progress ducks. Clicking it (or Enter with the keyboard cursor on it; the cursor reaches the rock while nothing is armed) plays a squeak and a duck animation and sets `peeker_poked` (once is enough). It has no effect on play, rewards, or medals.
- **In Act I a poke is a UI event, not a command.** It changes nothing in the sim, so it never enters the command log; story records it and sets `peeker_poked` ([../tech/story-runtime.md § Flags](../tech/story-runtime.md#flags)). The rock is still a named marker in map data (`peeker`), and the peeking animation is render-only.
- **A2M3 (A1M2 revisited): Pebble's rescue is player-triggered.** From W3's clear (W4's build phase, hands free) the rock holds Pebble, and lights up with its own call, a double squeak and a soft chime that turns heads over the fight (D135): a `setClickable` script action makes the `peeker` marker a **clickable world object** and shows the highlight ([maps.md §5.3](maps.md#53-map-scripts)). Clicking it issues an `interact` command (valid only while the marker is clickable), so it is in the command log, and fires the `interacted` trigger once: the rescue cue (Bricks picks her up), which pauses play until it's read. The `setClickable` action carries a fallback trigger (A2M3: W6 cleared) that fires the same `interacted` trigger if the player never clicks, so the story always lands. In A3M6 Pebble sits on top of the A1M1 rock.

### 6.10 Joke of the Shift and the optional bark button

- **Joke of the Shift.** On A1M1–A1M5, one of QUILL's radio slots per map is the **Joke of the Shift**, a short joke in its policy-and-lettuce voice (copy: [../story/campaign.md](../story/campaign.md)). It plants the jokes that pay off later.
- **Optional bark button (rule).** A radio line or bark may carry **one optional reply button** in the comms panel (for example the knock-knock joke's **"Who's there?"**). It is the finale's single-button grammar, rehearsed with nothing at stake:
  - It never pauses the game, blocks input, locks any control, or covers the playfield; the player can keep building, and hotkeys stay live.
  - It is mouse-only (no hotkey) and at least 16×16 game px, inside the comms panel.
  - It disappears when its line auto-dismisses. Ignoring it is always fine: there is no penalty, no timer on screen, and no reprompt.
  - Pressing it plays the authored reply line and emits a story-side UI event. It is **not a sim command**, so it can't affect play, the command log, or the balance harness. Story may record that it was pressed.

## Open questions

1. **Overlapping waves.** Is starting the countdown when spawning ends (not when the wave dies) too punishing in Act I? An alternative is a Story-difficulty-only rule that the countdown waits for the wave to resolve.
2. **Spared threshold** (settled, D097): Old Thrum must leak, plus at least 75% of the younglings, so a stray splash that catches a couple of younglings still counts as sparing, and killing Thrum first never does.
3. **"Not firing" detection.** Ten seconds with Skitterkin in range and no shots fired may trigger by accident on maps with poor coverage. Should it also require Hold Fire to be on, at the cost of missing players who spare by placement?
4. **Pests Processed on quit.** Kills are added when a run ends. Closing the tab mid-map loses that run's count. Acceptable, or should the host flush the count on `pagehide` (a save write mid-map, which the save rules otherwise forbid)?
